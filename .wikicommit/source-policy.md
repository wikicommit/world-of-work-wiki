Take in documents published by public bodies: occupational safety and health agencies,
statistics offices, labour and health ministries, public labour research institutes,
intergovernmental organisations (ILO, WHO, OECD, World Bank, Eurostat, Eurofound, EU-OSHA),
and the peer-reviewed journals those bodies publish. Prefer reports, survey results, guidance,
statistical analyses and knowledge compilations over press releases and campaign pages.

For now, register a document only when its own terms, or its publisher's terms, allow reuse
with attribution: public domain, an open government licence, a Creative Commons licence, a
statistics office's open licence, or an explicit statement that it may be reproduced with the
source acknowledged. Record those terms in source.license at registration.

Where a publisher's terms reserve all rights or require permission (the domains listed under
index_only above, among others), read its pages only to find the documents it cites — do not
register them yet. Never register anything from a site that refuses automated access.

Check the document's own copyright notice, not only the site's terms: a site under an open
licence can host a printed title that says "all rights reserved", and an organisation's
licence can change at a date (ILO publications before May 2023 are not openly licensed).

Do not take in commercial vendors' material, consultancy marketing, news articles, or
personal blogs. Laws and institutions are in scope through
documents in which a public body explains or compares them in prose (government guidance
pages, comparative reports, consultation documents). Do not take in statute texts to be
interpreted, and do not treat codes of practice as checklists to reproduce. Licences that
forbid adaptation (ND) are not reusable here.

Prefer the shortest official version that carries the substance (a summary edition, a
"first findings" release or an analytical article over a several-hundred-page full report)
when both exist.

Prefer documents in which the publisher interprets its own figures in prose — analytical
articles, statistical topics, "Statistics Explained" pages, statistical releases written as
text — over results tables, slide decks and chart-heavy summaries. When an article exists as
both a web page and a PDF, prefer the web page: charts in a PDF are extracted as loose
numbers, while on a web page they are images and only the written interpretation remains.
Do not take in a document whose substance is tables of figures.
