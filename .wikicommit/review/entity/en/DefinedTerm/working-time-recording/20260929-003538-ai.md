The frontmatter description and the lead sentence define working time recording as capturing 'the start and end of working time, breaks and overtime'. That list comes mainly from the German Federal Labour Court's December 2022 statement, with support from the Slovenian law. The EU-level requirement in the Commission Communication, based on CCOO, is narrower: a system that measures 'the duration of time worked each day by each worker'. The lead hedges with 'typically', but the description states the list without that hedge.
The lead says compliance can be checked 'by employers, workers and labour inspectorates'. The sources support labour inspectorates and authorities directly, and workers indirectly (the Spanish Supreme Court on proving overtime, and 'accessible' in CCOO). No source frames employers as checking compliance.
In the Spanish case, 'a union confederation then challenged a German multinational bank' slightly simplifies the source. The source names the Services Federation of the Trade Union Confederation of Workers' Commissions (FS-CCOO), a federation within a confederation.
On teleworkers, the source says remote workers 'might enjoy a degree of flexibility and autonomy'. The page drops the hedge and says they 'organise their work flexibly'.
