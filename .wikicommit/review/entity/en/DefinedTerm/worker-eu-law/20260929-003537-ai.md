Usage paragraph opens 'According to the European Commission's [[Report/interpretative-communication-on-the-working-time-directive]], it is for national courts to classify a person...'. The source gives this as a holding of the Court: 'The Court holds that "[i]t is for the national court to apply that concept of a 'worker' in any classification..."' (source lines 711-714). The Communication does state it, so the attribution is not wrong, but a reader could take it for the Commission's own view. Wording such as 'The Court has held (as the Commission's Communication records)...' would be more precise.
Lead paragraph glosses Directive 89/391/EEC as the directive 'on measures to improve the safety and health of workers at work'. Its actual title in the source is 'on the introduction of measures to encourage improvements in the safety and health of workers at work' (lines 687-689). The paraphrase reads like a formal title, so either quote the exact title or drop it.
The description's 'instruments such as the Working Time Directive' generalises a little. The source discusses the worker concept only as it applies to the Working Time Directive, and says the Court linked it to the free-movement concept under Article 45 TFEU (lines 707-709). It does not say which other instruments the concept governs.
The page restates the Yodel test in a positive form: whether independence 'appears fictitious' and whether subordination 'can be established'. The source quotes it in the negative, as the conditions for NOT being a worker: independence 'does not appear to be fictitious' and 'it is not possible to establish' subordination (lines 794-797). The substance is the same, but it is not a quotation, so it should not later be put in quotation marks.
