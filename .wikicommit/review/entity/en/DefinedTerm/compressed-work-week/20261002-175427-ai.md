The definition in the opening paragraph and the description ('the same weekly working hours … longer but fewer days') matches the source. Note the difference from the linked page DefinedTerm/four-day-week: there, the Eurofound review calls Ireland's 32-hour trial a 'compressed working week', which is a reduction in weekly hours. That page attributes the phrase to the review, so the two pages don't directly contradict each other. Still, a reader moving between them could take 'compressed work week' to cover both reduced-hours and same-hours schedules, while this page defines it as same-hours only.
The phrase 'the risks of long daily and long weekly hours' in the second Usage paragraph is a slightly expanded paraphrase. The source says only 'disentangle the differences in risks by studying specific combinations of working hours'. Both the surrounding paragraph and the attribution to the editorial support the paraphrase.
