The quoted definition of multitasking is attributed to [[Report/time-use-total-work-burden-unpaid-work-and-leisure]] as the party that 'defines' it; in the source the quoted wording is footnoted to earlier research (note 58, Offer and Schneider 2011). The page's 'drawing on earlier research' hedge keeps this from being an attribution swap, but a reader could still take the wording as Statistics Canada's own.
The alias 'Multitasking (time use)' treats multitasking as a synonym for simultaneous activity, but the source defines multitasking more broadly (simultaneous performance of several tasks *or rapid alternation between them*). The alias 'Secondary activity (time use)' does not appear in the article body; the only instance is the title of a cited document (note 63).
The body links Statistics Canada's 'total work burden' (paid plus unpaid work in combination) to [[DefinedTerm/total-work-time]], a page defined entirely in terms of Mexico's ENUT 'tiempo total de trabajo'. The two concepts are similar but are not the same measure, and nothing on either page says so.
