Usage para 2 opens with 'National data in the report show the term in use:' but then adds the Health Council of the Netherlands finding, which is about repetitive movements as a risk for carpal tunnel syndrome and lateral epicondylitis and never uses the term RSI, so the lead-in overstates what that sentence shows.
The source says RSI of the shoulder/upper arm was 'around 20 % of workers in 2016' out of 1,791 MSD-related occupational diseases; the page reads this as about 20 % of the recorded diseases, a reasonable reading of garbled source wording but not literal.
The claim that awareness of RSI may partly explain cross-country differences is the argument of one study (Coggon et al., 2013); the page's passive 'has been put forward' hedges it without naming the study and shortens 'individual and socioeconomic factors' to 'individual factor'.
