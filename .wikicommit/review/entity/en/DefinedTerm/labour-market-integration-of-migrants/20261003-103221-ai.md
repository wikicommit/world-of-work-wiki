The education paragraph says participation and employment both rise with education 'but the differences across education levels are smaller for foreign-born people and for the native-born with two foreign-born parents'. The labour market indicators source states the smaller-differences point only for labour force participation (Figure 5). For employment it frames the pattern differently: the gap between foreign-born and native-born people changes across education levels. As written, the sentence can be read as applying both findings to employment.
The bridging sentence 'National statistics can add measures that the EU indicators above do not cover, such as hours worked' is the page's own framing, not a statement from any source. The Eurostat employment conditions source does cover part-time employment, which is itself a working-time measure, so 'do not cover' could be read as overstating the contrast with the SSB figures (full-time share by stillingsprosent).
The page groups immigrants from the Nordic countries and from EU/EFTA countries as having the highest employment and full-time shares. The SSB source splits the second group into 'EU/EFTA up to 2004 excluding the Nordic countries' and 'new EU countries after 2004'. Both fit the claim, but the merged label hides that split.
