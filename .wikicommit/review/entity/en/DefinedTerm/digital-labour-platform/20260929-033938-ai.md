The 'Earlier formulations' paragraph quotes the OECD/ILO/Eurostat measurement handbook's description of a digital platform ('a "digital interface" or an "online service provider" … positioned between the providers of the services or goods and their clients'). The quote reaches the page only through Report V(1)'s own quotation (SOURCE 3, lines 317-321, footnote 14). The handbook itself is not among the evidence. The page presents this as the report adopting the description, and SOURCE 3 states that directly ('For the purposes of this report, this latter description has been used'), so it passes. A reader should still know the handbook wording is second-hand.
In the 'National legal definitions' paragraph, the page rewrites SOURCE 3 para. 113's varied connection formulas as 'connects clients or users with service providers (Washington State, India, Greece, France)'. These are passengers with drivers (Washington), buyers or users with sellers (India), providers, businesses or third parties with users, customers or consumers (Greece), and people in general (France). The merged wording is a fair summary but reads as more uniform than the laws are.
