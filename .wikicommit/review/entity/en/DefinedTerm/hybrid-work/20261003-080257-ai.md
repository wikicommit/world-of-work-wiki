The sentence "The Commission expects hybrid work to be widely adopted after the pandemic" is a little stronger than the Productivity Commission paper. The paper says many firms are "likely to experiment with the hybrid model", that it is "likely to be popular", and that it could become the accepted norm in only one of three scenarios. A reader could take this as a firm forecast, which the page's later hedging does not fully offset.
In the EU-OSHA paragraph, "the voluntary nature of the arrangement and the predictability of hybrid schedules playing a moderating role" is attached to job satisfaction, work–life balance and mental health together. In the source, predictability is tied to job satisfaction, and the voluntary nature is described as moderating psychosocial factors and mental health. The two factors are lumped across outcomes the source treats separately.
The Eurofound sentence saying hybrid work was "highest among" finance, public administration, ages 30–44 and employees with children under 12 compresses the source's wording. The source says employees in finance and public administration are "more likely" to hybrid work, that hybrid work is "most common" among 30–44 year olds, and that those with children under 12 are "more likely" to work in hybrid mode (23%). The figures are correct but the superlative framing is slightly loose.
