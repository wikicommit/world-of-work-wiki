In the France paragraph, 22.2% and 14.2% (2023) come from Insee's main-job private-sector field, while the age breakdown (4.3% to 24.9%), the 13.5% figure and the 13.5% to 9.7% top-1% figure come from a slightly narrower field. Insee flags that difference. The page does not, so a reader could take 14.2% and 13.5% as inconsistent measures of the same thing.
'women's under-representation among the best-paid 1% of employees, without whom the gap falls from 13.5% to 9.7%' is easy to misread. Insee's statement is that excluding the best-paid 1% of employees (men and women) from the calculation narrows the gap. 'Without whom' could be read as removing women.
The France paragraph mixes years: 2023 for the salary and working-time gaps, 2022 for the parenthood gaps. The page does give both years, but they sit close together in one sentence.
The Taiwan comparison (Korea 2017, Japan, US) uses figures built on different bases. The page names those bases, but the figures are not comparable with each other or with Eurostat's unadjusted indicator.
