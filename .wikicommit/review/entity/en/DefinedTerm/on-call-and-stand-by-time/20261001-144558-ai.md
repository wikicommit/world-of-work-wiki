The 'Limits on stand-by classed as rest' paragraph calls Directive 89/391/EEC 'the framework directive on safety and health at work'. The Commission source names the directive only as 'Directive 89/391/EEC' / 'on the introduction of measures to encourage improvements in the safety and health of workers at work' and never uses the word 'framework'. The identification is correct, but the 'framework' label comes from outside the evidence.
ILO paragraph: 'Because Convention No. 30 applies to employees only, the report treats waiting periods ... as a normative gap' is firmer than the source, which says the issue 'may therefore be considered as a normative gap'. The source's 'therefore' also follows the whole paragraph (employees-only scope, CEACR on on-call time, payment), not just the employees-only point.
Chile figure: the page quotes the source accurately ('must not be less than 120 per cent of the minimum monthly income' for hourly remuneration of active working time, para. 188). The source's own wording mixes hourly and monthly units, and para. 176 gives a differently worded rule for independent workers ('proportion of the minimum monthly income ... increased by twenty per cent'), so readers may find the figure confusing.
The sentence beginning 'ILO surveys found that workers on microtask platforms spend about 20 minutes...' continues with 'and on location-based delivery platforms unpaid time includes waiting between assignments'. In the source, the delivery-platform point is cited to an ETUI policy brief (Pulignano et al.), not to the ILO surveys, but the sentence structure suggests ILO surveys found both.
