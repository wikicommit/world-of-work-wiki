Lead sentence says the three rest periods 'together with paid annual leave, give concrete expression to the right to daily and weekly rest periods in Article 31(2)'. The Communication says Article 31(2) enshrines the right to daily and weekly rest (V, line 2147) and that the Directive as a whole gives concrete expression to Article 31 (I, line 279). Paid annual leave belongs to the Article 31(2) right to annual paid leave, not to the right to daily and weekly rest periods, so the sentence runs two rights together and is easy to misread.
Under 'Breaks', 'National transposition may not leave them to individual agreement between worker and employer' is stated as unqualified fact. The Communication derives it from the Directive's wording ('This in turn means…', lines 2293-2295), and the paragraph is set out like the Commission's own positions. Saying 'the Commission considers', as the next sentence does, would be more precise.
Under 'Weekly rest', 'not necessarily on the same day each week' is attributed to the Court. In the source the Court's holding is that rest may be given at any time within each seven-day period. The 'same day' point is the Communication's own gloss on what the Directive does not require (lines 2366-2369). The content is accurate; only the attribution is loose.
