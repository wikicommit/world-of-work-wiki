Weekly-rest bullet: the page says 'The Court has held that the weekly rest may be given at any time within each seven-day period, not necessarily on the same day each week.' In SOURCE 3 (lines 2366-2369), only the 'at any time within each seven-day period' part is attributed to the Court (Maio da Rosa). The sentence 'The Directive does not require the weekly rest to be granted on the same day of the week' is the Communication's own statement. As written, the page credits the Court with the second half as well.
Opening paragraph: the page says the three rest periods 'together with paid annual leave, give concrete expression to the right to daily and weekly rest periods in Article 31(2)'. SOURCE 3 says two separate things here: the rest right is 'enshrined in Article 31(2)' (line 2147), and the Directive as a whole 'gives concrete expression to Article 31' (line 279). The page merges them into one phrase. Also, Article 31(2) covers paid annual leave as its own right, not as part of the rest-period right. The substance is supported, but the phrase is easy to misread.
Shortened weekly rest: the page says the 24-hour reduction 'was introduced mainly for shift workers'. SOURCE 3 (lines 2417-2423) says it was introduced 'with the aim to cover the possible problem of shift workers' working late Saturday and early Monday, and that it 'may also apply to different cases'. 'Mainly' is a light paraphrase, not a claim the source makes in those words.
