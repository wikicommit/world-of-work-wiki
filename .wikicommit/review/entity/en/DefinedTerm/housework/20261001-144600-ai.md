The frontmatter description states as general time-use fact that housework 'is the part of unpaid work that excludes caregiving', and the body says 'Together with caregiving it makes up unpaid work' unqualified. The source supports both only as this chapter's own operational usage (note 72: 'Housework includes all unpaid household activities, excluding caregiving'; intro: 'unpaid work (i.e., housework and caregiving)'). The linked unpaid-work page shows other surveys also count volunteering as unpaid work and use 'housework' more narrowly, so scoping these two sentences to the GSS/this report would avoid a reader taking them as universal.
The opening of the Usage section ('Housework is a standard category in analyses of the gendered division of labour') is a framing generalisation; the source shows much research on housework and gender but does not literally call it a 'standard category'.
Income is grouped under 'Consistent with the time availability and relative resources perspectives'; the source explicitly ties only the education finding to the relative resources perspective and offers outsourcing of housework as a possible explanation for the income pattern.
