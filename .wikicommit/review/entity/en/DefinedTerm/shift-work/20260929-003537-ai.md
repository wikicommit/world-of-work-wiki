Offshore sentence: the source (C 143/75, lines 5138-5140) says the aim of allowing 'shift systems based on 2 shifts × 12 hours × 14 days to continue' belongs to 'this flexible approach to rest periods for offshore workers'. That approach covers the Article 17(3) derogations and the Article 20 twelve-month reference period together. The page ties the aim only to the twelve-month averaging option. The quoted aim does mention 'an annualised calculation of working hours', so the claim is supported, but the page narrows the source's framing.
The page says the 24-hour weekly-rest reduction 'was introduced mainly for shift workers working the late shift on Saturday and the early shift on Monday'. The source (lines 4417-4423) says it was introduced 'with the aim to cover the possible problem of shift workers' in that situation, and adds that it 'may also apply to different cases than that of shift workers'. The word 'mainly' is the page's own reading, not the source's wording.
The WikiLink [[DefinedTerm/rest-breaks-at-work]] is attached to an EU-level derogation (the Article 5 reduction to 24 hours). The target page covers only UK and Japanese rules: a UK weekly rest of 24 hours a week or 48 hours a fortnight. The two do not contradict each other, because they describe different jurisdictions. A reader following the link will not find the EU rule there.
