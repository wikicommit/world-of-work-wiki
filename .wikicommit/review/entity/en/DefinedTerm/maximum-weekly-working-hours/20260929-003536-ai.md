Usage (EU limit), para. 2 — the sentence 'National law may use reference periods that start and end on fixed calendar dates, provided it has mechanisms ensuring that the 48-hour average is also respected over each six-month period straddling two consecutive fixed periods' reads as a general rule. SOURCE 2 (lines 4844-4850) gives it as the Court's ruling in a case where a Member State had extended the reference period to six months, and that is why the straddling period there is six months. Without that context, a reader could take a six-month straddle check to apply to any fixed reference period, such as the standard four months. Adding 'in a case where the reference period had been extended to six months' would fix this.
The sentence 'The limit itself can be set aside only for autonomous workers … and for individual workers who agree to a working-time opt-out' follows SOURCE 2 line 2455. However, SOURCE 2 (lines 5149-5156, 5236-5241) also says Article 21 disapplies Article 6 for workers on seagoing fishing vessels, though it still requires a 48-hour average over up to 12 months. The hop-out page Legislation/working-time-directive.md more loosely says Member States may derogate from the maximum weekly working time 'for specific categories of workers or sectors'. The two pages don't contradict each other on a specific fact. But a reader comparing them may see a tension, which the word 'only' makes stronger.
