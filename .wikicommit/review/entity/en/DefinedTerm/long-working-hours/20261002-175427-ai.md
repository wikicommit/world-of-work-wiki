The Safety and injuries paragraph says the editorial 'describes payroll data as the gold standard for measuring working hours'. In the source, the editorial reports that Matre and colleagues point to payroll data as the 'golden standard'. It then partly endorses this ('This is particularly true when...') and adds caveats about unpaid extra hours and secondary jobs. The page's wording makes the label sound like the editorial's own unqualified judgement, so a reader may misread who made the claim.
'Long weekly hours, especially overtime, may be due to high workload, which offers an alternative explanation for health effects' compresses the editorial's point. The editorial says alternative mechanisms apply 'in particular when it comes to more long-term effects', and the page drops that qualifier.
