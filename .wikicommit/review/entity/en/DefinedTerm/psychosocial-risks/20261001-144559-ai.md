The opening definition ('risk factors related to work — how it is designed, organized and managed, and its social context — associated with poor mental, as well as poor physical, health') is stated as unqualified general fact, but it merges WHO's framing (Box 1: 'risk factors for poor mental health (as well as poor physical health) related to the workplace') with EU-OSHA's 2007 wording, which the page later quotes and attributes. The EU-OSHA report says 'for psychosocial factors at work there are no globally accepted definitions', and the WorkSafe glossary says 'there is no single set of agreed terms'. The lead also calls psychosocial risks 'risk factors', which WorkSafe would call hazards; the page itself raises this distinction further down. A reader may take the lead to be a settled, agreed definition.
'Statistics Canada researchers' slightly overstates the authorship: one of the five authors (Simon Houle) is listed as being with Concordia University.
The JD-R sentence says the Standard's factors 'count as' job resources. The StatCan article is more hedged: they 'would be considered job resources in this framework', citing Schaufeli & Taris.
