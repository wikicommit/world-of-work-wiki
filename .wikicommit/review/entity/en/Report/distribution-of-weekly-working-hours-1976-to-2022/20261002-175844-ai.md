The source labels the top hour band inconsistently. The body text lists it as '50 hours or more', while the indicator definition box and Table 1 use 'more than 50 hours'. The page uses '50 or more' throughout and does not mention the discrepancy.
The page links [[DefinedTerm/usual-hours-of-work]], but that linked page describes the EU Labour Force Survey concept. Under that concept, usual hours refer to the main job and include extra hours, whether paid or unpaid. Statistics Canada's indicator differs: it covers all jobs and, from 1997, excludes overtime for employees. The page does state the Canadian definition in its Measurement paragraph, so this is not a contradiction. A reader following the link could still assume the two definitions are the same.
The page also links [[DefinedTerm/part-time-employment]]. That page describes the EU's self-assessed classification, while this page uses Statistics Canada's fixed threshold of under 30 hours. Both are correctly attributed in their own contexts, but readers may confuse the two definitions.
