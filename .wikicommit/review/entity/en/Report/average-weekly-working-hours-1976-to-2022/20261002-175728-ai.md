The page links its subject to [[DefinedTerm/usual-hours-of-work]]. That term page's description uses the Eurostat/EU-LFS concept, which counts extra hours normally worked, whether paid or unpaid. This page instead describes Statistics Canada's post-1997 LFS concept, where usual hours for employees are normal paid or contract hours excluding overtime. The two pages do not contradict each other, because they describe different agencies' definitions. A reader following the link could still assume the Canadian indicator uses the EU definition.
The sentence on women's hours says they 'rose to 33.2 by 2022' straight after '32.7 between 1976 and 1996'. This could be read as a rise from the 1996 level. The source measures the rise from the 1997 level instead (32.5, so +0.7), after the break in the series.
