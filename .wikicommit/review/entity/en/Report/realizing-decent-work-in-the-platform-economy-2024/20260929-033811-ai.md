The lead sentence says the report 'opens the standard-setting process on decent work in the platform economy'. The source never says this. It describes the report as prepared under the double-discussion procedure to inform the Conference and help Member States answer the questionnaire (para. 4), and the Governing Body's decisions at its 346th and 347th Sessions came before it. A reader could take 'opens' to mean the process began with this report.
The 'International labour standards' paragraph links 'the remuneration of waiting periods' to DefinedTerm/on-call-and-stand-by-time, which is a page about the EU-law concept under the Working Time Directive. The source (para. 79) says the CEACR 'did not address specifically the issue of "waiting time" in platform work' and discussed only the 'related notion' of on-call or standby time under ILO Convention No. 30. So the link could suggest the two concepts are the same, or that EU law applies.
The 'case for a standard' paragraph says 'It recommends that they ... address algorithms, deactivation, personal data and the cross-border nature'. In the source, only the scope points (location-based and online platforms, employees and self-employed workers) are phrased as 'The Office recommends' (paras 302–303). The thematic points are things 'The Office considers' the instrument(s) should also do (para. 301). The substance is supported, but the two verbs are merged.
