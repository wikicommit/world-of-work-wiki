The page links its subject to [[DefinedTerm/gender-pay-gap]], which defines the gap as a difference in average gross hourly earnings (Eurostat's unadjusted indicator). The Insee study measures several different things: net annual salary income (revenu salarial), net salary in full-time equivalent, and annual volume of work. A reader following the link could take these for the same measure. The page's wording does not say so, but a short note on the difference would help.
The 13.5% full-time-equivalent figure, and the age, category, size and sector breakdowns, are on a slightly different field from the 14.2% headline. The page says 'on a slightly narrower field' once, but the same paragraph then quotes the 3.8% 'same job, same establishment' figure, which the source estimates on only part of the private sector (40% of full-time-equivalent). That caveat is not stated on the page.
The parenthood figures are for 2022 (panel Tous salariés 2022), while the rest of the page is 2023. The page states the year, but the 2022 'all employees' working-time gap (9.9%) is easy to confuse with the 2023 figure (9.3%).
