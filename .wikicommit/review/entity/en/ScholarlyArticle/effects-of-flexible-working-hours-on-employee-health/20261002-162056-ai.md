Key Points bullet 6 says that among women who did NOT change employer, fully and partially self-determined flexibility reduced the age-related rise in health worries. This matches Table 7, where Model 6 is Sample B ('nur Beschäftigte ohne Betriebswechsel') with Vollkommene Flexibilität * Alter -0.005* and Partielle Flexibilität * Alter -0.005**. The source's own prose on p. 15 says the opposite subgroup: 'Einzig für Betriebswechslerinnen zeigt sich ...', i.e. women who changed employer. The page silently resolved this internal inconsistency in favour of the table. A reader checking the prose against the page would find a mismatch, so the discrepancy may be worth noting on the page.
Key Points bullet 3 says no form of flexibility had a significant effect on sick-leave days or health worries 'in the pooled models'. In panel econometrics, 'pooled' usually means pooled OLS, but these are fixed-effects models estimated on men and women together (Table 2). Wording such as 'in the models for all employees' or 'with men and women combined' would be harder to misread, especially since the gender-split model does show a significant effect for women (Table 5, -2.069*).
The final Key Points bullet says the negative effects appear 'mainly' for employer-controlled flexibility and flextime. The source is stronger: 'Diese zeigen sich allerdings nur bei der betrieblich gesteuerten Flexibilität ... und partieller selbstgesteuerter Flexibilität ..., nicht aber bei vollkommen selbstgesteuerter Flexibilität', i.e. only for these two and not for full self-determination. The page's softening is not wrong, but it is less precise than the source.
