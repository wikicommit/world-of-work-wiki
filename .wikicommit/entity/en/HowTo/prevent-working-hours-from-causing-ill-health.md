The Swedish Work Environment Authority (Arbetsmiljöverket) advises employers on how to stop the way working time is arranged from leading to ill health. It states that long shifts of 10 hours or more, long working weeks of 50 hours or more, and [[DefinedTerm/night-work]] and [[DefinedTerm/shift-work]] carry risks of ill health — sleep disturbance, depression, gastrointestinal problems, diabetes, cardiovascular disease and cancer — which can result from insufficient recovery and a disrupted circadian rhythm.

## Prerequisites

The employer is responsible for countering these risks, even where the Swedish Working Hours Act (arbetstidslagen) is complied with as regards the maximum hours employees may work and the minimum rest periods they must have. The social partners may agree on working-time arrangements, but the employer must still assess whether those working hours carry health risks.

## Steps

1. Find out how working time is actually distributed for employees in practice; a planned schedule alone is not enough to judge whether working hours could lead to ill health.
2. Where long shifts occur, carry out a risk assessment and take countermeasures — for example by reducing the demands placed on employees during the long shifts.
3. Check whether the possibility of working at different times and places, or expectations of being constantly reachable (for example by e-mail or phone), in practice amount to long shifts and long working weeks.
4. Check whether split shifts — a working day divided into several shifts with free time in between — in practice mean shifts of more than 10 hours or weeks of more than 50 hours, for example when employees must stay at the workplace between shifts and cannot recover during that time.
5. Where risks are found, take measures to prevent the working hours from causing ill health, such as changing staffing, moving tasks from night to day, or adding more breaks.

## Notes

Unplanned long shifts or long working weeks can be a sign of an imbalance between demands and resources, in which case the workload needs to be reviewed. The guidance refers to the authority's provisions on the organisational and social work environment in AFS 2023:2.
