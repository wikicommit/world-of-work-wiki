HSE's Management Standards approach uses a five-step cycle to identify and reduce causes of [[DefinedTerm/work-related-stress]] in the organization. Its leaflet [[Report/how-to-tackle-work-related-stress]] asks managers, employees and their representatives to work together throughout the process.

## Prerequisites

HSE advises securing support from senior and line managers and employees before starting. A steering group can bring together managers, worker representatives, human resources, and occupational health. Arrange support for staff taking part in surveys or discussions, and explain why the process is being used.

## Steps

1. **Identify risks.** Explain the six [[DefinedTerm/management-standards-for-work-related-stress]] areas and how they apply locally: demands, control, support, relationships, role and change.
2. **Gather evidence about who may be affected.** Combine staff surveys or the HSE Indicator Tool with sources such as sickness absence, turnover, exit interviews, occupational health referrals and staff forums. HSE cautions against relying on a survey alone.
3. **Evaluate risks and develop solutions.** Check the analysis with employees, identify priority areas, and discuss gaps between current conditions and the standards' desired states. Focus groups can propose actions, which a project group can then prioritize.
4. **Record findings and implement action plans.** Set actions at the appropriate team, department or wider level, decide how their effects will be measured, and communicate the plans to employees.
5. **Monitor and review.** Check whether actions have the intended effect, reflect on underlying causes and revisit the process as needed. HSE describes stress management as continuing improvement rather than a one-time survey.

## Notes

The leaflet points to measures such as giving groups more control over their work, improving communication, developing managers' interpersonal skills and reviewing jobs or procedures. It suggests piloting decisions where practical, since needs can differ between departments.
