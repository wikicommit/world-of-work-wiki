[[Report/fundamentals-of-total-worker-health-approaches]] sets out a process for using its five [[DefinedTerm/defining-elements-of-total-worker-health]] to assess current workplace efforts and plan changes. The completed worksheets provide a baseline that can be revisited to track progress.

## Prerequisites

Assemble people familiar with policies and practices that affect worker safety, health, and well-being. The workbook suggests representation from across the workforce, including workers, safety staff, human resources, occupational health, and other relevant functions. Have the workbook's self-assessment and action-plan worksheets available.

## Steps

1. Read each defining element and its examples with the team.
2. For each element, record existing policies, programs, and practices in the self-assessment worksheet's “Where we are now” column.
3. Record changes that could better meet the element in its “Where we want to be” column.
4. Review the desired changes, choose improvement needs, and use the action-plan worksheet to identify who to involve, possible obstacles, solutions, and steps to take.
5. Revisit the completed worksheets regularly to assess progress and address new challenges or opportunities.

## Notes

NIOSH says the examples are illustrative and that workplaces differ. Its workbook presents the process as a starting point for aligning or improving initiatives rather than a fixed program design.
