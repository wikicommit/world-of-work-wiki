The Fair Work Agency is a state body for enforcing employment rights in the UK whose establishment is allowed by the [[Legislation/employment-rights-act-2025]]. The Act's Explanatory Notes set it against a system in which most employment rights are enforced by individuals through employment tribunals — a process the notes describe as often challenging for workers with limited resources — and only a limited number of mainly pay-related rights are enforced by the state on workers' behalf.

## Activities & Products

The agency brings together existing state enforcement functions:

- the regulation of employment agencies and employment businesses;
- the penalty scheme for unpaid employment tribunal awards;
- enforcement of the National Minimum Wage and of [[DefinedTerm/statutory-sick-pay]];
- the licensing regime for businesses operating as "gangmasters" in certain sectors;
- enforcement of Parts 1 and 2 of the Modern Slavery Act 2015.

It also takes in a wider range of employment rights, such as holiday pay and social care pay. The Act additionally brings umbrella companies — which the notes say can be responsible for denying employment rights to those who work through them — within regulation, enforced by the Employment Agency Standards Inspectorate and subsequently by the Fair Work Agency.
