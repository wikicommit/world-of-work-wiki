The Authority for Social Relations of Employment Platforms (ARPE) is an administrative body established by the French Government in 2021 to support social dialogue between [[DefinedTerm/digital-labour-platform]]s and the workers who use them. The International Labour Office's report [[Report/realizing-decent-work-in-the-platform-economy-2024]] (2024) presents it as an example of government action to support social dialogue in the platform economy.

## History

The legal basis the report cites for ARPE is Ordinance No. 2021-484 of 21 April 2021 on the Authority for Social Relations of Labour Platforms. In 2022, a decree established the criteria for determining the representativeness of an organization of platform workers, the subjects that must be covered by annual negotiations, and more detailed provisions on ARPE's role in mediating disputes relating to social dialogue. Separately, a French law of 7 February 2022 on the terms and conditions for the representation of self-employed workers regulates the representation of platform workers classified as self-employed and the possibility of negotiating sectoral agreements.

## Activities & Products

According to the report, ARPE's functions include ensuring the representativeness of workers using platforms, supporting the establishment and development of social dialogue, guaranteeing and preserving the rights of worker representatives, and promoting social dialogue through studies and statistical reports; it plays a supporting and mediation role between representatives of workers and of platforms. The report records collective agreements concluded in the chauffeured passenger transport (VTC) sector: in January 2023, agreements regulating the minimum payment per trip were signed between the representative organizations of workers and of platforms in the sector, and in September 2023 an agreement was signed on the transparency of platform operations and the conditions for suspending and deactivating services (see [[DefinedTerm/account-deactivation]]).
