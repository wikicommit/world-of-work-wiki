The Spanish Labour and Social Security Inspectorate (Inspección de Trabajo y Seguridad Social, ITSS) is Spain's labour and social security inspectorate. The EU-OSHA policy brief [[Report/osh-in-digital-platform-work-lessons-from-regulations-policies-actions-and-initiatives]] presents its work on the platform economy as a good example — with some room for improvement — of how inspectorates can tackle the challenges of digital platform work.

## Activities & Products

**Platform work.** According to the brief, the ITSS's first monitoring actions on platform work, before 2017, were mainly triggered by complaints from platform workers; they were dispersed, and different approaches led to different outcomes. In 2017 the ITSS decided to harmonise its monitoring of the digital platform economy and began aggregating information from previous cases, complainants, trade unions and platforms' websites. It developed a guide on the collaborative economy to help inspectors monitor platform work and enforce applicable legislation, covering the platform economy, investigation procedures, indicators (such as analysis of a platform's website or app and the concept of [[DefinedTerm/algorithmic-management]]), guidelines and case examples.

Campaigns against bogus self-employment in platform work formed part of the 2018–2020 Labour and Social Security Inspectorate Strategic Plan, which included operational measures such as giving inspectors the technical means to identify those involved in digital platforms, issuing an operations manual and training specialists, and conducting a campaign to inspect platforms. In 2019 and 2020 the ITSS identified 11,013 false self-employed workers on a single platform. Some actions involving several regional divisions were coordinated by a Special Unit at the Central Services.

**Assessment.** The brief attributes the ITSS's success to a predetermined strategy with considerable attention to training inspectors, and to coordinated actions and unified investigation procedures. It argues that these actions, which predate the [[Legislation/riders-law]], show that inspection services can enforce compliance despite uncertainty over the qualification of the employment relationship, and recommends the ITSS guide as a practice for other Member States' inspection services. It also uses the Spanish case to highlight what is lacking elsewhere: coordinated action, strategic and operational information on the sector, cooperation between authorities, and adequate capacity and resources.
