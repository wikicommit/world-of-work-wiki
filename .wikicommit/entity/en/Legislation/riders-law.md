The Riders' Law is a Spanish law on digital platform work, adopted as Royal Decree Law 9/2021 of 11 May 2021 and ratified by Law 12/2021 of 28 September 2021. It amends the [[Legislation/workers-statute]] in two ways: it introduces a legal [[DefinedTerm/presumption-of-employment-relationship]] for [[DefinedTerm/digital-platform-worker]]s in the delivery sector (Additional Provision 23 of the Statute), and it establishes a right to algorithmic transparency (Article 64.4). The EU-OSHA policy brief [[Report/osh-in-digital-platform-work-lessons-from-regulations-policies-actions-and-initiatives]] describes it as the first law to establish such a right at national level, and as the outcome of tripartite social dialogue between the Spanish Ministry of Labour and Social Economy, trade unions and business associations.

## Details

**Presumption of employment.** The law brings within the scope of the Workers' Statute people who provide paid services delivering or distributing consumer products or merchandise for employers that exercise powers of organisation, management and control — directly, indirectly or implicitly — through [[DefinedTerm/algorithmic-management]] of the service or of working conditions via a digital platform. This creates a rebuttable presumption of a dependent employment relationship, with the burden of proof on the employer to show that the worker is self-employed. According to the brief, a consequence is that Spain's Law 31/1995 on the prevention of occupational risks also applies to delivery platform workers, obliging platforms to carry out OSH risk assessments, implement prevention measures, and consult and inform workers on safety and health. The brief notes that it remains to be seen how courts will interpret these provisions, but that combining the presumption with algorithmic management means legal dependence may be established whenever service and working conditions are set by algorithms.

**Algorithmic transparency.** The law obliges platforms to inform workers' legal representatives about how the algorithms that may affect working conditions and access to and maintenance of employment, including profiling, function — for example the parameters, rules and instructions that guide them.

**Assessment.** The EU-OSHA brief regards the law as a leap forward in addressing OSH risks specific to platform work and as an example of the importance of social dialogue. It identifies three limitations: the presumption applies only to the delivery sector; platforms may try to circumvent the law by working through subcontractors; and the technical and practical scope of the algorithmic-transparency provision needs clarification.
