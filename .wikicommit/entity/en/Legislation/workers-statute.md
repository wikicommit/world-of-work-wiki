The Workers' Statute is a Spanish employment law whose recast text was approved by Royal Legislative Decree 2/2015 of 23 October. The Spanish Government's Your European Space information page on working hours, leave and holidays treats it as the main legal reference for those subjects: working time is regulated in Article 34, overtime in Article 35, night and shift work in Article 36, paid leave in Article 37 and annual holidays in Article 38.

The page describes a layered system in which the law establishes conditions and limits, regulations apply them, and the details may be specified in collective agreements. It presents the Statute's working-time rules alongside the Labour Guide, which also sets out these issues.

## Details

**Working time (Article 34).** As summarised on the Government page, working time is set by collective agreement up to a maximum of 40 hours of effective work, calculated as a weekly average over a year. Workers are entitled to at least 12 hours' rest between working days, and ordinary working time may not exceed 9 hours a day unless a collective agreement, or failing that an agreement between the company and the workers' representatives, distributes daily working time differently while still respecting that rest period. A break of at least 15 minutes is required when more than 6 consecutive hours are worked; for workers under 18 the break is at least 30 minutes whenever continuous work exceeds 4.5 hours. Working time may be distributed irregularly over the year by collective agreement or agreement with workers' representatives; without such an agreement, the company may distribute 10% of working time irregularly, provided minimum daily and weekly rest is respected and the worker is given at least five days' notice. Companies must keep a daily record of working time showing each worker's start and finish times. Adjustments to the length and distribution of working time, its organisation and the way work is performed, including remote working, may also be made to facilitate work–life balance, provided they are reasonable and proportionate to the worker's needs and the company's organisational or production needs. Special working-time arrangements for certain activities are set by Royal Decree 1561/1995, and stricter rules apply to night work and shift work under Article 36.

**Overtime (Article 35).** Overtime is work performed beyond ordinary working time. It is voluntary unless a collective agreement or individual contract provides otherwise, and it must be compensated either with pay at no less than the ordinary hourly rate or with paid time off, which, in the absence of agreement, must be granted within four months. It is capped at 80 hours a year, excluding hours worked to prevent or repair accidents and other extraordinary and urgent damage, with a proportionally lower cap for workers whose annual working time is shorter than the company's general working time; hours compensated by time off within four months do not count towards the cap. For calculating overtime, working time is recorded day by day and shown on the payslip. See [[DefinedTerm/overtime]].

**Paid leave (Article 37).** Weekly rest is a day and a half, which may be accumulated over 14 days and as a general rule includes Saturday afternoon or Monday morning and the whole of Sunday; workers under 18 have at least two uninterrupted days. There are 14 paid, non-recoverable public holidays a year. Paid leave with prior notice and justification is available for specified reasons, including 15 days for marriage or registered partnership; 5 days for the accident, serious illness, hospitalisation or surgery requiring home rest of a spouse, registered partner, relative up to the second degree or cohabiting person; 2 days (4 where travel is needed) for the death of a spouse, registered partner or relative up to the second degree; 1 day for moving house; and the time necessary for public and personal duties, trade union duties, prenatal examinations and preparation for adoption or foster care, and organ or tissue donation. Up to four days are available when the worker cannot reach the workplace because of restrictions issued by the competent authority or a serious and imminent risk, extendable while the circumstances persist.

**Annual holidays (Article 38).** Paid annual holiday is at least 30 calendar days and may not be replaced by financial compensation. Holiday periods are set by agreement between the employer and the employee.
