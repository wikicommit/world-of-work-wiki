The El Khomri law is French Law No 2016-1088 of 8 August 2016 on labour, the modernisation of social dialogue and the securing of professional careers. The EU-OSHA policy brief [[Report/osh-in-digital-platform-work-lessons-from-regulations-policies-actions-and-initiatives]] treats it as one of the first laws in an EU Member State aimed specifically at (part of) the platform economy, and the first in a series of French legislative initiatives on digital platform work introduced since 2016.

The law provides that platforms which determine the characteristics of the goods sold or services provided, and set their price, have a "social responsibility" towards the workers active on them. Its personal scope is limited to self-employed workers who use [[DefinedTerm/digital-labour-platform]]s in the course of their professional activity.

## Details

For [[DefinedTerm/digital-platform-worker]]s within that scope, the law provides the right to form and join a trade union and to defend their collective interests through it. Those who also earn at least 13% of the annual social security ceiling through platform work (€5,347.68 in 2021) are entitled to continuous professional training and to insurance against work-related accidents and occupational diseases, with the platform bearing the cost. In practice, the brief notes, this means platforms reimburse workers in the following year once they can prove that their turnover met the threshold, and taking out the insurance remains voluntary.

The brief reports that research and policy have often described the law as a major step towards protecting platform workers that could inspire other Member States, but that a more critical assessment points to its limited scope and the limited rights it grants, and to its only indirect links to occupational safety and health.
