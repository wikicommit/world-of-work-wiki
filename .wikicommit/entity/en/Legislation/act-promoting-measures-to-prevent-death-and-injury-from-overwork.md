The Act Promoting Measures to Prevent Death and Injury from Overwork (Act No. 100 of 2014; 過労死等防止対策推進法) is the Japanese law that frames the government's policy on [[DefinedTerm/karoshi]]. It came into force in November 2014. It gives "death and injury from overwork" a statutory definition and obliges the government to report annually to the Diet on the situation and on what it has done to prevent it.

According to the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's workers' mental health portal *Kokoro no Mimi*, the Act aims to promote measures against karoshi so as to help bring about a society without it, in which people can balance work and life and keep working in good health and with fulfilment.

## Details

**Definition.** Article 2 defines death and injury from overwork as death due to cerebrovascular disease or heart disease brought on by an overload of work; death by suicide due to a mental disorder brought on by an intense psychological burden at work; or cerebrovascular disease, heart disease or a mental disorder brought on by such work-related causes.

**Outline of measures.** Under the Act the government sets an Outline of Measures to Prevent Death and Injury from Overwork to promote prevention effectively. The first outline was adopted by Cabinet decision in July 2015, and the outline has been reviewed roughly every three years since; the portal cites the version adopted by Cabinet decision on 30 July 2021, while the 2025 white paper refers to the outline adopted by Cabinet decision on 2 August 2024, which designates industries and sectors as priority targets for survey and research.

**Council.** The Act establishes a Council for the Promotion of Measures to Prevent Karoshi etc. within the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare, and the Minister of Health, Labour and Welfare is to hear the council's views when drafting the outline.

**Annual reports.** Article 6 requires the government to submit to the Diet every year a report summarising death and injury from overwork in Japan and describing the implementation of the measures it has taken to prevent it. This report is the statutory White Paper on Measures to Prevent Karoshi, etc., published every year since 2016; its 2025 edition was the tenth ([[Report/white-paper-on-measures-to-prevent-karoshi-2025]]).

**Research and awareness.** The ministry conducts surveys and research to clarify the actual state of karoshi. Every November is designated Karoshi Prevention Awareness Month, during which symposiums on promoting prevention and a campaign to eliminate overwork are held.
