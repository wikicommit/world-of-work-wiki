The Spanish Remote Work Act — Law 10/2021 of 9 July on remote work (*Ley 10/2021, de 9 de julio, de trabajo a distancia*) — regulates remote work and telework in Spain. According to the Spanish National Institute for Safety and Health at Work (INSST), it implicitly repealed Royal Decree-Law 28/2020 of 22 September on remote work, a transitional regulation adopted during the COVID-19 pandemic. It applies to employment relationships meeting the conditions of Article 1.1 of the [[Legislation/workers-statute]], that is, to employees working for others.

Article 2 of the law defines remote work as a way of organising work or performing the work activity in which it is carried out at the worker's home or a place the worker chooses, for all or part of the working day, on a regular basis; telework is a subcategory of remote work carried out through the exclusive or predominant use of computer, telematic and telecommunication means and systems.

## Details

As summarised by INSST in [[Report/guidance-on-managing-ergonomic-and-psychosocial-aspects-of-telework]]:

**Scope.** Remote work is "regular", and the law applies, when it is performed for at least 30% of working time over a three-month reference period, or the proportional equivalent depending on the length of the contract; collective agreements may set a lower percentage or reference period and may include a minimum amount of on-site work. The law does not apply to remote work used as a containment measure in health crises, remote work by employees that falls below these thresholds, self-employed remote work or remote work in the public administrations, for which a separate provision was added to the Basic Statute of Public Employees by Royal Decree-Law 29/2020.

**Voluntariness and the agreement.** Remote work is voluntary for both the worker and the company and, where a worker moves to it from on-site work, reversible by either party. Regular remote work must be formalised in a written remote work agreement whose minimum content, under Article 7, includes an inventory of the equipment and tools provided and their useful life, the working schedule and availability rules, the split between on-site and remote work, the company's work centre, the place or places of remote work chosen by the worker, the means of employer control, the procedure for technical difficulties, instructions on data protection and information security, and the agreement's duration. Under Article 9 the company must provide the training needed for remote work, both when the agreement is formalised and when the means or technologies used change, and Article 4.3 obliges companies to avoid discrimination, including on grounds of sex and age.

**Occupational safety and health.** Remote workers keep the right to adequate safety and health protection under the Law on the Prevention of Occupational Risks. The law requires the planning of preventive action for remote work to take account of its characteristic risks, paying special attention to psychosocial, ergonomic and organisational factors and to the accessibility of the working environment, and in several articles it points to particular aspects: the distribution of working time, availability, guaranteed rest periods, disconnection during the working day, the risk of computer fatigue and the various forms of harassment. Under Article 16, the risk assessment covers only the area designated for work, not the rest of the home, and must use a methodology that gives confidence in its results. Where obtaining information requires a visit to the place of remote work by the person responsible for prevention, a written report justifying it must be given to the worker and the prevention delegates, and the worker's permission is required if it is their home or that of a third party; if permission is refused, the preventive activity may be based on information collected from the worker following the prevention service's instructions.
