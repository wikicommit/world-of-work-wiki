The Bologna Charter — the Charter of Fundamental Rights of Digital Labour in the Urban Context — is a charter adopted in the Italian city of Bologna in 2018. According to the EU-OSHA policy brief [[Report/osh-in-digital-platform-work-lessons-from-regulations-policies-actions-and-initiatives]], it originated after a heavy snowfall in autumn 2017, when a group of delivery riders went on strike and marched to the city hall to demand decent working conditions for platform workers. The City Council, reasoning that the riders' workplace is the city's streets, opened negotiations with trade unions and [[DefinedTerm/digital-labour-platform]]s.

The brief places the Charter's legal status within tripartite social dialogue, with the city authorities as the state-side party. It applies only in Bologna's territory but covers all platform workers irrespective of their employment status, thereby avoiding questions about the qualification of the employment relationship over which the local level has no legal competence; in practice its main focus is platform delivery services.

## Details

**Provisions.** Because OSH concerns were among the main demands of the riders' union that appealed to the City Council, the Charter is particularly ambitious on OSH: it requires platforms to develop an OSH management system, to adopt appropriate measures to assess, prevent and reduce risks and hazards, and to provide insurance against work-related accidents and occupational diseases. It gives workers the right to refuse tasks without repercussions in extraordinary weather conditions. It also contains provisions on [[DefinedTerm/algorithmic-management]] and its impact on OSH, minimum remuneration and how it is calculated, transparency and worker participation.

**Scope and effect.** The brief notes the Charter's limited reach: only four delivery platforms had signed the voluntary agreement, and its non-binding nature, combined with the city's lack of competence to legislate on its core provisions, makes hard enforcement impossible. It nonetheless credits the Charter with a direct positive effect on platform workers' working conditions in Bologna — stakeholders consulted acknowledged a difference between signatory and non-signatory platforms — and with raising awareness in Italy, reflected nationally in Legislative Decree No 101/2019 and regionally and locally in similar agreements (for example in Piedmont, Lazio, Milan and Modena). Regional initiatives in Lazio and Piedmont in 2019 appear, in the brief's assessment, to have been inspired by Bologna's actions.
