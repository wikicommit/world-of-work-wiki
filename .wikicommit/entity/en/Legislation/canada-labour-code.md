The Canada Labour Code is the federal statute whose Part III sets labour standards for federally regulated workplaces in Canada. Division I of Part III governs hours of work: it fixes standard hours of 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week, a weekly maximum of 48 hours in most cases, overtime pay, a weekly day of rest, breaks and rest periods, and minimum notice of work schedules and shift changes. The hours-of-work division does not apply to managers, superintendents, employees who exercise management functions, or members of the architectural, dental, engineering, legal or medical professions.

Several regulations adapt these provisions for particular classes of employees — drivers in the trucking industry, employees in East Coast, Great Lakes and West Coast shipping, railway running trades, and commission salespeople in broadcasting and banking — and the Exemptions from and Modifications to Hours of Work Provisions Regulations list further classes, by sector, for whom specified provisions are modified or do not apply.

## Hours of work

For hours-of-work purposes a day is any period of 24 consecutive hours and a week runs from midnight on Saturday to midnight on the following Saturday; the standard week is reduced by 8 hours for each general holiday in it. Hours beyond the standard are [[DefinedTerm/overtime]], paid at no less than 1.5 times the regular hourly wage or, on the employee's written request and agreement, compensated with 1.5 hours of paid time off per hour worked, to be taken within three months (or a longer period set by a collective agreement or, without one, agreed in writing up to 12 months). Employees may refuse overtime to meet family responsibilities for the health or care of family members or the education of family members under 18, after taking reasonable steps to meet them otherwise, except where the overtime is needed to deal with an unforeseeable situation posing an imminent or serious threat to life, health or safety, to property, or to the normal operation of the establishment.

The 48-hour maximum can be exceeded under an excess hours permit granted for exceptional circumstances, for emergency work, under an averaging plan, or under a modified work schedule. Where the nature of the work makes hours irregular, the employer may average hours over two or more weeks. Modified work schedules, such as compressed work weeks or flexible hours, must not exceed an average of 40 standard and 48 maximum hours a week over the schedule's period; they require written agreement with the union where there is a collective agreement, and otherwise approval in writing by the individual employee and the employer or, for a group, approval by at least 70% of the affected employees; in either case the employer must post notice at least 30 days before the schedule starts.

Employees are entitled to an unpaid break of at least 30 minutes in every five consecutive hours of work (paid if they must remain at the employer's disposal), a rest period of at least 8 consecutive hours between shifts, written notice of their schedule at least 96 hours in advance — with a right to refuse shifts starting within that period, subject to the same threat-based exception — and at least 24 hours' written notice of a shift change. Employers must keep records of the hours each employee works each day for 36 months.

## Trucking industry

In the trucking industry, the Motor Vehicle Operators Hours of Work Regulations replace the standard and maximum hours of the Code for bus operators, city motor vehicle operators and highway motor vehicle operators — in each case those involved in the interprovincial transportation of passengers or goods or in the transportation of mail anywhere in Canada. Overtime becomes payable after 60 hours a week for highway operators, after 9 hours a day or 45 a week for city operators, and after 8 hours a day or 40 a week for bus operators, shunt drivers and non-driving personnel; drivers' maximum hours are those set by the Commercial Vehicle Drivers Hours of Service Regulations. A city operator is one who works within a 16 km radius of the home terminal or is classified as such by a collective agreement or by prevailing industry practice, which the Labour Program establishes through surveys of trucking companies in the area.
