The Employment Rights Act 2025 (2025 c. 36) is a UK Act that, according to the policy background in its Explanatory Notes, implements the Government's Plan to Make Work Pay. The Government presents it as serving both businesses and working people: maximising job security to raise productivity, improve skills and cut the costs of staff turnover, while offering working people opportunity and security.

The Act is broad rather than focused on a single right. Its measures fall into seven groups: ending "one-sided" flexibility in variable-hours work, family-friendly rights, dismissal and redundancy, equality at work, fair pay and conditions, trade union law ("voice at work"), and the enforcement of employment rights. Several of its provisions are framed as powers to be exercised later through secondary legislation.

## Details

**One-sided flexibility.** The notes accept that variable hours can benefit both sides but say that, without safeguards, workers bear the financial risk. The Act introduces a right to a guaranteed hours contract reflecting the hours a qualifying worker regularly works over a reference period, a right to reasonable notice of shifts, and a right to payment when shifts are cancelled or curtailed at short notice, applying to agency workers as well as directly engaged workers.

**Family-friendly rights.** The Act expands powers to ban dismissals of women who are pregnant, on Maternity Leave or in a six-month return-to-work period (except in specific circumstances), with similar powers for those returning from Adoption, Shared Parental, Neonatal Care and Bereaved Partners Paternity Leave. It raises the bar for refusing flexible working requests so that employers must accept one unless it is not reasonably feasible (see [[DefinedTerm/right-to-request-flexible-working-arrangements]]); makes Paternity Leave and Parental Leave day-one rights, removing the previous 26-week and one-year service requirements; lets Paternity Leave and Pay be taken after Shared Parental Leave and Pay; and creates a new right to Bereavement Leave covering the loss of other loved ones, including pregnancy loss before 24 weeks.

**Dismissal and redundancy.** The qualifying period for claiming unfair dismissal falls from two years to six months, as does the period for the right to a written statement of reasons for dismissal, and the cap on compensatory awards for unfair dismissal is removed. Dismissals for refusing certain contract changes become automatically unfair unless the employer shows financial difficulty and that the change was unavoidable, restricting [[DefinedTerm/fire-and-rehire]]. Collective consultation duties extend to redundancies spread across more than one establishment, the maximum protective award rises from 90 to 180 days' pay, and operators can no longer avoid redundancy notification duties by registering ships outside the UK.

**Equality at work.** Employers already required to publish gender pay gap data (those with 250 or more employees) can be required to publish an equality action plan. The duty to prevent sexual harassment is strengthened from "reasonable steps" to "all reasonable steps", with a power for ministers to specify what those steps are; employers must not permit harassment of their employees by third parties; sexual harassment is added to the failures that qualify for whistleblowing protection; and provisions in non-disclosure agreements that stop a worker speaking out about harassment and discrimination are void.

**Fair pay and conditions.** Employers must consult workers when developing or revising tipping policies. The Act establishes a School Support Staff Negotiating Body for school support staff in England and enables Fair Pay Agreement processes in adult social care in England and in the social care sectors of Scotland and Wales. It extends eligibility for [[DefinedTerm/statutory-sick-pay]] and removes its waiting days, gives ministers tools to protect the terms of employees on newly outsourced public contracts, and provides for a legally binding Seafarers' Charter setting higher standards on pay and safety, including tours of duty, fatigue management and safety training.

**Voice at work.** The Act repeals the Strikes (Minimum Service Levels) Act 2023's amendments and most of the Trade Union Act 2016's amendments to the Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992. It simplifies industrial action ballot and notice requirements, extends the mandate for industrial action from 6 to 12 months, sets a 10-day notice period for industrial action, streamlines statutory union recognition, creates a framework for union access to workplaces (including digital access), strengthens facilities and facility time for union representatives, protects striking workers against detriment, requires employers to tell workers of their right to join a union, and widens protection against blacklisting.

**Enforcement.** The notes observe that most employment rights are enforced by individuals through employment tribunals, which is often hard for workers with limited resources. The Act allows the establishment of the [[Organization/fair-work-agency]], extends the time limit for most tribunal claims from three to six months, and brings umbrella companies within regulation and enforcement.
