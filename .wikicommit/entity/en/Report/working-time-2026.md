Durée du travail ("Working time") is a sheet in the July 2026 edition of *Emploi, chômage, revenus du travail*, a volume in Insee's Insee Références collection, published on 2 July 2026. Based on the French Labour Force Survey (enquête Emploi), it sets out how long full-time employees in France worked in 2025, how that compares with earlier years since 2003, and how working time differs between socio-occupational groups, sectors, women and men, and the self-employed.

It reports that in 2025 the usual weekly working time of full-time employees was 38.9 hours, up 6 minutes on 2024 and back to its 2023 level, and that their annual actual working time averaged 1,656 hours. Annual working time fell sharply in 2020 (-101 hours) because of the measures taken in response to the health crisis, in particular the massive use of short-time working, recovered until 2023, and has declined over the last two years, leaving it in 2025 slightly below its 2019 level (-8 hours, or -0.5%).

## Details

The sheet uses two measures. Usual weekly working time ([[DefinedTerm/usual-hours-of-work]]) applies to a normal week without exceptional events such as a public holiday or leave, and includes regular overtime. Annual actual working time ([[DefinedTerm/actual-hours-of-work]]) is time worked including paid or unpaid overtime, excluding hours not worked because of annual leave, public holidays, sickness, accidents, maternity or paternity leave, short-time working, strikes and the like; it is calculated as the average of hours actually worked in the survey reference weeks over the year, multiplied by 52. The series covers metropolitan France until 2014 and France excluding Mayotte from 2014, and Insee notes that changes between 2012 and 2013 may partly reflect a change in the survey questionnaire.

Among full-time employees, managers reported the longest working time in 2025: 1,775 actual hours a year, 42.0 usual hours a week and 8.3 hours a day. The other socio-occupational groups worked markedly less (between 1,596 and 1,632 hours a year and between 37.3 and 38.2 hours a week). Annual working time was higher in market services (1,692 hours) than in non-market services (1,576 hours); within market services, information and communication and financial services had the longest usual weeks (40.0 hours each) and among the longest annual hours. In non-market services, employees in education worked the fewest annual hours (1,481), mainly because they worked fewer days (193 against an average of 212); the sheet notes that teachers do not always report their preparation time on top of their time in class.

Women working full-time worked on average 1,591 hours a year, significantly less than men (1,708 hours), a gap that partly reflects differences in occupations and sectors. Working time is also linked to family situation, in opposite directions for women and men: among full-time employees aged 25 to 49, women living alone worked on average 93 hours more than women in couples with children, while men living alone worked 24 hours less than men in couples with children.

The self-employed reported working on average 1,903 actual hours in 2025, 247 hours more than full-time employees. Their daily working time (7.9 hours) was very close to that of employees, but they worked on average 29 more days a year. Part-time employees worked 966 hours a year and 23.2 usual hours a week ([[DefinedTerm/part-time-employment]]).
