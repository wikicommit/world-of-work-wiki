The *Encuesta Nacional sobre Uso del Tiempo (ENUT) 2024* results report (Reporte de resultados 29/25) is the release in which Mexico's Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Geografía (INEGI) presented the main results of the 2024 edition of the [[Dataset/mexico-national-time-use-survey]]. Published on 28 August 2025, it describes how people aged 12 and over spent the reference week on paid work for the market, on unpaid domestic, care, community and volunteer work, and on personal activities such as study, leisure and personal care, and how this differed between women and men, by state, by size of locality and for Indigenous-language speakers, Afro-descendant people and people with a disability.

The report is organised around [[DefinedTerm/total-work-time]], the sum of paid and unpaid work. In 2024 this averaged 59.6 hours a week: 61.1 hours for women and 58.0 for men. Market work accounted for 48.4% of the population's total work time, and the rest was [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]]. The split differed sharply by sex: women spent 66.8% of their total work time on unpaid work, men 33.2%.

## Details

**Types of work.** In 2024, 98.3% of the population did some unpaid domestic, care, community or volunteer work, 23.5% produced goods for their own household's consumption, and 60.2% did paid work for the market. Market work showed the largest gap in participation, 26.7 percentage points higher for men than for women ([[DefinedTerm/time-use-participation-rate]]). Men spent 9.1 hours a week more than women on market work, while women spent 21.5 hours a week more than men on unpaid domestic, care, community and volunteer work. Total work time was highest in Hidalgo, Querétaro and Durango and lowest in Chihuahua, Sinaloa and Baja California.

**Change since 2014.** For comparison with earlier editions the report excludes emotional care, a variable first captured in 2024. On that basis average total work time fell from 60.2 hours a week in 2019 to 55.8 in 2024, and women's lead over men narrowed to 2.3 hours, from more than 3 hours in 2014 and 2019. Participation in caring for household members fell between 2014 and 2024 for both women (by 5.8 percentage points) and men (by 5.1), while men's participation in domestic work rose by 1.8 points.

**Unpaid domestic and care work.** In 2024, 97.3% of people did domestic work for their own household, 67.8% cared for household members and 21.6% did volunteer or community work or helped other households. Average weekly time was 20.5 hours on domestic work, 11.4 on care and 6.9 on volunteer, community and support work; women spent more than men on all three, with the widest gap (16.7 hours) in domestic work. The largest single domestic gap was in preparing and serving food (13.0 hours for women against 4.7 for men). Women working 40 hours or more a week in paid work still did 12.7 hours more domestic work than men in the same situation. Children aged 0 to 5 received the most care time and showed the largest gap (9.4 hours more from women); counting [[DefinedTerm/passive-care]] as well, women spent 33.4 hours a week caring for this age group and men 14.8.

**Paid work arrangements.** Among employees who worked in the reference week, 94.9% worked only in person, 3.5% worked in a hybrid arrangement and 1.6% worked only remotely. Those working in person or hybrid averaged about 52 to 53 hours of paid work a week (including travel), against 37.6 hours for those working only remotely.

**Personal activities.** People aged 12 to 29 spent more than 28 hours a week attending classes or studying. Use of mass media (television, radio or mobile phone) was the most widespread leisure activity, reported by 91.7% of people for an average of 15.4 hours a week. People slept an average of 52.5 hours a week, about seven and a half hours a day.

**Satisfaction with time use.** Among people aged 20 to 49 who did economic activity in the reference week, about 3 in 10 women and men said they would like to spend less time on their work, and about 6 in 10 were content with it. More women (15.2%) than men (6.4%) wanted to spend less time on domestic work, while most people wanted more time for caring for household members (68.2% of men and 62.4% of women).

**Hired domestic workers.** Six per cent of households hired someone for domestic work, nursing, or the care of older people, people with a disability, or children. The share was higher in households headed by a woman, particularly for carers of older people or people with a disability.
