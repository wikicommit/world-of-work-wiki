"Organisering av arbete och dess betydelse för hälsa och välbefinnande" (The organisation of work and its significance for health and well-being) is a knowledge compilation published in February 2020 by the Swedish Agency for Work Environment Expertise (Myndigheten för arbetsmiljökunskap) as number 2020:7 in its Kunskapssammanställningar series. In June 2018 the Swedish government asked the agency to compile knowledge on factors that create healthy and thriving workplaces, with a particular focus on the organisational and social work environment; the agency commissioned researchers to produce compilations in four areas — the physical work environment, leadership, the organisation of work and the psychosocial work environment — and this report covers the organisation of work. It was prepared by researchers at Luleå University of Technology, who chose their own theoretical and methodological starting points and are responsible for its conclusions.

The compilation is a rapid review based on a systematic literature search in Scopus and Web of Science, supplemented by manual review of two Nordic working-life journals. It includes 80 publications from 2009–2019, mostly concerning Swedish and other Nordic workplaces. A majority deal with health care, social care and social services, largely in the public sector; most use quantitative methods; and where an established theoretical model is used, the demand–control–support model is the most common, and the [[DefinedTerm/effort-reward-imbalance]] model also appears.

## Details

**Conflicting logics in the public sector.** The first main theme concerns welfare organisations reshaped by New Public Management (NPM), which the reviewed studies describe as shifting the organisation of work from the logic of the profession towards the logic of the organisation (bureaucracy) and, to some extent, the market. Because welfare professionals identify more with the professional logic, the clash is described as producing conflicting demands, reduced professional influence, increased workload (notably more documentation and detailed control), reduced social support and poorer conditions for learning and competence development. The authors conclude that welfare organisations may need to consider giving the professional logic more legitimacy, which could lead to fewer conflicting demands, more influence and possibly a more reasonable workload.

**Organisation of work in time and space.** The second main theme covers employment security and employment forms (permanent, fixed-term and [[DefinedTerm/temporary-agency-work]]), the scheduling of working time over the day, week and year, and the spatial organisation of work, such as activity-based offices and organisational changes like outsourcing. Insecure employment is associated with negative effects for the individual (see [[DefinedTerm/job-insecurity]]), but the picture is complex: whether a given arrangement is experienced as good or bad depends largely on the individual's own wishes, situation and social position. The authors conclude that flexibility can be health-promoting, but only if it starts from employees' perspectives — for example through influence over their own working time.

**Efficiency and productivity.** The review finds that a focus on a causal relationship between the organisation of work and employee health on the one hand and organisational efficiency and productivity on the other is almost absent from the included publications, and that health, well-being, productivity and efficiency are rarely defined explicitly.

**Knowledge gaps.** These include research on health-promoting factors rather than problems, on standard permanent employment, on how digitalisation and artificial intelligence affect health and well-being, on explicit gender, class, ethnicity and age perspectives, and on conditions for continuous learning at work.

**Advice to employers.** The report advises employers to organise work so that employees have influence over their work situation and less detailed control; for public-sector employers, to consider which governing logics they give precedence; to support meaningful work, career development and workplace learning; to work for inclusive practices; to build lasting social relations and trust; to balance demands and resources and work and leisure, including the possibility of disconnecting fully from work; to include agency workers in competence development; and, together with other actors, to address the use of fixed-term and agency employment, which it describes as generally worse for individuals' health and well-being.
