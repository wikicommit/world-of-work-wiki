*Arbeidsomstandigheden* ("Working conditions") is chapter 4 of *De arbeidsmarkt in cijfers 2025* ("The labour market in figures 2025"), an overview of the Dutch labour market published by Statistics Netherlands (CBS) in 2026. The chapter describes the working conditions of employees and self-employed people and how they have changed in recent years. Its figures come mainly from the [[Dataset/netherlands-working-conditions-survey]] (NEA) among employees and the Self-employed Labour Survey (ZEA) among self-employed people, both conducted with TNO and both with 2025 as the most recent reporting year; figures on sickness absence by industry come from CBS's quarterly survey of sickness absence.

The chapter's framing is that physical and mental strain at work can lead to fatigue, sickness absence, work accidents and occupational diseases. It finds that work pressure among employees has fallen over ten years while work-related mental fatigue and the sickness absence rate are higher than a decade ago, and that the health and care and education sectors stand out on most measures of mental strain.

## Details

**Physical strain.** In 2025, 37% of employees and 41.5% of self-employed people said they regularly did physically heavy work, with larger differences between men (46%) and women (34%) among the self-employed than among employees (38% and 36%). Work requiring a lot of physical force was most common in construction and in agriculture and fishing. Employees spent on average 4.4 hours a day at a screen at work, rising to 7.3 hours in financial services and in information and communication. Repetitive movements fell from 35% of employees in 2015 to 28% in 2025. Employees sat on average 8.8 hours per working day across work, commuting and leisure, with those who regularly work from home sitting 6.4 hours a day against 3.0 hours for those who always work on location (see [[DefinedTerm/sedentary-work]]). Thirteen per cent of employees and 14% of self-employed people did dangerous work often or always, most commonly among young employees.

**Work pressure and autonomy.** The share of employees who often or always experienced work pressure fell from 38% in 2015 to 31% in 2025; among the self-employed it was 22%. Female employees reported it more often than male employees (34.5% against 28%), which CBS links partly to the industries in which they work. Emotionally demanding work affected 9.1% of employees, most in health and care (20%) and education (16%). Fifty-seven per cent of employees and 86% of self-employed people regularly experienced autonomy in their work ([[DefinedTerm/job-autonomy]]). Fifteen per cent of employees had a stressful job combining high work pressure with little autonomy, most often in hotels and restaurants (28%) and in education and health and care (23% each); such employees were more pessimistic about their future at work and more often said measures against work pressure were insufficient or absent.

**Discrimination and undesirable behaviour.** Eleven per cent of employees experienced discrimination at work in 2025, most often on grounds of origin, skin colour or nationality (3.2%), age (2.6%) and sex (2.2%). Seventeen per cent of employees and 18% of self-employed people experienced undesirable behaviour at least once — mainly intimidation and threats, bullying, unwanted sexual attention and physical violence — with women reporting it more often than men and health and care the most affected industry ([[DefinedTerm/workplace-harassment]]).

**Mental fatigue and sickness absence.** Twenty-one per cent of employees felt mentally fatigued by work several times a month or more in 2025, against 13% ten years earlier; among the self-employed the share rose from 7.5% in 2015 to 12%. The sickness absence rate of employees was 5.4% in 2025, against 3.9% in 2015, and was highest in health and care (7.5%). Older workers who were absent stayed away longer: 27 days on average for employees aged 55 to 65 against 14 days for those aged 25 to 35. About 22.5% of employees and 23% of self-employed people said their most recent absence was wholly or partly caused by work, and among employees with work-related absence 28% named excessive work pressure as an important cause.

**Accidents and occupational diseases.** In 2025, 2.5% of employees and 2.2% of self-employed people reported one or more work accidents in the previous twelve months, the highest employee share being in hotels and restaurants (5.4%); 24 fatal work accidents were registered in 2022, the most recent year available ([[DefinedTerm/accident-at-work]]). A newly arisen occupational disease diagnosed by a doctor was reported by 3.8% of employees and 2.1% of self-employed people, more often by female than male employees (4.4% against 3.2%); counting older ones, 11% of employees had one or more diagnosed occupational diseases ([[DefinedTerm/occupational-disease]]).
