*Strategy for the Promotion of Formalization in Latin America and the Caribbean (2024-2030)*, known as FORLAC 2.0, is a strategy document published by the International Labour Organization (ILO) on 21 May 2024, also available in Spanish. It sets out the ILO's initiative to relaunch its formalisation strategy in Latin America and the Caribbean, starting from the observation that half of all working people in the region work in the informal economy and lack access to decent work in the formal economy — a challenge the ILO calls an urgent priority.

The ILO describes informality ([[DefinedTerm/informal-employment]]) in the region as structural, multidimensional and persistent. It mainly affects women, youth, people belonging to certain ethnic groups and migrants, and falls more heavily on the less educated, people in low value-added agricultural activities, the working poor, smaller enterprises, the self-employed and people in new forms of employment, in both rural and urban areas; according to the latest figures it cites, informality increased during the recovery from the pandemic. Drawing on the ILO's Recommendation on the transition from the informal to the formal economy (No. 204), the document presents high informality as an obstacle not only to guaranteeing workers' rights but also to the development of sustainable enterprises and to the financial sustainability of the State.

## Details

**Background.** The ILO Regional Office for Latin America and the Caribbean launched the Programme for the Formalization of Informality in Latin America and the Caribbean (FORLAC) in 2013 and implemented it over 2013–2017, linked to projects at country and sub-regional level. It offered the ILO's constituents knowledge generation, management of information systems such as surveys and administrative records, advice and technical assistance on comprehensive strategies, training for representatives of governments, employers and workers, institutional capacity building, exchange of successful experiences, and sensitisation of economic and social actors. Its achievements included contributing to the design of the ILO's global formalisation strategy, strengthening the Office's technical teams, promoting social dialogue and public policy advocacy, and increasing the exchange of experience between countries and ILO offices. Citing the recovery of the informal employment rate after the pandemic, a growing trend in some countries and increasing demand from constituents, the Regional Office decided to relaunch the initiative as FORLAC 2.0, to be implemented over three and a half ILO two-year planning and budgeting cycles (2024–2025, 2026–2027, 2028–2029 and 2030).

**Contents.** The document reviews conceptual issues and the regional context, then describes the strategy's objectives and premises, its theory of change, an integrated policy framework for formalisation, a portfolio of ILO services and technical cooperation, and the role of social dialogue and co-creation. It also contains an implementation roadmap, guidelines for monitoring and evaluation, a management model, and guidance on programming, budgeting, fund mobilisation and communication.
