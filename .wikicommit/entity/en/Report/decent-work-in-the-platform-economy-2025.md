*Decent work in the platform economy* (ILC.114/Report V(3)) is a report of the International Labour Office, first published in 2025 for the fifth item on the agenda of the 114th Session (2026) of the International Labour Conference. It sets out the texts of a draft Convention concerning decent work in the platform economy, supplemented by a draft Recommendation, together with an Office commentary on them. The Office drafted the texts from the Conference's first discussion of the standard-setting item and from Member States' replies to the questionnaire in its preliminary report. Governments were asked to send any comments, after consulting the most representative organizations of employers and workers, by 14 November 2025, so that they could be reflected in a fourth and final report for the Conference's session in June 2026.

The report is the third of the Office's reports on this item. The ILO Governing Body, having taken note of a normative gap analysis at its 347th Session in March 2023, had placed the item on the agenda of the 113th Session (June 2025) under a double-discussion procedure. On 13 June 2025 the Conference adopted a resolution containing proposed Conclusions. Because time ran short, its Standard-Setting Committee had examined only the Conclusions with a view to a Convention, and had agreed on the form of the instruments, their definitions and their scope. The Office therefore placed the parts of the draft texts that came from Conclusions the Committee had not considered in square brackets and italics.

## Details

**Office commentary.** The Office read the Committee as favouring a "principles-based" Convention that states key principles rather than detailed rules, and it points to Articles that could be moved to the Recommendation, reworded, deleted or merged. To give Member States more room to adapt how some provisions apply, including to the self-employed, without narrowing coverage, it proposes adding the phrase "according to the nature of work arrangements and the classification of digital platform workers' status in employment" to several provisions. It also flags the ambiguity of "organizes and/or facilitates work" in the definition of a [[DefinedTerm/digital-labour-platform]]. Read as "or", the definition would reach social media, online advertising and search engines, as well as private employment agencies that use automated decision-making; read as "and", it would leave out platforms that only match workers with work. The Office notes that this would be the first time "and/or" appeared in a definition in a Convention. It further asks whether "or 'payment'" should be added throughout the texts, so that the labour income of employees and of the self-employed is distinguished consistently.

**Draft Convention.** The Convention as drafted may be cited as the Decent Work in the Platform Economy Convention, 2026. It defines digital labour platforms, [[DefinedTerm/digital-platform-worker]]s, intermediaries, and "remuneration" or "payment". It covers every digital labour platform and every digital platform worker, in the formal or the informal economy, and lets a Member exclude limited categories of platforms or workers after consultation where special problems of a substantial nature arise. Its provisions deal with fundamental principles and rights at work, occupational safety and health (including injury to health linked to long working hours and too little rest), violence and harassment, employment promotion, correct classification of the employment relationship, remuneration, social security, automated systems used to monitor or evaluate work or to generate decisions, personal data and privacy, suspension or deactivation of accounts, terms and conditions, migrants and refugees, dispute resolution, and compliance and enforcement. Article 17 requires that, where an automated system generates decisions, workers have access without undue delay to a written explanation of any decision affecting their working conditions or access to work, and to a human review of any decision that refuses payment, suspends or deactivates their account, or ends their employment or engagement.

**Draft Recommendation.** The Recommendation adds more detailed measures. Among them: remuneration at least equivalent to the statutory or negotiated minimum wage applicable to other workers in a comparable situation; leaving expenses and tips out of account when compliance with a minimum is assessed; compensation for time spent waiting for work; protection in relation to limits on daily and weekly hours, rest breaks and minimum daily and weekly rest; the ability to turn down a task or disconnect from a platform without retaliation; platforms notifying the competent authority of their automated systems; portability of workers' personal data, ratings included; and the minimum content of contracts between platform workers and platforms.

**Replies and revision.** The comments on this report, and the revised drafts that followed, appear in the Office's fourth and final report, [[Report/realizing-decent-work-in-the-platform-economy-2026]] (ILC.114/Report V(4), March 2026), which draws on replies from 74 governments, 106 employers' organizations and 131 workers' organizations. Noting a broadly shared view that the text agreed by the Committee in 2025 should not be reopened, the Office left the definition of a digital labour platform — "organizes and/or facilitates work" included — and the scope unchanged; it added "or payment" to the title and provisions on remuneration, but not to the second sentence of the definition. A majority of employers' and workers' organizations wanted the "nature of work arrangements" formula cut back to a reference to the classification of status in employment, and the revised drafts instead distinguish explicitly between platform workers in an employment relationship and those outside one. Several Articles this report had suggested moving or deleting — among them the detailed occupational safety and health measures and most of the personal data provisions — were moved to the Recommendation or removed, and the drafts shrank from 31 Articles and 35 Paragraphs to 24 Articles and 19 Paragraphs.
