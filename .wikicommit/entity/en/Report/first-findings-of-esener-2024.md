This EU-OSHA publication presents a first analysis of the main findings of the 2024 wave of the [[Dataset/esener]], the agency's fourth European establishment survey. Between May and October 2024 the survey asked "the person who knows best about health and safety in the establishment" in 41,458 establishments with at least five employees — across all activity sectors, in the EU-27, Iceland, Norway and Switzerland — how safety and health risks are managed at their workplace, with a particular focus on [[DefinedTerm/psychosocial-risks]], that is, work-related stress, violence and harassment.

The findings are organised around the four areas ESENER explores: the general approach to managing occupational safety and health (OSH); how psychosocial risks, including those linked to digitalisation, are addressed; the main drivers of and barriers to OSH management; and how worker participation works in practice. Most results are reported for the EU-27 and compared with the 2014 and 2019 waves. EU-OSHA describes the report as only a first look, says its conclusions should be interpreted with caution, and announces more detailed analyses for 2026 and beyond.

## Details

**A changing world of work.** The share of EU-27 workplaces with employees [[DefinedTerm/working-from-home]] on a regular basis almost doubled since 2019, to 23% in 2024 — over 40% in Finland and the Netherlands, and 53% in information and communication. Risk assessments increasingly cover home workplaces (8% of all establishments, up from 3% in 2019); counted among establishments that both carry out risk assessments and have regular home-workers, the share covering such workers rose to 48%, from 31% in 2019. Less than a fifth (18%) of workplaces consult employees on working-from-home practices. Risk assessments cover the use of digital technologies in 43% of workplaces, and training on their use is provided in 42%.

**Risk factors.** The most frequently reported risk factors in the EU-27 are prolonged sitting (64% of establishments, up from 61% in 2019), repetitive hand or arm movements (63%), having to deal with difficult customers, pupils or patients (56%) and lifting or moving people or heavy loads (52%); time pressure is reported by 43% and is the top risk factor in Sweden. Only 3% of establishments report none of the general OSH risk factors considered, but a quarter (25%) report none of the psychosocial risk factors.

**Managing OSH.** 76% of establishments carry out risk assessments regularly, with a positive correlation with size and a range from 95% in Spain to 46% in Luxembourg. Those that do not mostly say the risks are already known (80%) or that there are no major problems (78%). Fulfilling a legal obligation is a major reason for addressing health and safety for 87% of establishments, followed by meeting the expectations of employees or their representatives and avoiding fines from the labour inspectorate. The share reporting a labour inspectorate visit in the previous three years kept falling, to 40% in 2024 (41% in 2019 and 49% in 2014). The complexity of legal obligations remains the most reported major difficulty (40%), and a lack of time or staff has become the second (32%).

**Psychosocial risks and digitalisation.** Among establishments reporting psychosocial risk factors, 21% find them more difficult to manage than other risks, and reluctance to talk openly about these issues is the main difficulty cited by those that do. Among establishments with 20 or more employees, action plans against stress rose to 39% (from 33% in 2019) and procedures for bullying or harassment to 55% (from 45%). 64% of establishments carrying out risk assessments say they have sufficient information on how to include psychosocial risks in them. Personal computers at fixed workplaces (87%) and mobile devices (83%) are widespread, while AI-based systems and systems monitoring workers' performance or behaviour are each reported by 7% on average. A new 2024 question on risk factors linked to digital technologies puts prolonged sitting (54%) and repetitive movements (47%) first, followed by increased work intensity (34%) and information overload (32%); 35% of establishments using such technologies consulted employees about their health and safety impact, up from 24% in 2019.

**Worker participation.** 55% of establishments that used measures to prevent psychosocial risks say employees had a role in designing them, down from 61% in 2019. A health and safety representative is the most common form of employee representation (58% of establishments), while almost a third (31%) have none of the forms of representation considered.
