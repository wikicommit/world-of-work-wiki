*Encuesta de Población Activa (EPA) Módulo sobre conciliación entre la vida laboral y la familiar. Año 2025* is a press release from Spain's National Statistics Institute (Instituto Nacional de Estadística, INE), published on 19 June 2026 and updated on 13 July 2026, presenting the final results of the 2025 module of the Spanish Labour Force Survey (EPA) on reconciling working and family life. Its headline findings are that 33.4% of people aged 18 to 74 looked after a family member in 2025 (20.5% only their own or their partner's children under 15), that 18.2% of people with children under 15 regularly used professional services for their care, and that almost one in four employed people changed something at work to reconcile work and family life.

The module was carried out in collaboration with Eurostat. Its variables were set by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/2312 of 25 November 2022 to provide harmonised information on the subject at European level (see [[Dataset/eu-lfs-ad-hoc-module-on-reconciliation-between-work-and-family-life]]), and the European Commission co-financed its costs.

## Details

**Care responsibilities.** Of the 36,170,300 people aged 18–74 in 2025, 66.6% had no [[DefinedTerm/care-responsibilities]] for children or grandchildren under 15 or for relatives (including a partner) aged 15 or over who were disabled, ill or elderly. 20.5% cared only for children, 8.1% only for dependent relatives, 3.0% only for grandchildren and 1.9% for both children or grandchildren and at least one relative. Women were more likely than men to have a care responsibility (36.2% against 30.6%), and more so in every activity status: 38.1% against 35.6% among the employed, 39.5% against 25.2% among the unemployed and 32.3% against 19.8% among the inactive. The difference was largest for caring for grandchildren (3.8% against 2.1%).

**Professional childcare.** 18.2% of people caring for their own or their partner's children used [[DefinedTerm/professional-childcare-services]] for all of them (19.6% of the employed, 10.6% of the inactive). Among those who did not, the main reason was a preference for organising care alone or with a partner (54.2%, rising to 65.1% among the inactive), followed by help from grandparents, other relatives or friends (18.6%), children being able to look after themselves (13.6%) and the cost of the services (7.7%).

**Hours of care.** Among employed people caring for a partner or relative, 24.0% did so for five to ten hours a week and 18.3% for 40 hours or more. Men outnumbered women in the bands up to ten hours, and women outnumbered men above that, most of all at 40 hours or more (20.5% against 15.7%). The self-employed were more represented than employees in the lowest band (15.3% against 12.6%) and employees in the highest (19.0% against 14.2%).

**Changes at work.** Three in four employed people with care responsibilities (75.2%) made no change at work to reconcile work and family life, and 67.7% found no particular difficulty in their job for doing so. 9.6% adapted their working schedule without changing their hours and 4.4% reduced their working time. Women were more likely than men to have made a change (67.9% of women made none, against 82.1% of men), with the largest gap in reducing working time (8.3% against 0.8%). Among men, the share making a change was highest in services (19.2%) and lowest in construction (12.5%); among women it was highest in construction (40.1%) and lowest in agriculture (22.4%). Adapting the schedule without changing hours was the main change for both public-sector (8.9%) and private-sector (8.4%) employees. The main difficulty cited was [[DefinedTerm/long-working-hours]] in agriculture (11.2%) and construction (12.7%), and unpredictable or difficult working schedules in industry (10.9%) and services (11.2%); long working days were the main difficulty for 9.6% of private-sector and 4.6% of public-sector employees.

**Leave.** Of people aged 18–54, 52.5% had not raised a child under 15. Of those who had, 40.9% used neither birth leave (taken directly after the birth) nor a childcare leave of absence (*excedencia*), 52.4% used only birth leave, 0.8% only a leave of absence and 4.0% both. Men were more likely than women to have used only birth leave (56.4% against 49.0%), while women were more likely to have combined both (6.8% against 0.8%).

**Method.** The module questions were put to people in the EPA subsample at their sixth interview — including, in the first two quarters, an additional subsample collected by the Galician statistics institute in Galicia — about their usual situation at the time of the interview. It covers people aged 18 to 74 living in private households across Spain, with data collected by personal, telephone and web interview; each year the EPA module addresses a different labour-market topic. The data published are final.
