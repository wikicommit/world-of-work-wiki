*Estadísticas a propósito del Día Internacional de la Juventud* ("Statistics on the occasion of International Youth Day") is press release no. 481/24 of Mexico's National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI), issued on 9 August 2024 ahead of International Youth Day on 12 August. It gives an overview of young people aged 15 to 29 based on first-quarter 2024 data from the National Survey of Occupation and Employment (Encuesta Nacional de Ocupación y Empleo, ENOE).

In the first quarter of 2024 Mexico had 31 million young people, 23.8% of a total population of 129.7 million; 51.1% were women. Of these, 16.7 million (54.1%) were economically active: 15.9 million employed and 810,000 (4.8%) unemployed. Among the 14.2 million who were not economically active, 86.1% were not available for work, mainly because they were studying (64 in 100) or doing domestic chores (31 in 100) — the latter reason given by 43.3% of women in this group against 8.2% of men.

## Details

**Who the employed young people are.** Of the 15.9 million employed young people, 60.3% were men and 39.7% women, with an average age of 23.3 years and an average of 11.6 years of schooling; 62 in 100 had upper secondary education and 35 basic education. The largest occupational groups were elementary and support activities (25.3%), professional and technical work (16.5%) and commercial activities (15.6%), with occupations classified under the National Occupation Classification System (SINCO) 2019.

**Where they work.** 62.5% worked in the tertiary sector, 27% in the secondary sector and 9.8% in the primary sector. Of the 13.3 million in non-agricultural work, 47.3% were in micro-businesses and 3.1% in government. By type of unit, 61.1% worked in companies and businesses, 30.5% in households and 7.8% in institutions; of those working in households, 85.7% were in the informal sector.

**Employment status and hours.** 81.3% worked as subordinate paid workers, 10.3% on their own account, 1.6% were employers and 6.7% received no pay. The average working week was 40.7 hours — 42.9 for men and 37.4 for women. Among subordinate young workers, 54.8% worked 35 to 48 hours and 24.5% more than 48 hours (see [[DefinedTerm/long-working-hours]]).

**Pay and benefits.** Average earnings were 33.8 pesos per hour worked (33.5 for men, 34.2 for women). Among subordinate paid workers, 46.8% earned up to one minimum wage a month and 33.6% more than one and up to two (see [[DefinedTerm/minimum-wage]]). 48.1% had access to health services, 59.3% had holidays and a year-end bonus (aguinaldo) or other benefits, and 48.5% had a written contract.
