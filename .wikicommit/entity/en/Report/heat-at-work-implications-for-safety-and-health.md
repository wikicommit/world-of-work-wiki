*Heat at work: Implications for safety and health* is a report published by the International Labour Organization on 25 July 2024, edited by Manal Azzi, Andreas Flouris, Halshka Graczyk, Balint Nafradi and Natasha Scott. It starts from the observation that heat stress is having serious impacts on workers' safety and health as they are exposed to higher daily temperatures and to more frequent and severe heatwaves (see [[DefinedTerm/climate-related-occupational-risks]]). Its centrepiece is an analysis of national legislation addressing heat stress in 21 countries around the world, which identifies common provisions for workplace-level measures.

## Details

The ILO describes heat stress as affecting workers immediately on the job, leading to illnesses such as heat exhaustion and heatstroke, and even death, and in the longer term to serious and debilitating chronic diseases of the cardiovascular and respiratory systems and the kidneys. It calls the number of workers suffering the consequences of excessive heat alarming, and says occupational safety and health protections have struggled to keep up. National legislation does contain provisions to protect workers from excessive heat, but in most cases they are general in nature and do not adequately address the intensifying climate change-related dangers that many workers face daily; some countries are now revising their laws or developing new, specific regulations on heat at work.

The report covers an overview of heat stress at work, the global burden of disease from excessive heat at work, global and national action on heat stress, workplace action on heat stress, and key findings and lessons learned.
