*Multiple job-holders* is a quarterly release of the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) on the number and prevalence of people working more than one job, their demographic and employment characteristics and their working arrangements. The issue for the June 2026 reference period was released on 11 September 2026. It is a multi-source release published alongside the quarterly Labour Account, and brings together headline estimates from the Labour Account (by industry, sex and age group), socio-demographic and employment detail from the Labour Force Survey, and characteristics of multiple job-holders from Jobs in Australia, which is based on personal income tax data in the Linked Employer-Employee Dataset (LEED).

Its key statistics, in seasonally adjusted terms, were that there were 1.0 million multiple job-holders in June 2026 — 6.9% of employed people, against 14.1 million single job-holders — up 6.6% from 984,200 in March 2026; that 7.7% of employed women were multiple job-holders compared with 6.2% of employed men; and that workers aged 20-24 were the most likely to hold more than one job (11.2%).

## Details

**Trend.** Between 1994 and 2019 the [[DefinedTerm/multiple-job-holding]] rate stayed between 5.0% and 6.0%. After a large fall in June 2020 caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, it rose to a record 6.9% in June 2026, and it has been at or above 6.4% since June 2022.

**Who holds multiple jobs.** Over the past five years the rate among women has averaged around 1.2 percentage points above the rate among men. By age, the rate ranged from 11.2% among 20-24-year-olds down to 3.1% among people aged 65 and over.

**Industry and occupation.** By industry of main job, the rate was highest in administrative and support services (9.8%) and agriculture, forestry and fishing (9.7%), and lowest in electricity, gas, water and waste services (3.4%). The ABS notes that some industries lend themselves to holding several jobs, such as seasonal work in agriculture or labour hire workers placed with several businesses. Jobs in Australia data for 2022-23 show that many multiple job-holders have their second job in the same industry as their main job — for example, most women whose main job was in health care and social assistance also had their second job there. By occupation (February 2026), community and personal service workers were the most likely to hold more than one job (10.7%) and machinery operators and drivers the least likely (4.4%).

**Hours worked.** In March 2026 multiple job-holders usually worked 30.0 hours a week on average in their main job and 9.3 hours in their other job or jobs, 39.3 hours in total, while single job-holders usually worked 34.8 hours. Most multiple job-holders worked part-time hours (under 35 a week; see [[DefinedTerm/part-time-employment]]) in both their main and secondary jobs, followed by those combining full-time hours in the main job with part-time hours in a secondary job; very few held two full-time jobs.

**Geography.** In March 2026 the rate was higher outside the capital cities (7.0%) than in them (6.2%), and it was highest in Greater Hobart (8.6%) among capital cities and in the rest of Tasmania (8.2%) among the remaining areas of states and territories.

**Changes to the release.** As part of the modernisation of the Labour Force Survey, the ABS is not updating the items produced directly from that survey — multiple job-holders by occupation, average usual hours worked, state, capital city area and educational qualifications (Table 4) — for the June, September and December 2026 quarters, while it undertakes further quality analysis. The June 2026 issue also revised previously published Labour Account and Labour Force estimates.
