*Les salariés du commerce de détail : des horaires souvent atypiques, un temps partiel fréquent* ("Retail employees: often atypical hours, frequent part-time work") is an Insee Première study, no. 1836, published by Insee on 27 January 2021 and written by Arnaud Rousset. It compares the working hours of employees in retail trade (including commercial crafts such as bakeries and butchers) with those of all employees in France's market services sector, using Labour Force Survey (enquête Emploi) results for 2017 to 2019, presented as averages for 2019, for employees in France excluding Mayotte.

In 2019, 1.8 million employees worked in retail trade, 59% of employees in trade and 16% of those in market services. Because their activities are generally tied to selling products to individuals, they more often work [[DefinedTerm/atypical-working-hours]] — which the study takes to include Saturday, Sunday, evening and night work and hours that vary or alternate from one week to the next — and more often work part time.

## Details

**Atypical hours.** 77% of retail employees work on Saturdays, against 40% across market services; Sunday work and week-to-week variation in hours are also more frequent, but retail employees are less often concerned by evening and night work. To compare occupations the study uses a score that counts how many of the five kinds of atypical hours employees accumulate, from 0 to 5: bakers-pastry cooks score highest (2.33), followed by cashiers (2.02), against 1.50 for all retail employees and 1.13 for market services as a whole. Nine in ten bakers-pastry cooks work on Saturdays and a majority on Sundays and at night. Among retail sectors, constraints are strongest in food retail in stores (84% work on Saturdays, 31% on Sundays), and in large food stores 40% of employees have hours that vary from week to week; hours in pharmacies and opticians come closest to standard hours.

**Age and sex.** The younger the employees, the more atypical their hours, even within the same occupation: employees under 25 — one retail employee in five — work on Saturdays (85%) and Sundays (31%) more often than all retail employees (77% and 23%). The study links this to fewer family constraints and to Sunday work being an opportunity for students. Night work is almost exclusively male (10% of men, 2% of women); for the same occupation and age, a man is three times more likely than a woman to work at night. Women more often have hours that vary from week to week (32% against 27% of men), because they predominate in occupations such as cashiers, reception staff and salespeople.

**Part-time work.** In 2019, 25% of retail employees worked part time, against 19% in market services — 34% of women and 12% of men (see [[DefinedTerm/part-time-employment]]). For the same occupation, age and number of children, a woman in retail is three times more likely than a man to work part time. Part-time work is concentrated in predominantly female occupations — cashiers (60%) but also more qualified pharmacy staff — and is most common in home sales (51%) and market trading (44%). 46% of part-time retail employees say they work part time because they cannot work more in their current job ([[DefinedTerm/involuntary-part-time-work]]); they have hours that vary from week to week more often than other employees (45% against 30%) and are more often on fixed-term contracts (21% against 10%). 14% of part-time retail employees work part time to study, against 8% in market services, mainly because young people make up a larger share of part-time employees in retail.

**Cashiers.** A box on the 164,000 cashiers in retail (9% of the sector's employees, three quarters of them in large food stores, 90% women) finds their conditions particularly atypical: 60% work part time, rising to 75% among those under 25, and 46% of the part-timers say this is constrained; 54% have hours that vary from week to week; 87% work on Saturdays and 32% on Sundays; and 28% work in the evening, though they very rarely work at night.
