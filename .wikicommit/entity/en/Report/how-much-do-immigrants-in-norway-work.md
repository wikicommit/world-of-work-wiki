Hvor mye jobber innvandrere i Norge? ("How much do immigrants in Norway work?") is an article by Statistics Norway (SSB), written by Malin Pettersen and last updated on 30 April 2026. It is an update of a chapter of an earlier SSB publication on immigrants in Norway, and draws on SSB's [[Dataset/register-based-employment-statistics-for-immigrants-norway]]. Its main finding is that a lower share of immigrants than of the rest of the population is in work, and that fewer of those in work work full time.

In November 2025, 545 000 immigrants aged 20–66 had one or more jobs. The employment rate was 68% for immigrants against 80% for the population excluding immigrants (67.5% and 79.5% in the fourth quarter of 2025), and among the employed, 74% of immigrants worked full time against 79% of the rest of the population. Working full time means a combined position percentage of 100% or more across the main job and any secondary jobs.

## Details

**Trend.** The immigrant employment rate rose by over 4 percentage points between 2001 and 2014 (from 63% to just over 67%) and by almost 4 points between 2015 and 2025 (from 64% to just under 68%); because of a break in the series between 2014 and 2015 the two periods are treated separately. It fell to around 65% in the pandemic year 2020, rose to almost 69% in 2021 and just over 69% in 2022, fell to just over 68% in 2023, dipped slightly below that in 2024 and declined slightly again in 2025, though still rounding to 68%. Immigrants from Africa had the largest increase, of almost 17 percentage points between 2015 and 2022 (from just over 44% to just over 61%), reaching over 62% in 2025. The rate among immigrants from Europe outside EU/EFTA and the UK fell from 71% in 2021 to 58% in 2022 and just under 52% in 2023 — reflecting Ukrainian refugees who arrived after Russia's full-scale invasion on 24 February 2022 and who, while taking part in the introduction programme in their first period in Norway, are not available for work — before recovering to 54% in 2024 and 58% in 2025.

**Country background.** Full-time work is most widespread in the groups with the highest employment rates. In the fourth quarter of 2025, immigrants from the Nordic countries had an employment rate of 79% and a full-time share of 82%, those from EU/EFTA countries before 2004 (outside the Nordic countries) 74% and 80%, and those from the new EU countries after 2004 73% and 80%. Immigrants from Asia (64% employed, 71% full time) and Africa (62% and 64%) had the lowest rates on both measures. SSB links the lower full-time shares partly to these groups working more often in industries where part-time work is common, such as health and social services and food service, which also have high shares of temporary positions. Some countries break the pattern: immigrants from Eritrea, Thailand and the Philippines had relatively high employment rates (74%, 77% and 82%) but low full-time shares (63% for Thailand, 64% for Eritrea and the Philippines). Asian countries were spread widely, from Syria (47% employed, 59% full time) to India (74% and 87%).

**Part-time hours.** Among those working part time, position percentages of 80–99% were the most common in every immigrant category and country background. Under 4% of employed immigrants had a combined position percentage below 20% — the same share as in the rest of the population — ranging from around 6% of those from Africa to about 2% of those from the Nordic countries.

**Sex.** Employment and full-time shares are lower for women than for men among both immigrants and the rest of the population. In 2025, 65% of immigrant women were employed and 64% of them worked full time, against 70% and 83% of immigrant men — gaps of 5 and 19 percentage points, larger than the 4 and 15 points in the rest of the population. The gender gap in immigrant employment was smaller than in 2023, mainly because the rate for immigrant men fell by 2 percentage points.

**Length of residence.** Among employed immigrants registered as resident for 7 years or more, 76% worked full time in 2025 — more than among those with shorter residence but still below the 79% of the rest of the population. The difference by length of residence was clearest for immigrants from Africa, Asia and similar regions, and larger for women: 47% of women from that group with under 4 years' residence worked full time, against 64% of those with 7 years or more.

**Occupation.** Sales and service occupations were the largest occupational field among employed immigrants (25%, and 33% of those from Africa, Asia and similar regions, against 17% in the rest of the population), and 12% of employed immigrants were cleaners against 2% in the rest of the population. Part-time work is more widespread in these occupations. In sales and service occupations the full-time share in the main job was 53% for immigrants from EU/EFTA and similar countries, 43% for those from Africa, Asia and similar regions and 42% in the rest of the population; among cleaners, helpers and similar it was 62%, 52% and 62% respectively. In occupations where full-time work is more common, such as academic and university-college occupations, the differences were smaller.
