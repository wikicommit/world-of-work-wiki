This review by Statistics Finland presents results on remote work from the 2023 [[Dataset/quality-of-work-life-survey]]. In 2023, 35 % of wage and salary earners aged 18 to 67 worked remotely and 22 % worked remotely for at least half of their working hours, against only a few per cent when the measurement series began in 1997. Remote work became much more common over the previous 10 to 15 years with the digitalisation of working life and peaked during the coronavirus pandemic, when over 40 % worked remotely in spring 2021; it then declined but remained clearly above its pre-pandemic level. Statistics Finland notes that the survey moved from face-to-face interviews (until 2018) to web inquiries (2021 and 2023), so the latest results are not fully comparable with earlier years.

## Details

**Who works remotely.** In 2023 women worked remotely slightly more often than men (37 % against 33 %), reversing the earlier pattern, although among upper- and lower-level salaried employees considered separately remote work was still more common for men. Remote work was concentrated among upper-level salaried employees (68 %), followed by lower-level salaried employees (31 %). By employer sector, 64 % of central government employees worked remotely, 45 % in the private sector, 28 % in local government and 20 % in wellbeing services counties. Those working remotely for at least half their hours made up 48 % of central government and 41 % of university employees, but 8 % in wellbeing services counties; around 1 % of all wage and salary earners worked remotely practically all the time.

**Home as main workplace.** In 2018, 86 % of remote workers mainly worked at the employer's or customer's premises and 12 % mainly at home; in 2023 the shares were 53 % and 45 %. Across all wage and salary earners, 16 % mainly worked at home in 2023, up from 4 % in 2018 (see [[DefinedTerm/working-from-home]]).

**Influence and presence requirements.** 77 % of remote workers could influence a lot or fairly much when and how much they work remotely — 83 % of men against 71 % of women. About a quarter (24 %) were required to be at the workplace three to four days a week and 27 % one or two days a week, while 36 % — 13 % of all wage and salary earners — could choose almost freely how often they went to the workplace. Men had more freedom than women, a difference Statistics Finland links partly to the gender structure of employer sectors: the strongly female-dominated local government and wellbeing services sectors more often required presence three to four days a week (33 % and 53 %).

**Working time recording.** Among remote workers obliged to record their working hours, one in five did not record some of the hours they worked remotely — most often upper-level salaried employees, supervisors and university staff, and more often the older the worker (see [[DefinedTerm/working-time-recording]]).

**Remote work environment.** 26 % of remote workers said difficult or uncomfortable work positions hampered their remote work, against 5 % at the employer's premises, while only one in ten found the remote environment restless, against 32 % at the workplace. Women more often reported awkward positions (34 % against 16 % of men) and repeated one-sided movements (16 % against 11 %). Only 20 % of remote workers had assessed the conditions of remote work together with their employer.

**Interest in remote work.** 58 % of wage and salary earners felt remote work was not possible, or at least difficult, in their work; most of them would not have been interested even if it were possible. Overall, 40 % were not interested in remote work and 25 % were interested although it was mostly not possible for them. The "not interested and would not be possible" group made up 13 % of upper-level salaried employees, 33 % of lower-level salaried employees and 66 % of workers, though nearly 30 % of workers would in principle have been interested.
