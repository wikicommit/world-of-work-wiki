Organisation du temps de travail ("Organisation of working time") is a fact sheet published by Insee on 26 June 2025 in its Insee Références series, as part of the June 2025 edition of *Emploi, chômage, revenus du travail* (Employment, unemployment and earnings). Drawing on Insee's Labour Force Survey (enquête Emploi) for 2014 and 2024, it describes how often people in France (excluding Mayotte) work at [[DefinedTerm/atypical-working-hours]] and how many employees telework.

Both topics are measured over the four weeks before the survey interview. Atypical hours cover work on a Saturday, a Sunday, in the evening (8 p.m. to midnight) or at night (midnight to 5 a.m.) at least once in that period. Telework is defined as carrying out tasks that could have been done at the usual place of work outside the employer's premises, during usual working hours, using information and communication technologies.

## Details

In 2024, 46% of employees worked on an atypical day or at an atypical time at least once over the four-week period, compared with 49% in 2014. Saturday work was the most common form (36% of employees, down from 40% in 2014), followed by evening work (27%), Sunday work (22%) and night work (11%), the last three unchanged from 2014. The self-employed reported atypical hours more often than employees, especially evening work (45%) and weekend work (67% on Saturdays, 40% on Sundays).

The pattern differs by occupational category. Managers and professionals (*cadres*), who have longer working days, work more often in the evening (37%); clerical and service employees (*employés*) more often on Saturdays (48%) and Sundays (30%); and manual workers (*ouvriers*) more often at night (15%) and on alternating shift patterns (13%, against 6% of all employees). Part-time employees work evenings (22% against 28%) and nights (6% against 11%) less often than full-time employees, but work weekends as often; those working weekends more often say they work part time in order to study or train.

In 2024, 18% of employees teleworked at least one day a week over the four-week period, and a further 11% did not telework although they considered their job compatible with it. Nearly half of managers and professionals (48%) teleworked at least one day a week, with no difference between those who supervise others and those who do not, against 18% of intermediate occupations and 7% of clerical and service employees; manual workers did not telework. By sector, the share was highest in information and communication (70%) and financial services (48%), and low in retail (13%), where jobs are often judged incompatible with telework, and in health and social work (4%), where 9 in 10 employees said telework was incompatible with their job.
