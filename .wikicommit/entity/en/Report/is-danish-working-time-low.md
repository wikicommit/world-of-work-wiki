*Er den danske arbejdstid lav?* ("Is Danish working time low?") is an analysis published by Statistics Denmark (Danmarks Statistik) on 29 November 2017 as No. 2017:17. Danish working time is often presented as low by European standards, and average actual weekly working time per employed person in Denmark is indeed low compared with other EU countries. Because many men and women in Denmark take part in the labour market, the analysis asks whether that picture holds once employment is taken into account, measuring Danes' weekly working time in two ways — per employed person and per person in the population — and looking at which groups in Denmark work more or less than the average for other European countries.

## Details

The analysis's main conclusions are:

- Denmark's employment rate is relatively high, and Danish women have the third-highest employment rate in the EU.
- Measured per person in the population, average weekly working time in Denmark lies mid-field and slightly above the EU average, though below Sweden, Germany and the United Kingdom.
- The relatively low working time per employed person is partly because many students also work. Leaving students out brings Danish working time per employed person closer to, but still about 1½ hours below, the average for the European countries in the analysis.
- For people aged 60-74, working time per employed person is around the average of those countries, while working time per person in the population is well above it, because employment among 60-74-year-olds is relatively high in Denmark.
- Working time for people with a high level of education is generally below the average of those countries, however it is measured; for people with low and medium education it is slightly above average when measured against their numbers in the population.
