*近年我國女性勞動參與狀況* ("Women's labour force participation in recent years") is a Labour Statistics Bulletin (*勞動統計通報*) issued by the Department of Statistics of Taiwan's Ministry of Labor on 11 June 2019. It reviews women's participation in the labour market over the two decades from 1998 to 2018, drawing on the Directorate-General of Budget, Accounting and Statistics' manpower, manpower utilization, earnings and women's marriage, fertility and employment surveys and on Bureau of Labor Insurance data. The bulletin attributes the rise in women's participation to higher educational attainment, more jobs in services and the government's measures to create friendly workplaces and protect maternity, particularly since the Gender Equality in Employment Act (*性別工作平等法*, originally *兩性工作平等法*) came into force in 2002.

## Details

**Participation.** Women's labour force grew from 3.767 million in 1998 to 5.272 million in 2018, an increase of 40.0%, and their participation rate rose from 45.6% to 51.1%, having first passed 50% in 2012; annual gains, mostly 0.4 to 0.8 point a year up to 2007, slowed to 0.1 to 0.3 point from 2008. Women's participation by age forms an inverted V: in 2018 it peaked at 91.8% at ages 25-29, began to fall at the ages of marriage and childbirth (30-34), reached 74.0% at 45-49 and dropped by 13.0 points to 61.0% at 50-54. Over the two decades participation rose in every age group from 25 to 54, by 15 to 24 points, but fell among 15-24-year-olds as schooling lengthened. Compared with 2018 figures for the United States, Japan and Korea, Taiwanese women aged 25-34 had the highest participation (over 85%) and those aged 35-49 a similar level to the United States and Japan (74% or more), but from age 50 Taiwan's rates were lower than all three. Japan and Korea show a second peak in women's forties as women return to work after childbirth, whereas in the United States exit on marriage and childbirth is not marked.

**Employment.** Women's employment grew by 38.3% between 1998 and 2018 to 5.089 million, against 13.1% for men. 72.1% of employed women worked in services in 2018, up from 63.2% in 1998. The largest occupational group among women was service and sales workers (24.1%), followed by technicians and associate professionals (20.3%) and clerical support workers (19.8%); the share of professionals rose by 6.6 points over the two decades, more than among men (5.7 points). Women held 27.9% of legislator, senior official and manager posts in 2018, up 9.9 points in ten years, and 51.2% of professional and technical posts. In May 2018, 7.5% of employed women were in part-time, temporary or agency work, against 6.8% of men.

**Pay and hours.** In 2018 women employees in industry and services earned an average of NT$283 an hour against NT$332 for men, a [[DefinedTerm/gender-pay-gap]] of 14.6%, down from 18.6% in 2008. The bulletin translates the gap into Taiwan's [[DefinedTerm/equal-pay-day]]: women would have to work 54 more days to earn as much as men, so Equal Pay Day 2019 fell on 23 February, 15 days earlier than a decade before. Monthly hours of women employees were longest in other services (181.1 hours), manufacturing (174.0) and support services (169.2) and shortest in education (137.6).

**Marriage, childbirth and employment.** In October 2016, 57.2% of married women aged 15-64 were in work. Of these, 53.1% had worked continuously since before marriage, while 17.6% had at some point left work on marriage, 13.0% on childbirth or pregnancy and 8.1% for other reasons, and 11.4% had started work only after marriage and worked ever since. Of married women not in work, 29.1% had left on marriage and never worked again, 18.3% had left on childbirth or pregnancy and never returned, and 17.9% had not worked at any time, before or since marriage; the share of married women aged 15-64 not in work fell by 8.6 points between 1993 and 2016.

**Unpaid parental leave.** Between 66,000 and 72,000 women a year applied for parental leave without pay (*育嬰留職停薪*) over the four years to 2018, which the bulletin sees as helping women care for infants while keeping their jobs. Of the women whose leave ended in 2017, 92.5% had rejoined labour insurance — returned to work — by June 2018 (76.9% with their original employer), up from 91.4% for leave ending in 2014, while 7.5% had not.

**Women outside the labour force.** In May 2018, 3.336 million women aged 15-64 were outside the labour force. Of the 3.228 million with no wish to work, 32.9% gave housework as the reason, 31.5% study and 10.3% that the family was well enough off; among women aged 30-39, the need to care for children under 12 was the leading reason (55% to 65%), and from age 40 it was housework. A further 108,000 (3.2%) wanted to work, mostly full time and mostly as clerical support or service and sales workers.
