"Older People in Japan Seen Through Statistics — on the Occasion of Respect for the Aged Day" is number 149 in the Statistics Topics series of the Statistics Bureau of Japan's Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications, dated 20 September 2026. Issued ahead of Respect for the Aged Day (21 September), it brings together official statistics on the population aged 65 and over and on their employment. The population figures draw on Japan's population estimates and the UN World Population Prospects, and the employment figures on Japan's Labour Force Survey and OECD.Stat.

The report finds that the population aged 65 and over numbers 36.24 million, 20,000 more than a year earlier, and that its share of the total population, 29.6%, is the highest on record; Japan's share of people aged 65 and over is the highest among the 39 countries compared.

## Details

On employment, the number of employed people aged 65 and over has risen for 22 consecutive years to a record 9.43 million, and their share of all employed people has reached a record 13.8%. Employment rates in each age group over 65 are all at record highs, and Japan's employment rate for people aged 65 and over is high among the major countries. Among employees aged 65 and over, excluding executives, 76.1% are non-regular staff. By main industry, employed people aged 65 and over are most numerous in wholesale and retail trade and in medical care and welfare, among others.
