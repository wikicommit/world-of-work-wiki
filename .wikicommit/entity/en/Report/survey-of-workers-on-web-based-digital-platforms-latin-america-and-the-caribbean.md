*Encuesta sobre trabajadores en plataformas digitales basadas en la web: Nuevos datos para la región de América Latina y el Caribe* ("Survey of workers on web-based digital platforms: new data for Latin America and the Caribbean") is a report published by the International Labour Organization's Regional Office for Latin America and the Caribbean, listed as published on 10 April 2025 and presented in an ILO press release from Lima on 21 April 2025. It sets out the results of a regional survey of more than a thousand workers on web-based digital platforms (see [[DefinedTerm/digital-labour-platform]]) in 21 countries of the region, and analyses their sociodemographic characteristics, working conditions, income and motivations. The ILO describes the survey as one of the most extensive carried out in the region on the subject, intended to provide an empirical basis for better-informed discussion of the digital economy in Latin America and the Caribbean.

The study looks exclusively at workers who carry out tasks through web-based digital platforms, which connect workers and clients remotely and without a specific geographic location. The tasks range from programming and graphic design to repetitive microtasks such as data labelling or training artificial intelligence systems. Unlike transport or delivery platforms, these platforms connect workers and clients from all over the world through the internet.

## Details

According to the ILO's press release, the report finds that:

- 93 per cent of respondents live in urban areas, underlining the predominantly urban character of this kind of work.
- The workforce is young and qualified: the median age is 33 and more than half have university education.
- 8 per cent of respondents identify as migrants, and almost half of them come from Venezuela.
- More than half (53 per cent) work for clients outside their own country; 90 per cent of those who know where their clients are located say they are outside Latin America and the Caribbean, mainly in the United States and Canada.
- Median hourly income is USD 2.57 while the average reaches USD 5.48, reflecting highly heterogeneous earnings.
- 52 per cent said platform work is not their main source of income.

Many workers value the flexibility of this kind of work and its ability to supplement their income, but the report highlights persistent challenges in social protection: close to 40 per cent of the workers surveyed are excluded from health and social security coverage.

The ILO's Regional Director for Latin America and the Caribbean, Ana Virginia Moreira Gomes, described the web-based platform economy as a global phenomenon with local implications, and the report as a fundamental tool for strengthening social dialogue and enriching debates on how to promote decent work in a rapidly changing digital environment. The ILO presented the publication as particularly relevant to the discussion of platform work at the International Labour Conference in June 2025, where its tripartite constituents — governments, employers and workers — were to address the regulatory challenges of this form of work.
