Durée et conditions de travail ("Working time and working conditions") is a fact sheet published by Insee on 18 November 2025 in its Insee Références series, as part of the November 2025 edition of *France, portrait social*. It brings together figures on how long and when people in employment in France (excluding Mayotte) work, drawn from Insee's 2024 Labour Force Survey (enquête Emploi) with calculations by Dares, and on accidents at work and occupational diseases recognised by France's social insurance schemes.

The sheet carries a warning that L’Assurance Maladie – Risques professionnels observed a break in its statistical series on accidents at work and occupational diseases from 2022, so the sheet's figures on them are not comparable with those in earlier editions.

## Details

**Working time.** In 2024, people aged 15 or over in employment reported average [[DefinedTerm/usual-hours-of-work]] of 36.9 hours a week: 39.9 hours for those working full time and 22.7 hours for those working part time. Occupational categories made up mainly of the self-employed work much longer — farmers 52.6 hours on average, craftspeople, shopkeepers and business owners 42.7 hours — while the average for all other categories was 36.2 hours. Among employees other than apprentices, 17.4% worked part time: 26.7% of women and 7.9% of men.

**When people work.** Over four consecutive weeks, 41% of people in employment worked at least once on a Saturday (11.8 million people), 24% on a Sunday (7.0 million), 29% in the evening (8.5 million) and 11% at night (3.1 million) — the categories that make up [[DefinedTerm/atypical-working-hours]]. Clerical and service employees work more often than other employees on Saturdays and Sundays, manual workers at night and on alternating shifts, and managers and professionals in the evening and at home. These shares changed little in 2024 and remain below their levels before the health crisis. The share of people in employment who worked at home when it was not their usual place of work has been stable since 2022, after rising in 2020 and 2021; it was 30% overall and 68% among managers and professionals in 2024.

**Accidents at work.** In 2022, the general scheme, the agricultural scheme and the pension fund for local government employees (CNRACL) recognised 679,200 accidents at work ([[DefinedTerm/accident-at-work]]) involving time off work. Men accounted for 61% of victims, partly because they are over-represented in some industrial sub-sectors and in construction, as well as in temporary agency work and in tertiary activities such as transport and warehousing, where accidents are more frequent. Employees in social work, a largely female sector, are also more exposed; together with health, this sector accounts for 40% of women's accidents at work. 61% of victims were under 45 when the accident happened. In 2022, 41,000 accidents at work led to a permanent partial incapacity for work, and 789 were fatal.

**Occupational diseases.** The same schemes recognised 55,100 new cases of [[DefinedTerm/occupational-disease]] in 2022, and 28,800 permanent partial incapacities linked to an occupational disease. Occupational diseases arise later in life than accidents at work: three quarters of recognitions occur at age 45 or over. In the general scheme, 87% of recognised occupational diseases are musculoskeletal disorders ([[DefinedTerm/work-related-musculoskeletal-disorders]]), which affect women more, and 4% are asbestos-related conditions, which mostly concern men.
