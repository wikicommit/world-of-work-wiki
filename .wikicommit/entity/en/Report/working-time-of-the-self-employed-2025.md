Durée de travail des indépendants ("Working time of the self-employed") is a thematic sheet in the 2025 edition of *Emploi et revenus des indépendants*, a volume in Insee's Insee Références collection published on 21 May 2025. Using the French Labour Force Survey (enquête Emploi), with calculations by Dares, it measures how much the self-employed in France work in their main job and compares them with employees.

The sheet's central finding is that in 2023 the self-employed worked on average 1,971 hours a year in their main job — 422 hours, or 27%, more than employees (1,549 hours). More than two-thirds of the gap comes from the self-employed working more days in the year (243 against 206), and the rest from longer working days (8.1 hours against 7.5). Their usual weekly working time was 42.5 hours, against 36.2 hours for employees and 38.9 hours for full-time employees. It also shows that the self-employed's annual working time has fallen substantially since 2008, partly because of the rise of micro-entrepreneurs ([[DefinedTerm/micro-entrepreneur]]), and that the 2020 health crisis cut it more sharply than employees' working time.

## Details

The sheet measures working time as annual actual hours ([[DefinedTerm/actual-hours-of-work]]) — hours worked including paid or unpaid overtime and excluding time not worked because of annual leave, public holidays, sickness, accidents, maternity or paternity leave, short-time working, strikes and the like — broken down into the number of days worked and hours per day, alongside usual weekly hours ([[DefinedTerm/usual-hours-of-work]]) for a normal week. It covers people in employment aged 15 or over living in ordinary dwellings in France excluding Mayotte. The self-employed are defined as workers with no legal subordination to a client and no employment contract, who therefore do not benefit from labour-law protection ([[DefinedTerm/self-employment]]); they comprise non-salaried workers and salaried company directors.

Working time varies widely by sector. The longest annual hours were in food trades (2,865 hours, with 9.6-hour days), agriculture (2,667 hours over 315 days) and accommodation and catering (2,476 hours), where the self-employed work more days in the year than elsewhere; doctors and dentists combine long days (9.3 hours) with fewer days worked (205). By status, "classic" self-employed people worked 2,151 hours a year and micro-entrepreneurs 1,421.

Between 2008 and 2023 the average annual working time of the self-employed fell by 436 hours (-18%). The sheet attributes about half of this fall to the changing composition of self-employment, above all the arrival in 2009 of the auto-entrepreneur regime, renamed micro-entrepreneur in 2014, whose share has risen strongly; the declining weight of agriculture in classic self-employment (14% in 2023 against 17% in 2008) contributed more modestly. The series has a break in 2021, when a new version of the Labour Force Survey was introduced.

In 2020, during the health crisis, the self-employed's annual working time fell by 224 hours (-10.7%) from 2019, against 92 hours (-6.0%) for employees, with the steepest falls among the self-employed in transport and storage and in accommodation and catering. By 2023 employees' working time was slightly above its pre-crisis level (+0.6% on 2019), while that of the self-employed remained 6.3% below it, partly because the share of micro-entrepreneurs kept rising.
