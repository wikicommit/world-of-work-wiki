*Involuntary part-time work, 1997 to 2022* is an article in Statistics Canada's *Quality of Employment in Canada* series (catalogue no. 14-28-0001-X), released on 13 June 2023. It presents the [[DefinedTerm/involuntary-part-time-work]] indicator — part-time work done because of business conditions or because the person could not find work of 30 or more hours a week — which the article treats as an indicator of underemployment that may be associated with financial stress. Following the UNECE quality of employment framework, it reports both the involuntary part-time rate (involuntary part-time workers as a share of all workers) and the share of part-time workers who work part-time involuntarily.

All figures are annual averages from the [[Dataset/canadian-labour-force-survey]] for workers aged 15 and older. The article found 556,000 involuntary part-time workers on average in 2022, 2.8% of all workers. Employed women were more likely than men to be involuntary part-time workers (3.6% versus 2.2%), but involuntary work made up a larger share of men's part-time employment (17.4%) than of women's (14.5%).

## Details

**Trends.** The article describes the rate as varying with economic activity and labour market tightness: when unemployment is low and vacancies are high, workers more easily find jobs with their preferred hours. The rate was 5.9% in 1997, fell to 4.1% in 2007, rose to 5.5% in 2010 after the 2008/2009 recession, and fell from 5.4% in 2014 to 3.7% in 2019; in Alberta it rose 1.6 percentage points to 5.1% from 2015 to 2016 as oil prices declined. It rose to 4.2% in 2020 during the COVID-19 pandemic and then fell, in the tight labour market of 2021 and 2022, to 2.8% — the lowest since the series began in 1997. As a share of part-time employment it fluctuated more, rising from 19.5% in 2019 to 23.4% in 2020 before falling to a record low of 15.5% in 2022. Between 1997 and 2022 the rate fell in all major demographic groups, notably among youth aged 15 to 24 (11.1% to 5.2%) and core-aged workers aged 25 to 54 (5.2% to 2.3%).

**Who works part-time involuntarily (2022).** The rate was highest among young women (6.1%), young men (4.3%) and women aged 55 and older (3.5%), partly because more workers in these groups work part-time. Core-aged men were least likely to be involuntary part-time workers (1.7%), yet among the relatively few who worked part-time, 32.2% did so involuntarily — the highest proportion of any major group — followed by core-aged women (19.0%); the article notes that core-aged workers have typically finished their education, are less likely to be moving into retirement and may have more incentive to work more hours. Despite potentially having more control over their hours, self-employed workers had more than twice the rate of employees (5.1% versus 2.5%). Among provinces, Newfoundland and Labrador and Alberta had among the highest rates (3.6%) and Quebec the lowest (1.7%).

**Measurement.** In the Labour Force Survey, respondents are asked whether they want to work more or less than 30 hours a week at a single job or business, and the main reason for working part-time is then collected. Involuntary part-time work includes people whether or not they looked for full-time work in the previous month. Because of a change in concepts and definitions introduced in January 1997, the series begins in that year.
