*Canadian Survey on Working Conditions, 2024-2025* is a release in Statistics Canada's *The Daily*, published on 16 January 2026, presenting the first results from the [[Dataset/canadian-survey-on-working-conditions]], a new survey that aims to better understand the working conditions of Canadians. Statistics Canada presents it as complementing other sources such as the [[Dataset/canadian-labour-force-survey]] and its supplements, to give a fuller picture of the quality of employment in Canada.

The release's headline findings are that in 2024-2025 more than half of workers (54.3%) were frequently exposed to ergonomic risk factors, such as repetitive hand or arm movements, and that 17.0% did unpaid work in their free time — at times they were not originally scheduled to work — several times a month.

## Details

**Physical risks.** Ergonomic risks were the most common physical risk; frequent exposure (half of the time or more over the previous 12 months) was highest in natural resources, agriculture and related production (74.9%), trades, transport and equipment operation (74.5%) and manufacturing and utilities (73.2%). Ambient risks such as temperature extremes and loud noise were second (28.1% of workers), and 17.8% were frequently exposed to biological and chemical risks, rising to 46.6% in health occupations. Workers with a bachelor's degree or higher were the least likely to be frequently exposed to each type of physical risk. Men were more often exposed than women to ambient and to biological and chemical risks, while men and women were about equally likely to be frequently exposed to ergonomic risks.

**Emotional demands.** 16.1% of workers frequently had to deal with angry or dissatisfied clients, patients or students, rising to 35.2% in health occupations; women (20.1%) were more often exposed than men (12.5%), and youth aged 15 to 24 (22.1%) more than older workers.

**Working time and work-life balance.** Two-thirds (66.4%) of workers in natural resources, agriculture and related production occupations usually worked over 10 hours a day at least once a month, and a third (33.5%) of them had had less than 11 hours of rest between two workdays at least once in the previous month — the highest of any occupation. Men were more likely than women to work at night (22.3% against 14.5%) or to have workdays over 10 hours (43.3% against 25.1%) at least once a month. Half (50.2%) of workers in management frequently worked to tight deadlines, and 34.6% of managers and 29.5% of workers in jobs usually requiring a bachelor's degree or higher did unpaid work in their free time several times a month or more to meet work demands, against 6.0% in jobs usually requiring a high school diploma or less.

**Schedule flexibility.** Most employees (58.9%) had their schedule set by their employer and 8.9% could choose between fixed schedules; 26.2% could adapt their hours within certain limits and 6.3% determined them entirely themselves ([[DefinedTerm/flexible-working-hours]]). Employees in professional, scientific and technical services were the most likely to be able to adapt or set their hours (58.3%), those in business, building and other support services the least likely (24.2%). Core-aged parents living with a child under 6 (33.7%) were about as likely to have such flexibility as core-aged employees without children (32.7%).

**Support and meaningful work.** Most employees felt their colleagues (79.5%) and their manager (73.6%) were usually supportive. Frequent support from a manager was lowest in health care and social assistance (66.2%), and frequent support from colleagues was lower among several racialized groups, including Black (69.4%), Southeast Asian (70.7%) and Arab (72.3%) employees, than among non-racialized, non-Indigenous employees (81.6%). Over four in five workers (81.9%) felt they were doing useful work most of the time or always, with the highest shares in agriculture (92.3%) and construction (88.5%).

Statistics Canada says it will release further reports exploring specific dimensions of quality of employment in more detail. The release reports results that are significant at the 95% confidence level.
