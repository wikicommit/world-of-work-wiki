*Is hybrid working here to stay?* is an article by the UK Office for National Statistics (ONS), published on 23 May 2022, after government guidance to work from home when possible had been lifted. It asks what working patterns will look like once COVID-19 restrictions end, drawing on the Opinions and Lifestyle Survey of individuals and the Business Insights and Conditions Survey of businesses.

Its headline finding is that most people who took up homeworking because of the pandemic planned to combine working from home with working at their usual place of work — [[DefinedTerm/hybrid-work]]. In February 2022, 84% of workers who had had to work from home said they planned to do so. Between early February and May 2022 the proportion of workers hybrid working rose from 13% to 24%, while the proportion working exclusively from home fell from 22% to 14%.

## Details

**Plans.** The share of workers planning to hybrid work at all changed little between April 2021 and February 2022, but plans shifted towards spending most working hours at home: working mostly from home and sometimes at the usual place of work was the most common planned pattern in February 2022 (42%, up from 30% in April 2021). The share planning to return to their place of work permanently fell from 11% to 8%.

**Working patterns in spring 2022.** Between 27 April and 8 May 2022, 38% of working adults reported having worked from home at some point in the past seven days; 24% both worked from home and travelled to work, 14% worked from home exclusively, and travelling to work exclusively remained the most common pattern (46%). In March 2022 the most common reason given for working from home was that it was part of the worker's normal routine (62%).

**Who hybrid works.** Hybrid working increased with income: 38% of workers earning £40,000 or more hybrid worked, the only income group for which it was the most common pattern, compared with 8% of those earning up to £15,000, of whom 62% travelled to work exclusively. Workers aged 30 to 49 were the most likely to hybrid work (29%).

**Benefits and drawbacks.** In February 2022, 78% of those who worked from home in some capacity said it gave them an improved [[DefinedTerm/work-life-balance]]; about half said work was quicker to complete (52%) or that they had fewer distractions (53%), and 47% reported improved well-being. Younger workers aged 16 to 29 were less likely to report fewer distractions (32%). The most common disadvantage was difficulty working with others (48%); 26% reported more distractions, 31% no disadvantages, and 8% no advantages.

**Businesses.** The proportion of businesses using or intending to use homeworking as a permanent business model rose from 16% in autumn 2020 to 23% in early April 2022, varying widely by industry: 54% in information and communication, against 3% in accommodation and food services and 5% in construction. The most common reasons given were improved staff well-being (60%), reduced overheads (43%) and increased productivity (41%).
