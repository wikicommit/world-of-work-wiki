Durée et conditions de travail ("Working time and working conditions") is a fact sheet published by Insee on 21 November 2024 in its Insee Références series, as part of the 2024 edition of *France, portrait social*, a volume that also contains three analyses of perceived and experienced discrimination, two dossiers on household living standards in 2023 and some forty summary fact sheets. The sheet draws on Insee's 2023 Labour Force Survey (enquête Emploi) with calculations by Dares for working time, and on data from the Cnam and the MSA (accidents at work) and Insee's Tous salariés database for accidents at work and occupational diseases.

## Details

**Working time.** In 2023, people aged 15 or over in employment in France excluding Mayotte reported average [[DefinedTerm/usual-hours-of-work]] of 37.0 hours a week: 40.0 hours for those working full time and 22.7 hours for those working part time. The figure was 36.2 hours for all employees, and much higher for the self-employed, notably farmers. 17.2% of employees worked part time: 26.3% of women and 7.8% of men (see [[DefinedTerm/part-time-employment]]).

**When people work.** Over four consecutive weeks, two people in employment in five worked at least once on a Saturday (11.6 million people), nearly one in four on a Sunday (6.6 million), three in ten in the evening (8.5 million) and one in ten at night (3.2 million) — the categories that make up [[DefinedTerm/atypical-working-hours]]. Compared with other employees, clerical and service employees work more often on Saturdays and Sundays, manual workers at night and on alternating shifts, and managers and professionals in the evening and at home. The shares working atypical hours were stable in 2023 and had still not returned to their levels before the health crisis. The share of people in employment working at home was stable from 2022, after rising in 2020 and 2021; in 2023 it was 30% overall and 69% among managers and professionals.

**Accidents at work.** In 2019, manual workers in the general scheme were the most exposed to [[DefinedTerm/accident-at-work]] involving time off work, with 37 accidents per million paid hours, against 21 for all employees in the general or agricultural scheme. Men had 23 accidents per million hours worked and women 18, a gap that comes mainly from the over-exposure of manual workers, most of whom are men. Accidents were more frequent among female manual workers, but male manual workers had the most serious ones, and male clerical and service employees were over-represented in the riskiest activities, such as transport and mail delivery, retail sale of hardware, paint and glass, and fast food. Accident frequency falls with age, as employees under 20 more often work in high-risk sectors such as construction, trade, accommodation and food services, and temporary agency work.

**Occupational diseases.** The general and agricultural social security schemes recognised 52,500 cases of [[DefinedTerm/occupational-disease]] in 2019, 26,800 of which led to an incapacity. Manual workers accounted for 69% of the occupational diseases recognised by the general scheme and for most of the serious ones, including 92% of the recognised cases of deafness and of asbestos-related conditions in 2019. Asbestos-related conditions mostly affect men, while work-related musculoskeletal disorders ([[DefinedTerm/work-related-musculoskeletal-disorders]]) mostly affect women (53%).
