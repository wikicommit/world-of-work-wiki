*Smart working: da necessità a nuovo stile di vita - Anno 2023* ("Smart working: from necessity to a new lifestyle – Year 2023") is a press release published by Istat on 25 February 2026, with reference period 2023. Drawing mainly on the permanent population census, it describes how remote work in Italy grew with the COVID-19 pandemic and then stabilised, and how it varies across the country. Istat frames remote work as having become a structural part of society, redefining the relationship between work and private life, between physical and digital spaces and between periods of activity and rest, while reducing commuting time and costs ([[DefinedTerm/working-from-home]]).

## Details

**Prevalence and trend.** According to the 2023 permanent census, just under 3.4 million employed people, 13.8% of the total, had worked remotely in some form in the four weeks before the census reference date of 1 October 2023: about 1,436,000 (5.9%) had worked from home on at least half of their working days and the remaining 1,933,000 (7.9%) to a more limited extent. Flexible working peaked during and just after the pandemic: in 2021, 3,577,984 people, 15.1% of the 23,632,617 employed, had worked from home for at least a few days. Before the pandemic, in 2018 and 2019, the share of smart workers and teleworkers was only 4.8% according to Istat's Labour Force Survey. In both the 2022 and 2023 censuses, remote workers made up 13.8% of the employed, a stabilisation of the phenomenon.

**International comparison.** Istat notes that despite this growth Italy's share of remote workers remains below that of most other European countries. In Eurostat's figures for people who worked from home on at least half of their working days in 2023 ("usually working from home"), Italy's 5.9% was well below the EU average of 9.1%; Finland (22.2%) and Ireland (21.8%) led, followed by Sweden (15.3%) and Belgium (14.6%), and Germany and France were also above the EU average, at more than 10%.

**Regional differences.** Remote work was more widespread in the metropolitan cities and the regions of the Centre-North, with more advanced tertiary sectors and digital infrastructure: among municipalities with more than 150,000 residents, Milan, Rome, Bologna and Turin had the highest shares of remote workers. In 2023 the share of employed people who had worked remotely for at least one day in the four weeks before the census reference date was 17.1% in the North-East, one point more than in the Centre, 11.9% in the North-West, 10.2% in the South and 9.7% in the two main islands, against a national average of 13.8%. Lazio led the regions (21.5%), followed by Lombardy (18.6%), Piedmont (14.5%) and Liguria (14%). In every region of the South the share was below 10%, except Campania (11.1%), Abruzzo (10.3%) and Sardinia (10.2%), and Aosta Valley, like Basilicata, stopped at 8.8%.
