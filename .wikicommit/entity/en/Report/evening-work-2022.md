*Evening work, 2022* is an article in Statistics Canada's *Quality of Employment in Canada* series (catalogue no. 14-28-0001-X), released on 13 June 2023. It presents the evening work indicator: the share of employed people whose usual schedule in their main job is a regular evening shift or regular evening hours (see [[DefinedTerm/evening-work]]). Its data come mostly from a supplement to the [[Dataset/canadian-labour-force-survey]] collected in April 2022 among workers aged 15 to 69, with comparison points from the 2016 General Social Survey (Canadians at work and home) and the 2020 Survey on Quality of Employment.

The article found that about 1 in 20 workers (4.8%, or 918,000 people) had regular evening hours in April 2022. Evening work was most common in accommodation and food services, where 1 in 5 workers (21.8%) usually worked evenings, and was relatively high among students aged 15 to 24.

## Details

**Trends.** Rates from other surveys and seasons were 6.5% in March 2020, just before the pandemic, and 5.4% in fall 2016. Statistics Canada attributes the lower 2022 rate partly to employment in accommodation and food services, which in April 2022 was still 13.9% (170,000 jobs) below its February 2020 level.

**Who works evenings.** Youth aged 15 to 24 had a rate of 14.2%, compared with 3.3% for ages 25 to 54 and 3.7% for ages 55 to 69, and young students (19.7%) far exceeded young non-students (8.3%). Men and women had similar rates (4.8% and 4.9%). Part-time workers, defined as those usually working less than 30 hours a week, worked evenings more than three times as often as full-time workers (11.3% against 3.3%), which the article ties to shifts in public-facing industries being organized around peaks in demand. Filipino (11.8%) and Korean (10.0%) Canadians had the highest rates among racialized groups, and Arab (4.0%) and Chinese (4.4%) Canadians lower ones.

**Job conditions.** Evening workers had less flexibility than workers with regular daytime hours: 23.4% could vary their start and end times (against 37.2%) and 54.8% found it easy or very easy to take an hour or two off for personal reasons (against 73.2%). Most (94.1%) worked only at locations other than home, compared with 72.2% of daytime workers. The article draws the same contrast with a companion indicator on workers who usually work at night ([[DefinedTerm/night-work]]).

**Method.** The definition follows EU Labour Force Survey standards, under which evening work falls after typical daytime hours and before usual sleeping hours. Data refer to the main job, are not seasonally adjusted, and the analysis reports differences that are statistically significant at the 95% confidence level; industries are coded to the North American Industry Classification System (NAICS) 2017.
