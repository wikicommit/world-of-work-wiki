*Vrouwen werken meteen na afstuderen al vaker in deeltijd dan mannen* ("Women already work part-time more often than men right after graduating") is a news release published by Statistics Netherlands (CBS) on 12 April 2023. It reports on the first nine years in the labour market of men and women who left education with a diploma — from mbo (secondary vocational education), hbo (higher professional education) or wo (university) — in the period 2007–2009, with [[DefinedTerm/part-time-employment]] defined as working fewer than 35 hours a week.

CBS finds that men and women had paid work about equally often one year after graduating, but that men more often began their careers in a full-time job: among employees, 30% of women worked part-time one year after graduating, twice the share of men (14%). The gap then widened, because men moved towards fewer part-time jobs and women towards more. Women who started out full time also switched to part-time more often than men, especially when they lived with a partner, and the difference grew further after the birth of a first child.

## Details

**Nine years after graduating.** Fewer than 10% of male employees worked part-time nine years after leaving education. Among women the share had risen to 40% of those with a university diploma and to 67% of those with an mbo diploma. Part of the difference arises because women more often follow courses leading to sectors where part-time jobs are common, such as education and health care, but even after taking this into account women less often had a full-time job one year after finishing their education.

**From full-time to part-time.** In the first nine years after graduating, women who started in full-time work moved to a part-time job more often than men at every education level, whether they lived with their parents, alone or with a partner. Cohabiting women swapped a full-time for a part-time job more often than women living alone or with their parents, and cohabiting women with children did so most often. Among men without children the pattern was reversed: cohabiting men moved from full-time to part-time less often than men without a partner. The gap between the sexes was largest among mbo graduates: of full-time working women with an mbo diploma who lived with a partner without children, just over 7% a year switched to part-time, against just over 2% of men with a comparable diploma in a comparable sector (annual averages corrected for, among other things, educational background and sector).

**Around the birth of a first child.** For women, the move to part-time is linked to the birth of the first child. In the four years before the birth slightly more women each year moved from full-time to part-time, and in the year they became mothers the likelihood rose sharply: 58% of mbo-educated women who still had a full-time job when their first child was born moved to a part-time job in the first year after the birth. Women with an hbo and especially a university diploma made the transition less often, both before and after the birth. Among men the likelihood of moving from full-time to part-time also rose, at all education levels, in the year they became fathers and the years just after, but far less than among women; the great majority of men kept working full time.

**Hours and the division of work and care.** Citing other CBS figures, the release notes that women who work part-time increasingly have a large part-time job (28 to 35 hours a week). It also cites earlier CBS research according to which, in 2022, just over 4 in 10 partners with young children — fathers as often as mothers — said they would prefer to share paid work and childcare equally with their partner, a majority (54%) among those without children yet, whereas 1 in 10 parents with minor children said they actually shared care and paid work equally.
