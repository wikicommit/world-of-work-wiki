*Realizing decent work in the platform economy* (ILC.114/Report V(4)) is a report of the International Labour Office, published in March 2026 for the fifth item on the agenda of the 114th Session (2026) of the International Labour Conference. Known as the "blue report", it is the fourth and final of the reports the Office prepares under the double-discussion procedure for this standard-setting item: the first two informed the Conference's first discussion in 2025, and the third, [[Report/decent-work-in-the-platform-economy]] (ILC.114/Report V(3)), transmitted the draft texts of a Convention and a Recommendation on which this report collects comments. It does not repeat the explanations given in the earlier reports, which the Office asks constituents to read together with it as a whole.

Part I gives an illustrative overview of the replies to the third report, provision by provision, grouped into governments, employers' organizations and workers' organizations, each followed by an Office commentary. Part II contains the revised draft texts of a Convention concerning decent work in the platform economy — which may be cited as the Decent Work in the Platform Economy Convention, 2026 — and a Recommendation supplementing it, which, if the Conference so decides, serve as the basis for the second discussion in June 2026. The report is based on replies received until 12 December 2025 from the governments of 74 Member States, 106 employers' organizations and 131 workers' organizations, international and regional organizations of employers and workers, and the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights.

## Details

**Form of the instruments and the Office's approach.** A majority of governments and workers' organizations replied on the basis that a Convention supplemented by a Recommendation should be adopted, while a majority of employers' organizations and some governments repeated a preference for a Recommendation alone; the International Organisation of Employers argued that a stand-alone Recommendation was the most feasible path to consensus in 2026, given how slowly the 2025 discussion had progressed. The Office noted a broadly shared view against reopening the Articles the Standard-Setting Committee agreed in June 2025 and did not change that text, apart from a broadly agreed clarification in the definition of remuneration or payment. Responding to calls for a "principles-based" Convention — while observing that few concrete proposals had been made — it removed some provisions, moved others from the Convention to the Recommendation, consolidated provisions on similar subjects and reformulated others, reducing the drafts from 31 Articles and 35 Paragraphs to 24 Articles and 19 Paragraphs. It also removed cross-references to other international labour standards, while maintaining that such references would not bind a Member State to Conventions it has not ratified, and declined to add the new provisions that various constituents proposed, citing the overall preference for streamlined texts and the lack of broad support for new subject matter. Apart from the definitions, the scope and the agreed Article on informing workers about automated systems, the substantive provisions remain in square brackets as text the Committee has not yet considered.

**Employees and other platform workers.** The third report had proposed qualifying several provisions with "according to the nature of work arrangements and the classification of digital platform workers' status in employment". A majority of workers' and employers' organizations and some governments preferred to refer only to the classification of status in employment, and some governments asked for a clearer distinction between workers in and outside an employment relationship. The revised drafts draw that distinction explicitly. The remuneration Article now sets out principles for all [[DefinedTerm/digital-platform-worker]]s — remuneration or payment due is paid in full, on time and in legal tender or, where authorized, in kind — and separately requires, for those in an employment relationship, adequate remuneration never lower than any applicable statutory or negotiated minimum wage (excluding tips and other gratuities) and reasonable compensation for expenses or other costs, which Members are to extend to other platform workers "to the extent possible and as appropriate". In the Recommendation, Members should prohibit charging fees or costs to workers in an employment relationship and permit platforms to charge other workers only to compensate for specific services provided to them. The social security and no-less-favourable-treatment provisions now compare platform workers with other workers "with the same classification of status in employment" rather than workers "in a comparable situation".

**Other changes to the draft Convention.** On occupational safety and health, the Office replaced Article 4 with a principles-based text, drawing on the Occupational Safety and Health Convention, 1981 (No. 155), under which Members take appropriate measures to prevent occupational accidents, diseases and injuries and specify the respective functions and responsibilities of public authorities, digital labour platforms, platform workers and other relevant actors; a majority of workers' organizations and some governments objected that the obligations should rest on platforms. The more detailed OSH measures — on injury associated with long hours and insufficient rest, information and training, equipment and personal protective equipment — moved to the Recommendation. The Article on violence and harassment no longer refers to gender-based violence or to the Violence and Harassment Convention, 2019 (No. 190). The classification Article, retitled "Classification in respect of the existence of an employment relationship", keeps the principle that classification is guided primarily by the facts relating to the performance of work and remuneration but drops the reference to the Employment Relationship Recommendation, 2006 (No. 198) and a paragraph on non-interference with true civil and commercial relationships; the Office described a legal presumption of employment, which workers' organizations sought, as one method among others that Member States could adopt. On automated systems, where a majority of employers' organizations argued that regulating technology lies outside the ILO's mandate, the Office replied that technological change falls within its remit when it bears on employment, working conditions or labour rights. Platforms would have to give workers and their representatives access on request, without unreasonable delay, to a written explanation of automated decisions affecting their working conditions or access to work, and to a human review of decisions resulting in non-disbursement of amounts due, account suspension or deactivation, or termination. The personal data provisions were consolidated into a single principle that data be processed only to the extent strictly necessary, with the list of prohibited processing moved to the Recommendation. Access to dispute resolution was extended to digital labour platforms as well as workers, and four implementation Articles were merged into one.

**Changes to the draft Recommendation.** Working-time provisions were consolidated into one Paragraph under which Members should, as appropriate and considering platform workers' classification of status in employment, ensure that they are adequately protected in relation to maximum hours of work and minimum rest periods, are compensated for periods spent waiting for work, and can decline a task or disconnect from a platform without retaliation. The OSH Paragraph also covers access to sanitary facilities and drinking water where necessary and so far as reasonably practicable. Paragraphs dropped for streamlining include those on skills training, tips, extending social security coverage and the portability of social security rights, notifying automated systems to the competent authority, migrants and refugees, the jurisdiction of dispute resolution and the right to stay in a territory to pursue a claim, and two Paragraphs on compliance and enforcement. The Office also deleted a reference to social dialogue "at the cross-border level". A single implementation Paragraph covers formalizing work via digital labour platforms, tackling undeclared activities, promoting fair business competition — including through reporting obligations on platforms — raising awareness, international cooperation, and collecting statistics to monitor developments.
