*Employment Trends in Ireland – What CSO data tells us* is a feature article published by Ireland's Central Statistics Office (CSO) on 31 August 2026, written as an interview with one of its senior statisticians. It explains how the official estimate of employment is produced — from the [[Dataset/irish-labour-force-survey]], combined with administrative data gathered by other public bodies — and summarises what the data show about recent trends.

## Details

**Overall employment.** Ireland has seen record employment and low unemployment since the pandemic, but growth has become more subdued. The employment rate reached 75.3% in Q3 2024, the highest in the series since 1998, and eased to 74.3% by Q2 2026, although the 2.84 million people in employment that quarter was the highest level reported in the State. The article states that the unemployment rate rose to 5.1% from 4.8% in 2025.

**Who works.** More than 1.3 million women were in employment in Q2 2026, up almost 90% since Q1 2000, and women are a large majority of workers in human health and social work and in education; a gender gap remains in employment rates and pay, as women remain more concentrated in part-time work. People aged 55 and over made up around 20% of the labour force in Q2 2026, almost double the share in 2000, and employment among those aged 65 and over has risen by almost 30% since 2022 — which the article attributes to population ageing, changing retirement decisions and financial circumstances, improved health and the availability of flexible work. Young people, many of whom combine work with education, are more likely to work part-time and in accommodation, food and retail, and their employment is more sensitive to the economic cycle: the employment rate for 15-24-year-olds was 45.2% in Q2 2026, down from 46.7% a year earlier, and their unemployment rate was 13.2%, against 4.2% for people aged 25-74.

**Work patterns and earnings.** Close to 1 million people reported [[DefinedTerm/working-from-home]] at least sometimes in Q2 2026, a number that had fallen over the year, while over 1.8 million people in employment never worked from home. Non-Irish nationals accounted for 61.4% of the increase in employee numbers between 2019 and 2024. Average weekly earnings passed €1,000 in 2025 for the first time since the series began in 2008, and rose by just under 24% between Q1 2021 and Q1 2026, from €861 to €1,065 — the same period over which the Consumer Price Index rose by 24.0%. Financial and real estate saw the highest increase, almost 30% from €1,347 to €1,748, while wages in wholesale and retail trade rose by around 18%, from €624 to €739.
