*Decent work in the platform economy* (ILC.114/Report V(3)) is a report of the International Labour Office, first published in 2025, prepared for the fifth item on the agenda of the 114th Session (2026) of the International Labour Conference. Its purpose is to transmit to governments the texts of a draft Convention concerning decent work in the platform economy, supplemented by a draft Recommendation, which the Office drafted on the basis of the Conference's first discussion of the standard-setting item and of Member States' replies to an earlier questionnaire. Governments were asked to send any comments, after consulting the most representative organizations of employers and workers, by 14 November 2025, for reflection in a fourth and final report to the Conference in June 2026.

The standard-setting process began when the ILO Governing Body, at its 347th Session in March 2023 and having taken note of a normative gap analysis, placed the item on the agenda of the 113th Session (June 2025) under a double-discussion procedure. On 13 June 2025 the Conference adopted a resolution containing proposed Conclusions; owing to a lack of time, its Standard-Setting Committee had examined only the Conclusions with a view to a Convention and reached agreement on the form of the standards, the definitions and the scope. The Office therefore placed in square brackets and italics the parts of the draft texts that the Committee had not considered.

## Details

**Office commentary.** The Office understood Committee members to favour a "principles-based" Convention that sets out key principles without prescribing detailed rules, and it identifies Articles that could move to the Recommendation, be reformulated, be removed or be consolidated. To add flexibility, it proposes the expression "according to the nature of work arrangements and the classification of digital platform workers' status in employment" in several provisions, so that Member States can tailor how certain provisions apply, including to the self-employed, while preserving broad coverage. It draws attention to the ambiguity of "organizes and/or facilitates work" in the definition of a [[DefinedTerm/digital-labour-platform]] — "or" would extend the instrument to social media, online advertising, search engines and private employment agencies using automated decision-making, while "and" would exclude platforms that merely offer matching — and notes that this would be the first use of "and/or" in a definition in a Convention. It also asks whether "or 'payment'" should be added throughout, so that the labour income of employees and of the self-employed is distinguished consistently.

**Draft Convention.** As drafted, the Convention — which may be cited as the Decent Work in the Platform Economy Convention, 2026 — defines digital labour platforms, [[DefinedTerm/digital-platform-worker]]s, intermediaries, and "remuneration" or "payment", and applies to all digital labour platforms and to all digital platform workers in the formal or informal economy, with limited exclusions possible after consultation. Its provisions cover fundamental principles and rights at work, occupational safety and health (including preventing injury to health associated with long hours of work and insufficient rest periods), violence and harassment, employment promotion, the correct classification of the employment relationship, remuneration, social security, the use of automated systems to monitor or evaluate work or generate decisions, personal data and privacy, suspension or deactivation of accounts, terms and conditions, migrants and refugees, dispute resolution, and compliance and enforcement. Under Article 17, workers affected by decisions generated by an automated system must have access to a written explanation and to a human review of decisions refusing payment, suspending or deactivating an account, or ending their engagement.

**Draft Recommendation.** The Recommendation supplements the Convention with more detailed measures, including remuneration at least equivalent to the applicable minimum wage for workers in a comparable situation; excluding expenses and tips when assessing compliance with a minimum; methods to compensate time spent waiting for work; protection in relation to limits on daily and weekly hours, rest breaks and minimum daily and weekly rest; the ability to decline a task or disconnect from a platform without retaliation; notification of automated systems to the competent authority; portability of personal data, including ratings; and the minimum content of contracts between platform workers and platforms.

**Replies and revision.** The replies to this report, and the revised drafts that resulted, are set out in the Office's fourth and final report, [[Report/realizing-decent-work-in-the-platform-economy-2026]] (ILC.114/Report V(4), March 2026), based on replies from 74 governments, 106 employers' organizations and 131 workers' organizations. Noting a broadly shared view against reopening the text agreed by the Committee in 2025, the Office kept the definition of a digital labour platform — including "organizes and/or facilitates work" — and the scope unchanged, adding "or payment" to the title and provisions on remuneration but not to the second sentence of the definition. Instead of the "nature of work arrangements" formula, which a majority of employers' and workers' organizations wanted reduced to a reference to the classification of status in employment, the revised drafts distinguish explicitly between platform workers in an employment relationship and those who are not. Several of the Articles this report proposed to move or delete — among them the detailed occupational safety and health measures and most of the provisions on personal data — went to the Recommendation or were removed, and the drafts were cut from 31 Articles and 35 Paragraphs to 24 Articles and 19 Paragraphs.
