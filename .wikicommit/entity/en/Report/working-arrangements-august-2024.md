*Working arrangements* is the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) release on working arrangements and forms of employment — casual work, fixed-term contracts, independent contractors, shift work, job flexibility and job security. The August 2024 issue was released on 9 December 2024 (formerly ABS catalogue number 6336.0). Its statistics come mainly from the Characteristics of Employment survey, with data on casual employees' preferences and on digital platform work collected in the Multi-Purpose Household Survey.

Among its key statistics for August 2024: 2.6 million employees (22% of employees) had no paid leave entitlements and 20% of employees considered their job to be casual; 4.2% of employees were on a fixed-term contract; there were 1.1 million independent contractors (7.5% of all employed); 18% of employees had no minimum guaranteed hours, down from 21% in August 2022; 36% of employed people usually worked from home; and 56% of employees were entitled to employer-provided paid parental leave.

## Details

**Casual employment.** The ABS notes that there is no single measure of [[DefinedTerm/casual-employment]] and uses employees without paid holiday or sick leave as a common proxy, alongside asking employees whether they consider their job casual. Paid leave entitlements are concentrated among higher earners: 92% of employees earning at least the median weekly wage had paid sick or holiday leave, against 47% of the lowest-paid quarter. In 2023-24, 75% of casual employees said they currently preferred casual employment, mainly for flexibility (53%) or a higher hourly pay rate (22%); most (75%) had not discussed converting to non-casual employment with their employer.

**Working time arrangements.** In August 2024, 26% of employed people had an agreement to work flexible hours and 32% usually worked extra hours or overtime; in August 2023, 19% were usually required to be on call or standby, down from 22% in 2021.

**Working from home.** The ABS has measured [[DefinedTerm/working-from-home]] since 1970. Between 1989 and 2008 around 20-30% of people worked from home, with 4-8% working most of their hours there; the rate rose to 40% in August 2021 — the pandemic years 2020 and 2021 saw the highest rates ever recorded — before easing to 36% in 2024. The most common main reasons in 2024 were to work more flexibly or choose one's own hours (25%), operating a business from home or a home-based job (23%) and catching up on work after hours (21%). 60% of managers and professionals usually worked from home, compared with 21% of other occupations, and people who worked from home were more likely to work overtime (43% compared with 25%) and to have a flexible-hours agreement (48% compared with 22%).

**Fixed-term contracts and independent contractors.** Of the 512,000 employees on fixed-term contracts, 59% had a total term of one to four years, and 75% expected to be in their current job in 12 months' time, compared with 91% of other employees; median weekly earnings were higher for fixed-term employees ($1,574 against $1,381). Independent contracting was most common in construction (24% of people employed in the industry), administrative and support services (19%) and arts and recreation services (13%).

**Digital platform work.** Collected since 2022-23, the data showed that 1.2% of employed people had undertaken digital platform work in the previous 12 months in 2023-24; 71% of them were male, 68% did not consider it their main job, the most common tasks were delivering food and other goods and providing personal transport, and 43% preferred wage-based employment. These 2023-24 estimates were later revised under a new weighting approach in the August 2025 issue.
