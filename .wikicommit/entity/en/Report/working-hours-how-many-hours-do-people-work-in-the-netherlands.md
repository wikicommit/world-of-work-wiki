Arbeidsduur: hoeveel uren werken mensen in Nederland? ("Working hours: how many hours do people work in the Netherlands?") is a publication by two authors at the Centraal Bureau voor de Statistiek (CBS), published on 15 February 2024. It starts from the observation that working hours — the amount of time worked over a period — can be measured in several ways: the time people actually work can differ from their agreed contractual hours, falling because of sickness, public holidays, leave or too little work, or rising because of time banked under a working-time reduction scheme (ADV) or paid or unpaid [[DefinedTerm/overtime]]. Figures also diverge because they come from different data sources and cover different target populations.

The publication compares the working hours of employees from two CBS sources — the [[Dataset/dutch-labour-force-survey]] (EBB) and the [[Dataset/polis-administration]], the register of employers' wage declarations — and relates both to the hours measured by the [[Dataset/dutch-labour-accounts]], which combine the two for measuring labour productivity. It uses data for 2022.

## Details

**Published figures.** The Netherlands is known as a part-time country, with the lowest share of full-time work in the EU. Depending on the definition, the share of full-timers in 2022 ranged from 44.8 per cent of employee jobs (full-time according to the employment contract, Polis administration) through 49.7 per cent of employees usually working 35 hours or more a week in all jobs (EBB) to 53.5 per cent of employees who see their main job as full-time (EBB self-assessment, the measure Eurostat uses); for the self-employed the corresponding EBB shares were 65.5 and 69.4 per cent. Average weekly hours likewise ranged from 31.0 usual hours for employees in the EBB and 29.5 paid hours per employee job in the Polis administration to 26.0 actually worked hours per employee in the Labour Accounts (provisional figures); for the self-employed, the EBB gave 38.4 usual hours and the Labour Accounts 31.8 actual hours.

**Definitions.** The publication sets out the hours concepts held in each source — [[DefinedTerm/contractual-hours]], the full-time/part-time distinction, [[DefinedTerm/usual-hours-of-work]] (in the Polis administration, "base hours") and actually worked hours ([[DefinedTerm/actual-hours-of-work]]; in the Polis administration, "regular hours") — and how they differ in their treatment of time banked under ADV, overtime paid at a premium, leave, public holidays and sickness.

**Aligning the populations.** To compare like with like, the authors align the two sources' populations: only employee jobs with a Dutch employer (removing the jobs of director–major shareholders from the Polis administration, and foreign employers and self-employment from the EBB), converting jobs to persons by taking each person's main job, and restricting both to employees aged 15 to 74 living in private households in the Netherlands.

**Results.** On this common basis, average usual hours in the EBB (30.6 a week) were about one hour higher than average base hours in the Polis administration (29.6), and the two sources' distributions across hour bands were almost identical, so they can complement each other at aggregate level. Average actual hours agreed even more closely (25.9 in the EBB, 26.1 in the Polis administration), but the distributions diverged sharply: the EBB counts leave and sickness in the week they are taken, so in 23.0 per cent of weeks employees worked fewer than 12 hours and in 37.5 per cent 35 hours or more, whereas the Polis administration spreads collectively agreed leave evenly over the weeks worked. Quarterly actual hours from the Labour Accounts, which correct register hours for leave, sickness and unpaid overtime, closely matched the EBB for both employees and the self-employed, though the EBB is itself the main input for the self-employed because no register records their hours.

**Conclusion.** Base hours in the Polis administration match usual hours in the EBB well, both on average and in hour bands; regular hours match the EBB's actual hours on average but not in bands, chiefly because of the different handling of leave, sickness absence and unpaid overtime.
