*Evaluating the Effects of Fatigue on Police Patrol Officers: Final Report* is a 122-page report in the National Institute of Justice's NIJ Research Report series, published in February 2000 (NCJ 184188) under NIJ grant 96-IJ-CX-0046. Written by Bryan J. Vila, Dennis Jay Kenney, Gregory B. Morrison and Melissa Reuland and classed as a program/project evaluation, it evaluates the prevalence and effects of objectively measured [[DefinedTerm/work-related-fatigue]] on a sample of patrol officers.

The study analysed data on police work hours, work-hour policies and procedures, accidents, injuries, illnesses, misconduct and citizen complaints. It also used self-report surveys and focus groups involving officers and their families, and data from computer-based, non-invasive eye-reaction tests of readiness for duty.

## Details

According to the report's abstract, officers who routinely worked more consecutive hours than would be legal in other public service industries displayed levels of fatigue six times higher than those found among shift workers in industrial and mining jobs. Self-reported measures of sleep quality showed high levels of sleep pathologies: only 26 percent of officers reported averaging the seven or more hours of sleep per day that research finds to be the minimum required for good health (see [[DefinedTerm/short-sleep-duration]]). The study examined many causes and correlates of fatigue, reached preliminary conclusions about its impact on officers' health, safety and performance, and offers guidelines for reducing fatigue or ameliorating its effects.
