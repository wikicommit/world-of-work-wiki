How Australians use their time is the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) release of key findings from the 2024 [[Dataset/abs-time-use-survey]]. Published on 17 December 2025 for the 2024 reference period, it describes how people aged 15 and over spent their day on paid and unpaid work and other activities, and how this differed by sex, parenthood and whether people worked from home. A supplementary release on 5 August 2026 added tables on time use by disability status, carer status and volunteer status and on the time of day of paid work, together with disability data on feeling rushed or pressed for time.

The release groups activities into four broad types of time use. Among the people who took part in each, the average day held 10 hours 43 minutes of personal care for self (sleeping, eating, and their own health and hygiene), 8 hours 20 minutes of employment and education, 4 hours 25 minutes of [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]], and 5 hours 34 minutes of free time. The ABS notes that, at this highest level, the pattern was similar to 2020-21, although changes to the survey reduce comparability between the two cycles.

## Details

**Unpaid work and care.** Most females (90%) and males (81%) did unpaid work on their diary day, and among those who did, females spent about an hour more on it (4 hours 53 minutes compared with 3 hours 52 minutes). Parents of children under 15 did the most: lone parents averaged 7 hours 4 minutes and parents in couples 6 hours 16 minutes, while female parents averaged 7 hours 29 minutes against 5 hours 2 minutes for male parents. Female parents were also more likely than male parents to look after children under 15 (88% compared with 72%). Just under a quarter of people (23%) provided [[DefinedTerm/supervisory-care-of-children]] while doing another activity.

**Paid work and working from home.** More males than females did employment activities on their diary day (48% compared with 38%), and males who did so spent almost an hour longer on them (8 hours 55 minutes compared with 7 hours 58 minutes). Fewer people did unpaid work on a day they also did paid work (80% compared with 90% on other days). On workdays, people who worked from home did less paid work than those who did not (7 hours 8 minutes compared with 8 hours 18 minutes), about an hour more unpaid work (3 hours 24 minutes compared with 2 hours 26 minutes), and more free time. On the days they worked from home, females did more unpaid work than males (3 hours 48 minutes compared with 3 hours 1 minute).

**Free time.** Most people (91%) had some free time on their diary day; males averaged 5 hours 48 minutes and females 5 hours 21 minutes. Parents had much less free time than non-parents (3 hours 38 minutes compared with 6 hours 5 minutes). Watching TV or streaming was the most common free-time activity, reported by 62% of people for an average of 2 hours 55 minutes.

**Time pressure.** A third of people (33%) always or often felt rushed or pressed for time, most commonly because of balancing work and family; see [[DefinedTerm/time-stress]].
