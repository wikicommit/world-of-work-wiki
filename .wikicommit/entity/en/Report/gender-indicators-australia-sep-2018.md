Gender Indicators, Australia, Sep 2018 (catalogue number 4125.0) is a release of the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), published on 25 September 2018 and now archived. Its Work and Family Balance section brings together sub-topics on time use (parental leave in the non-public sector, engagement in paid and unpaid work, caring for children), providing care, time stress and [[DefinedTerm/work-life-balance]], overall life satisfaction and volunteering.

## Details

**Parental leave.** In 2016–17 women took 95% of the [[DefinedTerm/primary-parental-leave]] used by non-public sector employees, and men took 95% of secondary parental leave. Managers used parental leave proportionally more than non-managers: 7.3 per 100 women managers took primary parental leave, against 4.4 per 100 women in non-managerial positions. Overall, 35,600 men and 94,500 women employed in the non-public sector used some form of parental leave that year. The ABS notes that these rates are calculated over all employees, not just parents, so they do not show the proportion of parents taking leave.

**Time stress and work–family balance.** In 2007, 42% of women and 35% of men felt always or often rushed or pressed for time ([[DefinedTerm/time-stress]]), rising to 55% of women and 46% of men among those who provided care. Trying to balance work and family responsibilities was the main reason given by both women (31%) and men (27%). About 58% of employed Australians felt their work and family responsibilities were always or often in balance, while 15% of women and 16% of men felt they were rarely or never in balance.

**Care.** In 2015, 6% of women and 3% of men aged 15 and over provided primary care to a person with disability, and over half of these primary carers (57%) were not in the labour force. In 2007, 43% of employed women and 39% of employed men had provided care for someone.

**Paid and unpaid work.** In 2006, people aged 15 and over spent on average 7 hours 34 minutes a day (women) and 7 hours 25 minutes (men) on paid and [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]] combined. Women spent about half as long as men on employment-related activities (2 hours 21 minutes against 4 hours 33 minutes a day) and nearly twice as long on unpaid work, including domestic activities (2 hours 52 minutes against 1 hour 37 minutes) and childcare (59 against 22 minutes).

**Life satisfaction and volunteering.** In 2014, 81% of both women and men aged 15 and over rated their overall life satisfaction at 7 or more out of 10. Just under a third of Australians volunteered for an organisation or group, with women slightly more likely to do so (33%) than men (29%); volunteering rates for both had fallen by about 5 percentage points since 2010.
