*Estadísticas a propósito del Día Internacional de las Trabajadoras del Hogar* ("Statistics on the occasion of the International Day of Domestic Workers") is press release no. 204/24 of Mexico's National Institute of Statistics and Geography (INEGI), issued on 26 March 2024 ahead of the day on 30 March. The day was established in 1988 at the Congress of Domestic Workers held in Bogotá, Colombia, to claim the rights of the sector, promote work in decent conditions and recognise what this work contributes to the world economy. The release brings together indicators on the sociodemographic characteristics and working conditions of people in [[DefinedTerm/paid-domestic-work]] in Mexico, drawn from the third quarter of 2023 of the National Survey of Occupation and Employment (Encuesta Nacional de Ocupación y Empleo, ENOE).

In the third quarter of 2023, 2.5 million people aged 15 and over were employed in paid domestic work in households, 4.2% of all employed people in Mexico; nine in ten of them were women. The release presents the occupation as one of those with the highest rate of informal employment, and highlights that 69.5% of paid domestic workers received no employment benefits of any kind.

## Details

**Who does the work.** Paid domestic workers were concentrated in the 30–59 age group (71.7% of women and 63.4% of men), followed by those aged 60 and over, where the share was higher among men than women (20.2% against 13.5%); their average age was 44.5 years. They had on average eight years of schooling (women) and nine (men). 43.5% had secondary education and 25.5% had completed primary school; 25.9% of men but 13.3% of women had upper secondary or higher education. 30.2% of the women and 43.1% of the men were married, and more women than men were widowed (10.9% against 2.8%) or separated (12.2% against 5.7%). 38% were heads of their household — 36.1% of the women and 55.8% of the men.

**Informality and type of work.** Of the 2.5 million, 95.0% (2,361,408) worked informally and 5.0% (123,785) formally; informality was higher among women (96.3%) than men (83.4%) (see [[DefinedTerm/informal-employment]]). 82% (two million people) were domestic employees, who clean private homes and may also cook, wash and iron, and care for people. Among women, 88.0% were domestic employees and 9.4% cared for children, people with disabilities and older people in private homes; among men, the largest groups were chauffeurs (31.5%), domestic employees (26.8%), gardeners (22.7%) and watchmen and porters in private homes (6.4%).

**Contracts and hours.** 97.1% worked without a written contract, 2.5% had one and 0.4% did not know; of those with a written contract, 80.7% had a permanent one and 15.1% a temporary one. 37.9% of women in domestic work worked 15 to 34 hours a week and 32.6% worked 35 to 48 hours, while 45.4% of men worked 35 to 48 hours and 28.4% more than 48 hours (see [[DefinedTerm/long-working-hours]]).

**Pay and benefits.** 67.8% earned up to one [[DefinedTerm/minimum-wage]], 24.2% more than one and up to two, and 2.2% more than two. The gap by sex was marked: 70.4% of women earned up to one minimum wage against 43.5% of men, and 22.8% of women against 37.1% of men earned more than one and up to two. 71.3% of women and 53.1% of men received no employment benefits, and only 13.1% of men and 2.5% of women had access both to health institutions and to other benefits.
