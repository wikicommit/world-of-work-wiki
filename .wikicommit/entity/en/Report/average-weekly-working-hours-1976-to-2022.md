*Average weekly working hours, 1976 to 2022* is an article in Statistics Canada's *Quality of Employment in Canada* series (catalogue no. 14-28-0001-X), released on 13 June 2023. It presents the average weekly working hours indicator — the average hours usually worked per week by workers in their main job, not including overtime — using annual averages from the [[Dataset/canadian-labour-force-survey]]. Statistics Canada treats the indicator as a measure of quality of employment that gives insight both into economic conditions and into the characteristics of the employed population.

The article lists several circumstances that can change average usual hours: economic cycles, the structure of the economy (the share of services- or goods-producing industries), the socio-demographic composition of the employed population, the distribution of unpaid household and family responsibilities, and wages and marginal tax rates on employment income.

## Details

**Long-run decline.** Canadian workers averaged 38.6 hours a week in their main job or business in 1976 and 35.8 in 2022, nearly 3 hours less. Hours have generally fallen in economic downturns and edged up early in recoveries, around a gradual downward trend. Part of the decline reflects the growing share of women and students in employment, since both tend to work shorter weeks. Men's average hours fell from 41.6 in 1976 to 40.6 in 1996, and from 40.2 in 1997 to 38.1 in 2022; part of this reflects the fall in the share of employed men working in manufacturing, an industry with relatively long hours, from 22.7% in 1976 to 12.3% in 2022, alongside more men working in services-producing industries. Women's hours fell slightly from 33.7 to 32.7 between 1976 and 1996, then rose to 33.2 by 2022, so men still worked longer hours than women in 2022.

**Young and older workers.** Workers aged 15 to 24 saw the most pronounced change, largely because the share of students in that age group more than doubled, from 19.0% in 1976 to 39.2% in 2022; on average they moved from working full-time hours in 1976 to part-time hours in 2022, and their largest declines in hours came during the recessions of the early 1980s and early 1990s and, to a lesser extent, 2008/2009. Among workers aged 55 and older, men's hours declined in both periods while women's fell to 1996 and then rose.

**2022 snapshot.** 81.8% of workers worked full-time (30 or more hours a week) — 87.6% of men and 75.4% of women. Average usual hours were 40.3 in the goods-producing sector, ranging from 46.3 in natural resources to 38.4 in utilities, and 34.6 in services-producing industries, from 39.8 in transportation and warehousing to 29.0 in accommodation and food services. By occupation, hours were longest in natural resources, agriculture and related production (42.0) and management (40.7), and shortest in art, culture, recreation and sport (30.6) and in sales and services (30.7), categories with more women than men and among the highest shares of younger workers. Compared with employees, [[DefinedTerm/self-employment]] was associated with longer average hours among men (40.6 against 37.7) but shorter hours among women (31.5 against 33.4).

**Measurement.** Data before 1997 are not directly comparable with later data because the survey changed how it measures hours, although the change in concepts is not accompanied by a major break in the series. Before January 1997, usual hours were the hours a respondent usually worked in a typical week, whether paid or not; from January 1997, usual hours for employees refer to their normal paid or contract hours, not counting [[DefinedTerm/overtime]], while the definition is unchanged for the self-employed and unpaid family workers. The upper limit for responses was also raised from 99 to 168 hours a week.
