*Une photographie du marché du travail en 2024* ("A snapshot of the labour market in 2024"), subtitled "Youth employment falls back, senior employment keeps rising", is Insee Première no. 2044, published by Insee on 27 March 2025 and written by Émilie Pénicaud. It gives an annual picture of employment, unemployment and labour market slack in France in 2024 based on the Labour Force Survey (enquête Emploi), the only source that measures unemployment and activity as defined by the International Labour Office (ILO). The survey is run continuously; in 2024 around 90,000 people aged 15 or over living in ordinary dwellings answered it each quarter. From 2024 the continuous survey also covers Mayotte, so the 2024 figures cover the whole of France and the long series were backcast to include it; the extension lowers the 2024 employment rate of 15-64-year-olds by 0.15 point and raises the unemployment rate by 0.05 point.

## Details

**Employment.** In 2024, 68.8% of people aged 15 to 64 were in employment in the ILO sense, up 0.5 point on 2023 and the highest level since Insee began measuring it in 1975. After rising strongly in 2021 and 2022, the employment rate of 15-24-year-olds fell back by 0.6 point to 34.4%, still 5.6 points above its low of 2020. The rate for 25-49-year-olds rose to 82.7%, its highest since 2008, and that of 50-64-year-olds rose by 1.5 points to 68.4%; for 60-64-year-olds it rose by 3.4 points, mainly because of the implementation of the 2023 pension reform. The gap between men's and women's employment rates, 30.0 points in 1975, was 5.4 points in 2024 and widened slightly over the year.

**Forms of employment.** 13.3% of people in employment were self-employed, the highest share since 1999, and a little over a quarter of the self-employed were micro-entrepreneurs ([[DefinedTerm/micro-entrepreneur]]). 72.9% held an open-ended job (a permanent contract or a civil-service post), while the combined share of fixed-term contracts and temporary agency work fell for a second year to 9.5%, 0.7 point below its pre-crisis level ([[DefinedTerm/temporary-employment]]). Only 41.6% of employed 15-24-year-olds had an open-ended job, against 76.1% of those aged 25 or over, and 29.2% were in work-study programmes or internships. Managers and professionals made up 23.0% of employment, more than manual workers (18.0%) since 2019, whereas in the early 1980s manual workers were almost four times as numerous.

**Hours and telework.** Weekly hours worked per job, which fell by 6.0% in 2020, were stable in 2024 at 31.2 hours, 1.0% below their 2019 level. 18.2% of employees teleworked at least one day a week, down 0.6 point on 2023; the share fell among managers and professionals but remained close to half of them (48.4%). Among teleworking employees, 36.4% teleworked one day a week, 39.0% two days and 24.6% three days or more, and 80.3% were satisfied with their number of telework days ([[DefinedTerm/working-from-home]]).

**Part-time work and underemployment.** 17.6% of people in employment worked part time, up 0.2 point after falling from 19.4% in 2017 to 17.2% in 2022: 26.8% of women and 8.7% of men, and up to 41.5% of low-skilled clerical and service employees ([[DefinedTerm/part-time-employment]]). [[DefinedTerm/underemployment]] stabilised at 4.3% of people in employment, 1.5 points below its pre-crisis level and its lowest since 1992; it was higher among low-skilled clerical and service employees (12.3%), young people (6.9%) and women (6.1%).

**Unemployment and the halo.** The ILO [[DefinedTerm/unemployment-rate]] averaged 7.4% in 2024, almost unchanged from 2022 (7.3%) and 2023 (7.4%), 2.9 points below its 2015 peak and at its 2008 level. 1.7% of the labour force had been unemployed for at least a year ([[DefinedTerm/long-term-unemployment]]), 23.3% of all unemployed people and 40.6% of the unemployed aged 50 or over. The [[DefinedTerm/halo-around-unemployment]] fell by 0.2 point to 4.5% of 15-64-year-olds; counting unemployment and the halo together, 10.1% of 15-64-year-olds were without a job and wanted to work, and 15.3% of 15-24-year-olds.

**Activity.** The activity rate of 15-64-year-olds rose by 0.5 point to 74.4%, also its highest since 1975. Women's rate (71.5%) was 6.0 points below men's (77.5%), a gap that was 9.8 points in 2004 and 20.6 points in 1984.
