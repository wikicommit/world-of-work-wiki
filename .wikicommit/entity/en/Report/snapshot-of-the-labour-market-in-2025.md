*Une photographie du marché du travail en 2025* ("A snapshot of the labour market in 2025"), subtitled "Unemployment rises again, the activity rate at its highest for 50 years", is Insee Première no. 2096, published by Insee on 25 March 2026 and written by Émilie Pénicaud. It gives an annual picture of employment, unemployment and labour market slack in France in 2025 based on the Labour Force Survey (enquête Emploi), the only source that measures unemployment and activity as defined by the International Labour Office (ILO). The survey is run continuously over every week of the year in metropolitan France and the overseas departments and regions; in 2025 nearly 90,000 people aged 15 or over living in ordinary dwellings answered it each quarter. Compared with the previous edition, [[Report/snapshot-of-the-labour-market-in-2024]], the long series were revised to align with the latest population estimates.

## Details

**Employment.** In 2025, 69.3% of people aged 15 to 64 were in employment in the ILO sense, up 0.3 point after four consecutive years of increase, 3.0 points above 2019 and the highest level since Insee began measuring it in 1975. The employment rate of 15-24-year-olds was stable at 34.5% (+0.1 point), still 4.6 points above 2019, after strong growth in 2020-2022 in a context of expanding work-study contracts. The rate for 25-49-year-olds edged up to 82.8%, its highest since 2008, while that of 50-64-year-olds, rising without interruption since 2009, gained 0.9 point to 69.2%, its highest since 1975; for 60-64-year-olds it rose by 2.0 points, after a 3.4-point rise in 2024 driven mainly by the 2023 pension reform. The gap between men's and women's employment rates narrowed from 30.0 points in 1975 to 5.1 points in 2025, but has been broadly stable for a decade.

**Forms of employment.** 13.5% of people in employment were self-employed, almost unchanged on 2024 and the highest share since 1998; the self-employed include [[DefinedTerm/micro-entrepreneur]]s, who made up 3.7% of employment. 72.8% held an open-ended job (a permanent contract or a civil-service post), and the combined share of fixed-term contracts and temporary agency work fell for a third year, by 0.2 point to 9.4%, 0.9 point below its pre-crisis level ([[DefinedTerm/temporary-employment]]). Only 39.8% of employed 15-24-year-olds had an open-ended job, against 76.2% of those aged 25 or over, and 30.5% were in work-study programmes or internships. Managers and professionals rose to 23.8% of employment, while manual workers fell to 17.5%; in the early 1980s manual workers were almost four times as numerous as managers. Women were far more often clerical and service employees (37.5% against 11.7% of men) and, within employees and manual workers, more often in low-skilled jobs.

**Hours and telework.** Weekly hours worked per job were 31.2 hours, as in 2024 and slightly below 2019 (-0.8%). 19.7% of employees teleworked at least one day a week, up 1.5 points on 2024; before the health crisis, the publication notes, only 4% of employees in metropolitan France teleworked at least one day a week (2019). The share rose among managers and professionals to 51.7% and in finance and insurance to 55.3%. Telework became slightly less intensive: 38.2% of teleworkers did so one day a week, 39.2% two days and 22.6% three days or more, and 79.1% were satisfied with their number of telework days ([[DefinedTerm/working-from-home]]).

**Part-time work and underemployment.** 17.7% of people in employment worked part time, up 0.2 point: 26.6% of women (down 0.2 point) and 9.2% of men (up 0.5 point), and up to 41.7% of low-skilled clerical and service employees ([[DefinedTerm/part-time-employment]]). [[DefinedTerm/underemployment]] was almost stable at 4.4% of people in employment, 1.4 points below its pre-crisis level, and was higher among low-skilled clerical and service employees (12.3%), young people (7.6%) and women (6.1%).

**Unemployment and the halo.** The ILO [[DefinedTerm/unemployment-rate]] rose by 0.3 point to an average of 7.7% in 2025, its largest increase since 2013, though still 2.7 points below its 2015 peak; among 15-24-year-olds it rose by 1.0 point to 19.8%. 1.8% of the labour force had been unemployed for at least a year ([[DefinedTerm/long-term-unemployment]]), 23.1% of all unemployed people and 39.1% of the unemployed aged 50 or over. The [[DefinedTerm/halo-around-unemployment]] held at 4.5% of 15-64-year-olds; counting unemployment and the halo together, 10.3% of 15-64-year-olds were without a job and wanted to work, and 15.9% of 15-24-year-olds.

**Activity.** The activity rate of 15-64-year-olds rose by 0.6 point to 75.1%, also its highest since 1975. Women's rate (72.3%) was 5.7 points below men's (78.0%), a gap that was 9.4 points in 2005 and 20.2 points in 1985.
