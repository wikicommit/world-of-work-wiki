This is the results report of the 2018 [[Dataset/ecetss]], Argentina's second national survey asking workers themselves about their employment, working, health and safety conditions. It was published in Buenos Aires in October 2019, edited by the Superintendencia de Riesgos del Trabajo (SRT). The survey was promoted by the SRT through its Observatory of Occupational Safety and Health and was designed and carried out jointly with the Ministry of Production and Labour's directorate for macroeconomic studies and labour statistics (DGEMyEL), with the support of the Ibero-American Social Security Organization (OISS).

Based on 8,966 employed people interviewed in their homes, the report characterises the employment, working and health conditions of the whole employed population, including workers not covered by the occupational risk system. Its chapters cover employment conditions (labour market insertion, informality, social rights, multiple job-holding and working time), working conditions (work organisation, environmental and ergonomic risks, [[DefinedTerm/psychosocial-risks]] and workplace violence, technological change), prevention, training and labour relations, workers' health, and gender inequality. The authors present it as the first time the health and safety conditions of domestic workers in private homes, unregistered employees and formal and informal self-employed workers could be characterised, and as a contribution to evidence-based public policy.

## Details

**Employment conditions.** Labour [[DefinedTerm/informal-employment]] affected 42.8% of workers: 28.5% of employees in productive units, 76.8% of domestic workers in private homes (34% of all employees), 68.3% of own-account workers and 23% of employers. Among employees it was higher for women (37%) than men (31%), reached 66.4% of those aged up to 24, and 67.5% in establishments with up to five workers. 15.5% of workers held more than one job — 19.1% of women and 12.5% of men, driven mainly by domestic work and teaching.

**Working time.** Almost half of workers (47%) worked fewer than 35 hours a week and 19% worked extended weeks of more than 48 hours. The average working week was 38 hours for people with one job, and four hours longer for those with two. Short paid working weeks were more common among women (58%), which the report links to combining paid work with unpaid domestic work, while extended weeks were more common among men (27%), the self-employed (32%) and the primary sector (41%). Among workers, 29% had rotating or variable shifts, 8.9% worked at night (at least three hours between 22:00 and 06:00) and 21.7% had split shifts; 25.4% of employees worked overtime.

**Risks at work.** The most frequent physical risks were noise (23.2%), excessively high or low temperatures (22.6%) and direct sunlight (20%); 12.9% of workers were exposed to harmful or toxic chemicals, 9.2% to second-hand tobacco smoke and 6% to biological risks. Among ergonomic risks, repetitive movements (49.9%) and forced postures (24.5%) were the most frequent. Three in ten workers had experienced some form of violence at work, most often aggression from the public, clients, pupils or patients (25.2%); 12.8% of employees reported aggression from superiors.

**Prevention and labour relations.** 37.9% of employees reported a health and safety delegate, commission or joint committee at their workplace, 48.9% had access to an occupational medicine or health and safety service, and 29.5% had been offered periodic medical examinations. 65% of workers considered themselves very well or well informed about the risks of their work, and 31.9% had taken part in work-related training in the previous year. 36.9% of registered employees were union members.

**Health.** Work accidents were more frequent among men (8.1% against 4.5% of women) and work-related illnesses among women (8.8% against 6.4%). Low psychophysical well-being on the WHO-5 scale affected 21.9% of women and 15.6% of men. 7.6% of workers reported a work-related disease diagnosed by a doctor; of the diagnoses compatible with a work relation, about 40% were psychological manifestations and 34% musculoskeletal disorders. Across the analysis, workers exposed to more risks, excessive or atypical working hours, or no possibility of taking breaks showed worse health indicators, while autonomy, social support and development opportunities were associated with better ones.

**Gender.** 89.8% of employed women and 73% of employed men did unpaid domestic work, and women who did so spent about four and a half hours a day on it compared with about three for men. 30% of women and 21.4% of men reported [[DefinedTerm/double-presence]], the need to be at work and at home at the same time.
