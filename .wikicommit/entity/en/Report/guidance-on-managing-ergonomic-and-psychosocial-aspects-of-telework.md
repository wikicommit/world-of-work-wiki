*Orientaciones para la gestión de los aspectos ergonómicos y psicosociales en situación de teletrabajo* ("Guidance for managing ergonomic and psychosocial aspects in telework") is a technical document in the *Documentos Técnicos* series of Spain's National Institute for Safety and Health at Work (INSST), published in Madrid in July 2022. It offers an updated technical and preventive framework for the integrated management of telework in companies and organisations, with particular emphasis on its design, planning and implementation and on addressing the ergonomic and psychosocial risks associated with it. It builds on earlier INSST publications on telework and on the effects of information and communication technologies (ICT), and is framed by Spain's [[Legislation/spain-remote-work-act]].

The document identifies which aspects of prevention telework shares with on-site work and which are specific to it. Its starting point is that occupational safety and health obligations and responsibilities remain in the same terms as for on-site work, under Law 31/1995 on the Prevention of Occupational Risks and its implementing rules, while the characteristics of telework — the exclusive or predominant use of ICT, work away from the employer's premises and its regular character — raise questions that preventive management must address. It notes that the future of telework will depend on generating working conditions that allow its potential risks to be prevented and managed effectively.

## Details

**Preventive management.** Because the constitutional inviolability of the home can make on-site risk assessment difficult, the document proposes a management approach that goes beyond assessment and rests on six areas: design and primary prevention; the suitability of the job and of the people (teleworkers and their managers); information and training for staff and managers; risk assessment; two-way communication; and periodic review of working conditions. It stresses that self-assessment by teleworkers is ruled out — they supply information, but the assessment is carried out by the prevention service — and that assessments should take account of how intensively telework is performed, as well as gender and age.

**Ergonomic risks.** Computer-based telework exposes workers to physical risks (forced and sustained postures, repetitive movements and sedentary behaviour, which can lead to physical fatigue and musculoskeletal disorders), environmental risks (chiefly visual fatigue from screen work) and risks linked to mental workload. Preventive measures include a correctly adjusted workstation, external keyboard, mouse and screen when using a laptop, short and frequent breaks of 5 to 8 minutes every hour or hour and a half, changing from sitting to standing, lighting of 300 to 500 lux, and an internal policy that encourages disconnection.

**Psychosocial risks.** Following a classification based on INSST's earlier technical notes, the document examines working time, autonomy, quantitative and qualitative workload, psychological demands, variety and content, participation and supervision, interest in workers and compensation, role, relationships and social support, work–life balance, and equipment and environment. It warns that ICT can prolong the working day and blur the boundaries between work, family and rest, that technological monitoring can make autonomy more apparent than real, and that isolation weakens social support. Consequences include [[DefinedTerm/technostress]], cyberbullying, social isolation, work–family conflict, sleep disturbance and unhealthy habits. Among the measures it proposes are fixed start and end times with flexibility, guaranteeing an effective right to digital disconnection (see [[DefinedTerm/right-to-disconnect]]), realistic workloads, clear objectives and roles, close and individualised supervision that respects privacy, virtual support networks, equal opportunities for promotion, and anti-harassment protocols covering remote work. It cautions against treating telework as a work–life balance measure in itself, which it says would perpetuate gender roles that disadvantage women.

**Annexes.** Six annexes support information gathering: basic aspects to consider in designing telework, sources of information, a checklist of ergonomic risk factors based on the rules for screen work, a subjective questionnaire on lighting, a list of psychosocial issues to consider, and topics for a telework training plan. The document states that these do not replace risk assessment.
