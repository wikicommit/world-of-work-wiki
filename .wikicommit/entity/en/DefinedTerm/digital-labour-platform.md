A digital labour platform is an entity that uses digital technologies and automated decision-making to organize or facilitate work that people perform for pay. The draft Convention concerning decent work in the platform economy, set out in the International Labour Office's report [[Report/decent-work-in-the-platform-economy]], defines it as "a legal person or, where applicable under national law, natural person that, through digital technologies, using automated decision-making systems: (i) organizes and/or facilitates work performed by persons for remuneration or payment, for the provision of service, upon request of the recipient or requestor; (ii) regardless of whether that work is performed online or in a specific geographic location". The definition reproduces point 3(a) of the Conclusions agreed at the International Labour Conference's first discussion in 2025.

## Usage

The definition determines which platforms the proposed instruments would cover, since the draft Convention applies to all digital labour platforms. The Office points to the ambiguity of "organizes and/or facilitates work". Read as "or", facilitating work alone would suffice, bringing within scope a wide range of digital platforms — including social media, online advertising and search engines, which usually fall outside the regulation of platform work — as well as private employment agencies that use automated decision-making systems, raising questions about the link with the Private Employment Agencies Convention, 1997 (No. 181). Read as "and", both conditions would be needed, excluding platforms that merely offer a matching function. Some Committee members argued that coverage should be limited to platforms that organize, assign or manage work, not those that merely publish online advertising such as online job billboards. The Office notes that this would be the first time "and/or" is used in a definition in a Convention and invited constituents' views on it.
