A digital platform worker is a person who performs paid work organized or facilitated by a [[DefinedTerm/digital-labour-platform]]. The draft Convention concerning decent work in the platform economy, set out in [[Report/decent-work-in-the-platform-economy]], defines the term as "a person employed or engaged to work: (i) for the provision of service organized and/or facilitated by a digital labour platform; (ii) for remuneration or payment; (iii) regardless of their classification of status in employment". The definition therefore covers employees and self-employed workers alike, and the draft Convention would apply to all digital platform workers, whether in the formal or informal economy, unless otherwise specified.

## Usage

Because the category spans different employment statuses, the draft instruments distinguish between what is due to different workers. "Remuneration" or "payment" is defined as the amount due to a digital platform worker under national laws, regulations, collective agreements or contractual obligations, according to their classification of status in employment, in exchange for the work performed, and excludes compensation for expenses or other costs incurred in carrying out the work; the Committee added "or 'payment'" to distinguish the labour income of employees from that of the self-employed. The Office notes that while a principle in the Convention may apply to different categories of workers, the rights, levels of protection and ways of implementing them may differ between those categories, and it proposed qualifying several provisions with "according to the nature of work arrangements and the classification of digital platform workers' status in employment".

The draft Convention would require Member States to ensure the correct classification of digital platform workers in respect of the existence of an employment relationship, guided primarily by the facts relating to the performance of work and the worker's remuneration and taking into account the Employment Relationship Recommendation, 2006 (No. 198). It also defines an "intermediary" as a person that makes the work of a digital platform worker available through contractual relationships with both the platform and the worker, or as part of a subcontracting chain between them.
