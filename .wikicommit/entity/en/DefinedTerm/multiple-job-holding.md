Multiple job-holding is working more than one job at the same time: a multiple job-holder is an employed person who has a main job and one or more secondary jobs, as opposed to a single job-holder. The Australian Bureau of Statistics reports it both as a level (the number of multiple job-holders) and as a rate (the share of employed people who hold more than one job).

## Usage

In Australia, the Australian Bureau of Statistics measures multiple job-holding in its quarterly Multiple job-holders release (see [[Report/multiple-job-holders-june-2026]]), combining headline estimates from its Labour Account with Labour Force Survey and tax-based Jobs in Australia data. The release reports multiple job-holding by sex, age, industry and occupation of the main job, hours worked and geography. In June 2026, 6.9% of employed people in Australia held more than one job, a record high; the rate had stayed between 5.0% and 6.0% from 1994 to 2019.

The ABS figures show that multiple job-holding is more common among women than men, and most common among young workers aged 20-24. Multiple job-holders typically combine part-time hours in their main job with part-time hours in a secondary job, so that their total usual hours (39.3 a week in March 2026) exceed those of single job-holders (34.8), even though they work fewer hours in their main job. Some industries lend themselves to it — seasonal agricultural work for several employers, or labour hire work for several businesses — and second jobs are often in the same industry as the main job.
