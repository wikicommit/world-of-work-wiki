Split working hours (*heures fractionnées*) are usual working hours made up of periods of work interrupted by long unpaid breaks within the same day. In the Insee Références study [[Report/atypical-working-hours-women-less-qualified-and-foreign-born-most-exposed]] (2025), they are one of six types of usual working hours derived from employees' reported start and finish times in the [[Dataset/french-working-conditions-survey]]: employees who reported two periods of work at least three hours apart were identified as working split hours. The study counts them among [[DefinedTerm/atypical-working-hours]] and, citing research on low pay, describes them as the most constraining form in terms of time organisation and pay.

## Usage

In metropolitan France in 2019, 7.1% of employees had split usual hours: 9.3% of women and 4.9% of men, so that 65.5% of the employees working split hours were women. Split hours concerned the least qualified more than any other form of atypical hours — 61% of the women and 60% of the men working them had no baccalauréat — and people born abroad were over-represented (19% of the women and 23% of the men working split hours, against 9% and 10% of those on standard hours).

The occupations differ by gender. Among women, they were mainly unskilled employees and workers: 36% worked in personal services, such as home helps and domestic helps, and 11% were unskilled artisanal workers, that is, cleaning agents. Among men, drivers (19%), skilled and unskilled artisanal workers such as roofers, refuse collectors and apprentice bakers or butchers (22%), and personal-services staff such as waiters and hotel and restaurant staff (12%) were over-represented. At otherwise comparable characteristics, unskilled artisanal women workers had the highest probability of working split hours of all employees (19%).

The study links split hours closely to part-time work: at comparable characteristics, women working part time involuntarily had 11.3 times the risk of having split rather than standard hours, and those working part time for family or personal reasons 7.9 times the risk, compared with full-time workers (see [[DefinedTerm/involuntary-part-time-work]] and [[DefinedTerm/part-time-employment]]). Whereas women on long or shifted hours more often also worked regularly at weekends than men on the same hours, women on split hours did so less often than men on split hours.
