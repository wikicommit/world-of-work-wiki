Virtual presenteeism is working from home while one is sick, although not to an extent that precludes working — that is, carrying on with work tasks remotely while unwell instead of taking sick leave. Eurofound describes it as a relatively new phenomenon associated with telework and [[DefinedTerm/hybrid-work]].

## Usage

In [[Report/the-future-of-telework-and-hybrid-work]], Eurofound lists virtual presenteeism among the health and well-being outcomes, alongside fatigue and anxiety, that are associated with heavy workloads and continuous connectivity, and that are likely to be more common among employees who telework or have hybrid arrangements. The report warns that, if left unchecked, it may become more widespread to the detriment of employees' health, and it cites high levels of stress, isolation and virtual presenteeism as frequently mentioned disadvantages of telework. In its least favourable scenario for 2035, in which organisations disengage from telework and hybrid work, it describes increased reporting of both sick leave and virtual presenteeism.

The report's response is organisational: it recommends that managers be trained to recognise these health and well-being risks and support employees in self-managing them, that psychosocial risk assessments at company level be used to identify and mitigate risks to teleworkers and hybrid workers, and that organisations re-examine their risk assessment practices with these disadvantages in mind.
