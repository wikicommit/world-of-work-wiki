In Australian Bureau of Statistics gender indicators, primary parental leave is leave taken by a member of a couple or a single carer, regardless of gender, who is identified as having greater responsibility for the day-to-day care of a child. Secondary parental leave is leave taken by a member of a couple or a single carer, regardless of gender, who is not the primary carer. The ABS describes primary parental leave as the type of leave most likely to affect people's career trajectories.

## Usage

The distinction is used to report how parental leave is shared between women and men. In [[Report/gender-indicators-australia-sep-2018]], women took 95% of the primary parental leave used by non-public sector employees in 2016–17, while 95% of secondary parental leave was taken by men; women managers took primary parental leave at a rate of 7.3 per 100, against 4.4 per 100 for women in non-managerial positions, and the corresponding rates for men were 0.4 and 0.2 per 100. These take-up rates are calculated over all employees rather than parents only, so they do not show the share of parents who take leave.
