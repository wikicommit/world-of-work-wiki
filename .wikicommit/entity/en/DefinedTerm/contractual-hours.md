Contractual hours are the number of hours per week agreed as part of an employment contract — the fixed contractual hours, without taking into account extra hours worked and paid or fewer hours worked and not paid. In the Centraal Bureau voor de Statistiek's account of Dutch working-time measures, contractual working time is the basis for most other working-hours concepts, and the time people actually work can differ from it in either direction: less because of sickness, public holidays, leave or too little work, more because of time banked under a working-time reduction scheme (ADV) or paid or unpaid [[DefinedTerm/overtime]]. The concept does not exist for people who do not work fixed hours.

## Usage

In the Netherlands, contractual hours have been collected in the [[Dataset/dutch-labour-force-survey]] since 2021, which first establishes whether an employee has a contract for a fixed number of hours and then how many. The [[Dataset/polis-administration]] has had a contractual-hours-per-week variable since 2016, but because employers do not yet always fill it in correctly, CBS did not consider it suitable for publication as of 2024. For people with fixed hours, the survey's question on [[DefinedTerm/usual-hours-of-work]] starts from the contractual hours, asking whether the respondent usually works them in weeks without holidays or sickness — someone contracted for 36 hours who usually works 40 to build up ADV time can report 40 usual hours.
