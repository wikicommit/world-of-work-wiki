Physician interview guidance (面接指導) is the Japanese occupational health measure under which an employer has a physician meet a worker whose fatigue has accumulated through long working hours, ascertain the worker's physical and mental condition by interview and other means, and give guidance accordingly; the employer then hears the physician's opinion and takes follow-up measures based on it. Under the Industrial Safety and Health Act it rests on medical findings that the onset of cerebrovascular and heart disease is strongly associated with long working hours, and its purpose is to prevent such disease in workers whose health risk has risen because of accumulated fatigue ([[DefinedTerm/karoshi]]).

## Usage

The following describes the scheme as set out in a fiscal year 2015 seminar pamphlet produced under a commission from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare (MHLW).

**Who is covered.** Hours that count are overtime and holiday work, defined as the time worked beyond 40 hours a week excluding breaks; for a month they are calculated as total hours worked minus (calendar days in the month ÷ 7) × 40, at least once a month on a fixed date such as the wage closing date. The employer **must** provide interview guidance to a worker whose overtime and holiday work exceeds 100 hours in a month and who shows accumulated fatigue, when that worker requests it — unless a physician considers it unnecessary, for example because the worker had interview guidance within the previous month. The employer **should endeavour** to provide interview guidance, or an equivalent measure, to workers who exceed 80 hours a month and show accumulated fatigue or have health concerns (on request), and to workers who meet criteria the workplace sets for itself. Examples of equivalent measures are health guidance by a public health nurse, checking fatigue with a self-assessment checklist and interviewing those who need it, and the employer taking advice on health management from an occupational physician.

The pamphlet recommends that workplaces set their own criteria so that every worker with more than 100 hours of overtime and holiday work in a month, or an average of more than 80 hours a month over two to six months, receives interview guidance, and that they consider which workers above 45 hours a month need health measures. Workers on discretionary work systems are calculated on the basis of the "state of working hours" the employer tracks for their health and welfare. Small workplaces with fewer than 50 regular workers are also required to provide interview guidance, and may use a regional occupational health centre where they cannot appoint a physician themselves; for dispatched workers the obligation lies with the dispatching employer.

**Procedure.** The occupational physician encourages eligible workers to apply. A request is made in a form that leaves a record, such as a written form or e-mail, normally within about a month of the calculation date, and interview guidance follows within about a month of the request. The physician checks the worker's working conditions, accumulated fatigue and other aspects of physical and mental health; the employer must keep a record of the results for five years and hear the physician's opinion on necessary measures, normally within about a month. Follow-up measures include changing the place of work or the work assigned, shortening working hours, reducing the number of late-night shifts and reporting to the health committee. Those involved are bound by confidentiality, and workers must not be treated disadvantageously because of the results — particularly where mental health problems come to light, which may call for cooperation with a psychiatrist.

The pamphlet stresses that interview guidance does not make long hours acceptable: the first step remains for the human resources department and line managers to track and reduce overtime ([[DefinedTerm/overtime]]).
