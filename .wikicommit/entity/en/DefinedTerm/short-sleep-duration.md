Short sleep duration is sleeping fewer than 7 hours per day. The threshold follows the determination by the American Academy of Sleep Medicine and the Sleep Research Society that adults require at least 7 hours of sleep per day to promote optimal health; in the CDC analysis [[ScholarlyArticle/short-sleep-duration-by-occupation-group-29-states-2013-2014]], survey answers to the question of how many hours of sleep a person gets in a 24-hour period were split into at least 7 hours (sufficient sleep) and fewer than 7 hours (short sleep duration).

## Usage

According to that analysis, short sleep duration has been linked to adverse health outcomes including cardiovascular disease, obesity, diabetes, depression and anxiety, and to safety problems such as drowsy driving and injuries. Work-related factors such as job stress, work hours, [[DefinedTerm/shift-work]] and physically demanding work are associated with sleep duration and quality, and because these vary by occupation, so does the prevalence of short sleep. In 2013–2014 data from the [[Dataset/behavioral-risk-factor-surveillance-system]] for 29 US states, 36.5% of currently employed adults reported short sleep duration, ranging from 21.4% among air transportation workers to 58.2% among communications equipment operators.

The same article cites an estimate that short sleep among the US working population costs the economy $411 billion a year, equivalent to 2.28% of gross domestic product, and that 1.2 million working days are lost each year in the United States because of sleep deprivation.
