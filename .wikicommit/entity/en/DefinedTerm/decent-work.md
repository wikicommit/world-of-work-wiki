Decent work means that economic growth generates not just any kind of job but quality jobs: in the formulation Eurostat cites from the European Commission, work should deliver fair income, workplace security and social protection for families, better prospects for personal development and social integration, and equality of opportunity. A safe and healthy working environment — free of fatal and non-fatal accidents at work (see [[DefinedTerm/accident-at-work]]), occupational diseases and other work-related health problems — is described as a prerequisite for it. Decent work is one of the aims of Sustainable Development Goal 8 (SDG 8), which calls for sustained, inclusive and sustainable economic growth, full and productive employment and decent work for all, and recognises the importance of growth and productivity for creating well-paid quality jobs.

## Usage

Eurostat monitors SDG 8 in an EU context through three strands — sustainable economic growth, employment and decent work — in its series of monitoring reports on the SDGs. Its main indicators for the goal are real GDP per capita, the investment share of GDP, the employment rate of people aged 20–64, the [[DefinedTerm/long-term-unemployment]] rate, the share of young people neither in employment nor in education and training ([[DefinedTerm/neet]]) and the rate of fatal accidents at work. The 2026 edition assessed the EU as having made strong progress over the short term. The employment rate reached a record 76.1% in 2025, leaving the EU on track for its 2030 target of at least 78%, although progress towards the target of reducing the NEET rate to 9% had slowed.

Under the decent-work strand, Eurostat reports that the rate of fatal accidents at work fell by 29.4% between 2010 and 2023, to 1.63 per 100 000 employed persons, although mining and quarrying (10.78) and construction (6.29) remained especially prone to fatal accidents and the rate in agriculture, forestry and fishing rose by 31.4% over the same period. Fair income and social protection are tracked through [[DefinedTerm/in-work-poverty]], which fell to 8.2% in 2024, the lowest level since monitoring began in 2010.

Eurostat also treats non-standard work as part of the picture. Fixed-term contracts, part-time employment and platform work may offer flexibility to employers and workers but are not always the employee's choice: in 2025, 3.3% of employees aged 20–64 were involuntarily on temporary contracts (29.0% of all temporary employees) and [[DefinedTerm/involuntary-part-time-work]] fell to 3.1% of total employment, from 4.7% in 2019. The share of solo self-employed (those without employees) in total employment fell slightly, from 9.4% in 2019 to 8.9% in 2025; Eurostat notes that solo [[DefinedTerm/self-employment]] can point to entrepreneurial initiative but can also be used as a proxy for "bogus" self-employment that conceals a dependent employment relationship.

Among EU policies linked to the strand, the Directive on adequate minimum wages seeks to enhance the adequacy of the [[DefinedTerm/minimum-wage]], helping to improve living and working conditions while reducing in-work poverty, and the Quality Jobs Roadmap sets out a coordinated plan to improve working conditions through fair wages, secure employment, skills development, social protection and strong social dialogue.
