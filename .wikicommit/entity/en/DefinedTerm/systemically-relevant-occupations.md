Systemically relevant occupations (systemrelevante Berufe) are occupations that are indispensable for maintaining central infrastructure and public provision — for example in health care, retail and logistics. The term came into prominence during the COVID-19 pandemic, which brought the importance of these occupations into focus.

## Usage

The IAB report [[Report/working-conditions-before-during-and-after-the-covid-19-pandemic]] classifies employees as systemically relevant using the list of "first hour" systemically relevant occupations published by the Berlin Senate at the start of the pandemic, which can be applied to the occupational classification in survey data. In its data, a large share of systemically relevant employees work in health occupations, in transport and logistics, and in social and cultural services, but the category is not confined to particular fields and extends across many occupational groups; the report describes it as heterogeneous.

According to the report, earlier studies found that employees in systemically relevant occupations already had lower employment quality and lower incomes on average before the pandemic, and in its own data they reported significantly lower pay satisfaction and work autonomy than other employees before 2020. The report expects the pandemic to have affected these occupations differently from others: adjustments such as home-office arrangements were not possible in many of them, and high incidence rates and additional protective measures could instead worsen aspects of employment quality — higher patient numbers in health care being one example of added strain. In its data, their perceived workload rose clearly during the pandemic and remained significantly above its pre-crisis level afterwards. The report also notes that the Federal Employment Agency's bottleneck analyses show persistent labour shortages in occupations strongly represented in this group, such as care, logistics and passenger transport, and argues for targeted measures to improve their working conditions and attractiveness.
