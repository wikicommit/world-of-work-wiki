The Defining Elements of Total Worker Health are five features NIOSH says should be present in a workplace seeking to advance worker safety, health, and well-being through [[DefinedTerm/total-worker-health]]. [[Report/fundamentals-of-total-worker-health-approaches]] uses them as a framework for reflection and improvement, with examples and two planning worksheets.

## Usage

1. **Leadership commitment:** Leaders at all levels visibly treat worker safety and health as a core organizational function, provide resources, and build accountability. Supervisors can either enable or impede participation when program activities compete with production demands.
2. **Work design:** Eliminate or reduce safety and health hazards, including risks arising from how work is organized, before relying on individual measures. The workbook illustrates this priority with the [[DefinedTerm/total-worker-health-hierarchy-of-controls]].
3. **Worker engagement:** Involve workers and supervisors in identifying issues, designing programs, implementation, and evaluation. The workbook treats workers as participants in shaping interventions rather than merely recipients of services.
4. **Confidentiality and privacy:** Protect sensitive worker information, discuss its uses openly, and consider the risk of stigma or employment consequences when identifiable health data are collected.
5. **Integrated systems:** Coordinate relevant policies and teams across safety and health, human resources, benefits, and workplace health efforts; assess where existing work overlaps and where coordination can improve it.

The workbook proposes using these elements to compare present practice with desired changes and then make an action plan. Its examples are illustrative; it says that not every suggestion applies to every organization.
