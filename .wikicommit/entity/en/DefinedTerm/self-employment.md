Self-employment is work in which people are their "own boss", working independently to operate a business or professional activities with or without employees, as distinct from paid employees who work for someone else. In Statistics Canada's definition, the self-employed are working owners of an incorporated or an unincorporated business, farm or professional practice; the unincorporated group also includes self-employed workers who do not own a business, such as babysitters and newspaper carriers. Unpaid family workers — people who work without pay on a farm or in a business or professional practice owned and operated by another family member living in the same dwelling — are also counted among the self-employed. A person is classified by their job in the survey reference week, or, if they held more than one job, by the one at which they worked the most hours.

## Usage

Labour statistics distinguish types of self-employment because the umbrella category can mask important differences. Statistics Canada splits the self-employed by legal status and by whether they have paid help. The incorporated work for themselves in a business formed into a legal entity under provincial and federal law and are generally seen as entrepreneurs who own a separate entity; the unincorporated work in a business that is not a separate legal entity and are often described as own-account self-employed. Each group is subdivided into those with and without paid employees, giving four categories plus unpaid family workers. Whether someone is incorporated does not necessarily depend on the type of work they do — store owners and restaurant owners appear among both incorporated and unincorporated employers — but incorporation brings limited personal liability and tax advantages, and it is more common in some industries, such as professional, scientific and technical services, than in others, such as agriculture.

The *self-employment rate* is the share of working people who are self-employed. In Canada it was 12% in 1976, peaked at 17% in the late 1990s and has since stayed around 15%, falling slightly during the COVID-19 pandemic. It rises with age and is consistently lower for women than for men (11% versus 16% in 2022). Statistics Canada's study [[Report/self-employment-among-women-in-canada]] traces how women's self-employment shifted from unpaid family work, mostly in agriculture, toward unincorporated and incorporated work in health care and professional services, while self-employed women remained less likely than men to have employees or to be incorporated. It cites earlier findings that women more often than men give flexible hours and [[DefinedTerm/work-life-balance]] as reasons for becoming self-employed, and notes that lower self-employment rates among women are common to all OECD countries, partly because women are concentrated in industries where self-employment is less common.

The category also overlaps with newer forms of work. The same study points to the rise of the [[DefinedTerm/gig-economy]] and online platforms as part of the changing nature of self-employment.
