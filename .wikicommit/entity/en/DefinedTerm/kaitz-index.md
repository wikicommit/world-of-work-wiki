The Kaitz index is the ratio of a country's national [[DefinedTerm/minimum-wage]] to median gross earnings — the level of earnings that divides all employees into two equal groups, half earning less and half earning more. Expressing the minimum wage this way shows its level relative to the country's own pay distribution rather than in euro or purchasing power terms.

## Usage

Eurostat publishes the ratio for EU and candidate countries. Its 2024 figures combine the minimum wages in force with median gross earnings taken from the [[Dataset/structure-of-earnings-survey]] 2022 — which covers enterprises with 10 or more employees and excludes agriculture, forestry and fishing and public administration, defence and compulsory social security — and extrapolated to 2024 with the wage component of the Labour Cost Index. Overtime and shift-work payments are excluded from median earnings, part-time workers' earnings are converted into full-time equivalents, and for Germany, France and Ireland, whose minimum wages are set by the hour, the ratio is calculated against median hourly rather than monthly earnings.

In 2024 the ratio in the EU ranged from 44% in Estonia to 69% in Portugal. It was above 60% in eight countries (Portugal, Poland, France, Slovenia, Romania, Greece, the Netherlands and Luxembourg), between 50% and 60% in twelve, and below half of median earnings in Latvia (48%) and Estonia.
