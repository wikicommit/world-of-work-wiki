Under the US [[Legislation/fair-labor-standards-act]] (FLSA), the regular rate of pay is the hourly rate on which [[DefinedTerm/overtime]] pay is computed: covered employees who are not exempt must receive at least one and one-half times their regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek. Whether an employee is paid by the hour, by the piece, by salary, by commission or on some other basis, the regular rate is the average hourly rate derived from those earnings — the total pay for employment in the workweek, less the payments the Act itself excludes, divided by the total number of hours actually worked. It cannot be less than the minimum wage.

## Usage

According to the US Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division (Fact Sheet #23, revised October 2019), the regular rate includes all remuneration for employment except payments excluded by the Act. Excluded payments include reimbursement of expenses incurred on the employer's behalf, premium payments for overtime work and true premiums for work on Saturdays, Sundays and holidays, discretionary bonuses, gifts and gift-like payments on special occasions, and pay for occasional periods without work because of vacation, holidays or illness. Conversely, when an employer provides goods or facilities as non-cash payments, their reasonable cost to the employer or fair value must be included.

Where an employee does two or more kinds of work with different straight-time rates in a single workweek, the regular rate for that week is the weighted average of those rates: the earnings at all rates are added together and divided by the total hours worked at all jobs. Section 7(g)(2) of the Act also allows, under specified conditions, overtime to be computed at one and one-half times the hourly rate in effect when the overtime work is performed.

The Division illustrates several typical errors. A fixed salary for a regular workweek longer than 40 hours does not discharge the overtime obligation: for a 45-hour week paid $405, the regular rate is $405 ÷ 45 = $9.00, and the employee is owed an additional half of that rate for each of the 5 overtime hours ($4.50 × 5 = $22.50). A lump sum paid for overtime work without regard to the number of overtime hours does not count as an overtime premium even if it equals or exceeds what is owed by the hour; a flat sum for a job, such as 6 hours' pay regardless of the time actually spent, must instead be included in the regular rate.
