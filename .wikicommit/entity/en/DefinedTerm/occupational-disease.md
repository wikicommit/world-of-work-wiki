An occupational disease (*maladie professionnelle*) is, as defined in the French official statistics Insee publishes, harm to health contracted in the course of work that results from a series of slowly developing events to which no certain origin or date can be assigned. The figures Insee reports count new cases recognised by the general social security scheme, the agricultural scheme and the pension fund for local government employees (CNRACL).

## Usage

In 2022, these schemes recognised 55,100 new cases of occupational disease, and 28,800 permanent partial incapacities linked to an occupational disease. Occupational diseases arise later in life than accidents at work: three quarters of recognitions occur at age 45 or over. In the general scheme, 87% of recognised occupational diseases are musculoskeletal disorders, which affect women more, and 4% are asbestos-related conditions, which mostly concern men. L’Assurance Maladie – Risques professionnels observed a break in its series from 2022, so these figures are not comparable with those Insee published in earlier editions.
