Workplace harassment is objectionable or unwelcome conduct, comments or actions by an individual, at any event or location related to work, that can reasonably be expected to offend, intimidate, humiliate or degrade. As Statistics Canada's study [[Report/harassment-in-canadian-workplaces]] describes it, the term covers a range of behaviour, from interpersonal mistreatment such as disrespect, condescension and degradation — often called workplace incivility — to more physical forms such as physical assault (sometimes called workplace violence), sexual assault, bullying or the threat of harm.

## Usage

The same study notes that workplace harassment can have far-reaching effects on workers' health and well-being and on their job tenure, job stability and job satisfaction, and may affect the wider economy through the costs of absenteeism, lost productivity and job turnover. It also observes that how harassment is defined, the reference period and the data collection method differ between studies, which makes estimates hard to compare.

In Canada's [[Dataset/general-social-survey-on-canadians-at-work-and-home]] of 2016, workplace harassment was measured as self-reported experience in the past 12 months of five types of behaviour: verbal abuse, humiliating behaviour, threats, physical violence, and unwanted sexual attention or sexual harassment; within that fifth type, the survey cannot distinguish unwanted sexual attention from sexual harassment. Self-reported harassment does not necessarily mean that an official complaint was made. The survey also asked who was responsible — a supervisor or manager, a colleague or peer, an employee, a client or customer, a board member or shareholder, or someone else — because the relationship between harasser and target matters: harassment by someone in a position of authority may be worse for the worker. On this measure, 19% of women and 13% of men who worked for pay reported being harassed at work in the past year, most often by clients or customers.

Sexual harassment is also defined in law. The [[Legislation/canada-labour-code]] establishes employees' right to employment free of sexual harassment, requires employers to take positive action to prevent it, and defines it as any conduct, comment, gesture or contact of a sexual nature that is likely to cause offence or humiliation to an employee, or that the employee might reasonably perceive as placing a condition of a sexual nature on employment or on any opportunity for training or promotion.
