A deemed working hours system (みなし労働時間制) is a Japanese working-time arrangement under which an employee is treated as having worked a predetermined number of hours, instead of hours being counted as actually worked. The Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare describes three such systems.

## Usage

- **Deemed hours for work outside the workplace** (事業場外みなし労働時間制) applies where employees work outside the workplace and their working hours are difficult to calculate; they are then, in principle, deemed to have worked their prescribed working hours.
- **Discretionary work for specialised professional work** (専門業務型裁量労働制) covers 20 kinds of work, such as designers and systems engineers, in which the employer gives no specific instructions on how the work is done or how time is allocated. Employees are deemed to have worked the number of hours set in a labour–management agreement, regardless of the hours actually worked.
- **Discretionary work for planning work** (企画業務型裁量労働制) covers planning, drafting, research and analysis work on the running of the business, again where the employer gives no specific instructions on the means of performing it or on time allocation. Employees are deemed to have worked the number of hours set by a labour–management committee, regardless of the hours actually worked.
