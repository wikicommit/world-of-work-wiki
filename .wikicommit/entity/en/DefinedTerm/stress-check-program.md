The Stress Check Program (ストレスチェック制度) is a Japanese system that obliges employers to have the degree of psychological burden on their workers checked by questionnaire, to offer physician interview guidance to workers found to be under high stress, and to use the results to improve the workplace. It was created by the act amending the Industrial Safety and Health Act promulgated on 25 June 2014 and took effect on 1 December 2015. Its aim is primary prevention: helping workers become aware of their own stress and, by making workplaces easier to work in, preventing mental health problems before they arise ([[DefinedTerm/psychosocial-risks]]).

## Usage

The following describes the program as set out in a fiscal year 2015 seminar pamphlet produced under a commission from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare.

**The check.** Employers must carry out a stress check once a year for workers in regular employment; for workplaces with fewer than 50 employees it is, for the time being, only an obligation to endeavour. The questionnaire must cover all three domains of job stressors, psychological and physical stress reactions, and support from people around the worker. The check is conducted by a physician, a public health nurse, or a nurse or psychiatric social worker who has received specified training, preferably the occupational physician. It may be carried out together with the general periodic health check, though the results are handled differently.

**Results and interview guidance.** The implementer notifies each worker of the results directly, and they may not be given to the employer without the worker's consent. When a worker assessed as high-stress and in need of interview guidance asks for it, the employer must arrange interview guidance by a physician, hear the physician's opinion — for example on limiting overtime or changing work — and take work-related measures where needed, such as changing the place of work or the work assigned, shortening working hours or reducing late-night work ([[DefinedTerm/physician-interview-guidance-for-long-working-hours]]). Disadvantageous treatment on the grounds of the results of interview guidance is prohibited.

**Group analysis.** As an obligation to endeavour, the implementer analyses results collectively by workplace unit and passes the analysis to the employer for use in improving the working environment.

**Running the program.** Before starting, the employer states a policy and has the health committee deliberate on matters such as how the purpose is made known, who implements the check, how it is carried out, how information is handled and stored, how results are used and how complaints are dealt with; the implementation of checks and interview guidance is afterwards reviewed for improvements.
