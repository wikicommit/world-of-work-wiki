In the [[Dataset/abs-time-use-survey]], supervisory care of children is time when a person was in the same location as one or more children under 15 and reported having the child in their care — that is, being available to provide unpaid help if required. Respondents also report the main activity they were doing at the same time, so supervisory care is measured separately from actively looking after children, which is part of [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]].

## Usage

In 2024, just under a quarter of people (23%) provided supervisory care of children while doing another activity, for an average of 7 hours 8 minutes a day; females were more likely to do so than males (25% compared with 21%). Nearly as many people (22%) supervised a child while doing unpaid work activities, for an average of 2 hours 49 minutes. Nine percent supervised a child while doing paid work, for an average of 5 hours 41 minutes, with females again more likely to report this than males (10% compared with 8%).
