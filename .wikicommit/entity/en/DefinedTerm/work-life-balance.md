Work–life balance is an individual's perception that their work and non-work activities are compatible and promote growth in accordance with their current life priorities — a definition from Kalliath and Brough that Australia's Productivity Commission adopts. Work–family balance is one component of it.

## Usage

The term is central to debates about [[DefinedTerm/working-from-home]]. In [[Report/working-from-home-research-paper]], the Productivity Commission sets out two opposing mechanisms. Time saved by not commuting, and the flexibility to combine paid work with other tasks, can improve balance. But removing the physical separation between workplace and home can make it harder to switch off mentally, leading people to work longer than planned or outside their usual hours — a problem sometimes called "blurred boundaries", and one that motivates calls for a [[DefinedTerm/right-to-disconnect]].

Using 2019 data from the [[Dataset/hilda-survey]], the Commission found that employees who worked from home reported higher satisfaction with their flexibility to balance work and non-work commitments than those who did not, while average hours worked were not materially different between the two groups. It notes, however, that perceptions vary widely: many people say working from home improves their balance, while others report that maintaining it is the main difficulty they face when working at home. Research it cites suggests that households struggle more when working from home is forced rather than offered, and that women experience greater work–family conflict because of an inability to disengage from work, whereas men often struggle with integrating work into the family domain.

As a separate aspect of wellbeing, the Commission also examined whether working from home changed how [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]] is shared between partners. It found that working from home was not associated with a fairer division of household or childcare tasks: working men were likely to be rated by their partners as doing less than their fair share regardless of whether they worked from home.
