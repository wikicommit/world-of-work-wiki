The Hierarchy of Controls Applied to NIOSH Total Worker Health is a model for prioritizing interventions that protect safety, health, and well-being. In [[Report/fundamentals-of-total-worker-health-approaches]], NIOSH presents it as a companion to the traditional occupational safety and health hierarchy, extending its emphasis on prevention to organizational conditions and worker well-being.

## Usage

The model orders strategies from those expected to be more protective to those expected to be less protective: eliminate conditions that threaten safety, health, or well-being; substitute safer and more health-supporting policies and practices; redesign work environments; educate workers about safety and health; and encourage personal change. It puts changes to working conditions before efforts centered on individual behavior.

The workbook gives examples involving repetitive movement, force, and awkward posture, as well as work-related stress. For stress, its organizational-level example includes more worker control over work and schedules and opportunities to identify root causes; supervisor training and individual stress-reduction support follow at lower levels.

## When It Applies

NIOSH uses the model when choosing among interventions for a worker health problem. It assumes that recognized hazards and harmful working conditions can be identified and addressed, with progress assessed over time. Treating education or personal change as a substitute for feasible changes to harmful conditions would reverse the model's priority. NIOSH presents this applied hierarchy as a conceptual model, not as a replacement for the traditional hierarchy of controls.
