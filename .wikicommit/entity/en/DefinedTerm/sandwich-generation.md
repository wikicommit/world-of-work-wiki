The sandwich generation is made up of those caring for elderly relatives and young children at the same time. The U.S. Council of Economic Advisers uses the term for workers who combine [[DefinedTerm/eldercare]] with raising children and who, it says, face special challenges in balancing work and family.

## Usage

In [[Report/nine-facts-about-american-families-and-work]] (2014), the Council of Economic Advisers presents the sandwich generation as part of its argument that caregiving does not end when children are grown and that eldercare is a growing responsibility of workers. Drawing on the [[Dataset/american-time-use-survey]] for 2011 and 2012, it reports that one-fifth of people providing unpaid eldercare are also caring for young children, and that parents who provide eldercare have even higher rates of employment than eldercare providers in general: 78 percent are employed and 62 percent work full time, against 63 percent of all unpaid eldercare providers having jobs. It also notes that the share of households with both children under 18 and adults over 64 had been rising for the past 30 years.
