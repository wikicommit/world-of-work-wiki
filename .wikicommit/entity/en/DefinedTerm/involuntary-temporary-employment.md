Involuntary temporary employment is [[DefinedTerm/temporary-employment]] that a worker holds because they looked for a permanent job and did not find one. In the indicator published by Germany's Federal Statistical Office (Destatis), it is the share of employees aged 25 and over who are on a fixed-term contract for that reason, among all employees aged 25 and over with valid answers on the question; respondents who say they sought a permanent position but did not find one count as "involuntarily" temporarily employed.

## Usage

Destatis publishes the indicator among its indicators of the quality of work, noting that fixed-term contracts make longer-term career and personal planning harder. It is measured in the Labour Force Survey (Arbeitskräfteerhebung) integrated in the Mikrozensus, which asks why a contract is fixed-term. Employees can have fixed-term contracts for many reasons — apprenticeship and probationary contracts are automatically time-limited — so the indicator isolates only those who could not find a permanent post.

In 2025, 8.2% of employees aged 25 and over said they had entered a fixed-term job because they had not found a permanent one: 7.7% of women and 8.8% of men, and 16.6% among unskilled assistant workers (Hilfskräfte). Among the other reasons given, 12.9% named a probationary contract, 16.9% were in training, 2.7% had deliberately chosen fixed-term work, 28.0% gave other reasons, and the most common answer (31.4%) was that the job had only been advertised as fixed-term — a group for whom Destatis cannot tell whether the fixed term was involuntary or accepted for the sake of an interesting job.

The measure is sensitive to how the question is asked. In 2021 two answer categories were added ("the job was only advertised as fixed-term" and "other reasons"), which shifted the distribution of answers so strongly that results from 2021 onwards show much smaller shares of involuntary temporary employment and are not comparable with earlier years. The Mikrozensus itself was redesigned in 2020, so results from 2020 onwards are only partly comparable with earlier years, and figures from 2021 have been revised to population benchmarks from the 2022 census.
