Long-term unemployment is unemployment lasting 12 months or more. In Eurostat's EU indicator, the long-term unemployment rate is the share of the labour force (employed and unemployed people) aged 15 to 74 who have been unemployed for 12 months or more. Unemployed persons are those who were without work during the reference week, were available for work, and had either actively sought work in the past four weeks or already found a job starting within the next three months; the duration of unemployment is the length of the job search, or the time since the last job if that is shorter. The data come from the [[Dataset/eu-labour-force-survey]].

## Usage

Eurostat uses the rate as one of the main indicators for monitoring the EU's progress towards Sustainable Development Goal 8 on [[DefinedTerm/decent-work]] and economic growth. It notes that long-term unemployment usually follows trends in overall unemployment (see [[DefinedTerm/unemployment-rate]]) with a delay, and that being unemployed for a year or more can have long-lasting negative effects on individuals and society: it reduces employability, contributes to the depreciation of human capital, endangers social cohesion and raises the risk of poverty and social exclusion, and it can also lead to a deterioration of skills and health that hinders future employability, productivity and earnings.

In the EU, long-term unemployment has declined since 2014. It reached its lowest recorded value, 1.9%, in 2024 and stayed there in 2025, 0.8 percentage points below its 2019 level of 2.7%. The long-term unemployed made up 31.5% of all unemployed people in 2025, 7.9 percentage points less than in 2019. At EU level, the Council Recommendation on the integration of the long-term unemployed into the labour market aims to help them re-enter work.
