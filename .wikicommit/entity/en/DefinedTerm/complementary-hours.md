Complementary hours (heures complémentaires) are, in French labour law, the hours a part-time employee works beyond the weekly or monthly working time set in their employment contract. According to the French administration's Service Public information page, they are not [[DefinedTerm/overtime]]: overtime applies to hours beyond the statutory full-time duration, whereas complementary hours are measured against the part-time employee's own contractual hours.

## Usage

Complementary hours may be worked up to one tenth of the weekly or monthly working time in the contract — for a 30-hour weekly contract, at most 3 hours — and a collective agreement can raise that limit to one third. Every complementary hour carries a pay premium, which may be set by collective agreement; otherwise it is 10% for each hour within the one-tenth limit and 25% for each hour beyond it, up to the one-third limit.

The employee may refuse complementary hours if informed less than 3 days before they are scheduled, or if they would exceed the limits set in the employment contract, and such a refusal is neither misconduct nor a ground for dismissal. If the employee was informed at least 3 days in advance and the hours stay within the contractual limits, refusing is misconduct that can justify a disciplinary sanction or, depending on the circumstances, dismissal for misconduct.
