The Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) Model is a model of job stress which assumes that any job demand (such as workload or downsizing) and any job resource (such as balance or psychological support) may affect employee health and well-being through two basic psychological processes. In the first, a "stress process", excessive job demands and deficient job resources may lead, via burnout, to negative outcomes for the employee and the organization, such as health symptoms and poor job performance. In the second, a "motivational process", abundant job resources may lead, via engagement, to positive outcomes such as [[DefinedTerm/job-satisfaction]] and superior job performance. Burnout and engagement are thus the mediators of the two processes.

## Usage

Statistics Canada researchers describe the JD-R Model as one of the leading job stress models, and as particularly well suited to examining how the psychosocial factors of the [[DefinedTerm/national-standard-for-psychological-health-and-safety-in-the-workplace]] — all of which count as job resources in this framework — influence psychological health and safety. Their study [[ScholarlyArticle/measuring-workplace-psychosocial-factors-in-the-federal-government]] used it to frame an analysis of which of those factors predict job satisfaction among federal public servants. Earlier work they cite applied a JD-R lens to a theoretical model linking the 13 factors, burnout, engagement, psychological distress and turnover intentions, to guide research and workplace interventions.
