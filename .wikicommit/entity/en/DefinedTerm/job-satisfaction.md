In the definition adopted by [[ScholarlyArticle/measuring-workplace-psychosocial-factors-in-the-federal-government]], job satisfaction is a pleasurable or positive emotional state resulting from the appraisal of one's job or job experiences. The article describes it as one of the most important and widely researched outcomes in organizational psychology, and in the [[DefinedTerm/job-demands-resources-model]] it is one of the positive outcomes that abundant job resources are expected to produce through engagement.

## Usage

Meta-analyses summarized in [[ScholarlyArticle/measuring-workplace-psychosocial-factors-in-the-federal-government]] report moderate-to-strong correlations between abundant job resources — such as autonomy, clarity and positive leadership — and high job satisfaction, and small-to-moderate correlations between job satisfaction and correlates or presumed consequences such as health symptoms and job performance.

That study measured job satisfaction with a single overall item, citing a review of the appropriateness of single-item measures: the [[Dataset/public-service-employee-survey]] item "Overall, I like my job", rated on a five-point scale. In the 2017 and 2019 surveys, all of the workplace psychosocial factors of the [[DefinedTerm/national-standard-for-psychological-health-and-safety-in-the-workplace]] that could be measured were significantly related to this item, but recognition and reward and involvement and influence showed the strongest, moderate associations; the authors treat this as preliminary and call for replication with a fuller measure of job satisfaction before recommending interventions.
