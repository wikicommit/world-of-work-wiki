Total Worker Health (TWH) is a NIOSH approach to policies, programs, and practices that joins protection from work-related hazards with efforts to prevent injury and illness and advance worker well-being. It begins with a safe work environment and considers how work design, organizational practices, and psychosocial conditions shape health. NIOSH describes this approach in [[Report/fundamentals-of-total-worker-health-approaches]].

## Usage

The workbook treats wages, work hours, workload and stress, coworker interactions, leave, and the work environment as conditions that can affect workers and their families. Its examples of intervention areas include hazard control, organization of work, compensation and benefits, the built environment, and work-life integration. TWH therefore looks beyond a workplace program focused only on individual health behavior.

NIOSH traces the program from the Steps to a Healthier U.S. Workforce initiative through the WorkLife Initiative to the TWH program in 2011. The workbook organizes implementation around five [[DefinedTerm/defining-elements-of-total-worker-health]] and uses an adapted [[DefinedTerm/total-worker-health-hierarchy-of-controls]] to prioritize changes to working conditions.

## When It Applies

The approach applies when an organization wants to address worker safety, health, and well-being together. The workbook assumes that hazard protection remains foundational, that workers can participate in identifying problems and shaping solutions, and that leadership provides resources and accountability. An initiative centered on personal behavior while leaving harmful work conditions in place would miss that priority. The approach is presented by NIOSH in a workbook that builds on its earlier integrated workplace-program guidance.
