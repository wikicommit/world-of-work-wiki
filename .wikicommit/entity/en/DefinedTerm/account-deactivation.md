In platform work, account deactivation is the blocking or restriction, by a [[DefinedTerm/digital-labour-platform]], of a worker's access to the platform — making the worker ineligible to accept work through it. Seattle's App-Based Worker Deactivation Rights Ordinance, as quoted in the International Labour Office's report [[Report/realizing-decent-work-in-the-platform-economy-2024]] (2024), defines it as "the blocking of an app-based worker's access to the worker platform, changing an app-based worker's status from eligible to accept offers to perform services to ineligible, or other material restriction in access to the worker platform that is effected by a network company". The report describes deactivation as central to concerns about how platform work relationships end: in practice, the termination of a [[DefinedTerm/digital-platform-worker]]'s contract is in many cases the act of disconnecting or deactivating their account.

## Usage

**Why it is an issue.** For platform workers classified as employees, termination follows labour legislation; under commercial or civil contracts, it follows the terms of the platform's service agreement and is not ordinarily subject to labour law. The report notes that, unlike labour laws on termination, which set boundaries such as valid reasons, advance notice or compensation, most platform contracts have no such requirements and require no human oversight. Studies it cites document platforms automatically deactivating accounts on the basis of low customer ratings, and for reasons such as rejecting too many orders, violating the terms of the service agreement, long periods of inactivity or not being available at certain times; the absence of notice, reasons and human oversight has also been observed. Because the parameters of algorithmic and rating-based decisions are often not disclosed, workers find it harder to substantiate legal claims (see [[DefinedTerm/algorithmic-management]]), and challenging a decision may mean litigating in a foreign jurisdiction, while a worker who is no longer legally entitled to work in a country may be unable to stay there to pursue a claim. In international labour standards, the Termination of Employment Convention, 1982 (No. 158), does not extend to self-employed workers, and the issue of penalties such as the suspension or termination of a worker's account with a platform is not comprehensively addressed.

**Regulatory responses.** The report finds that platform regulations dealing with termination focus mainly on deactivation:

- **United States.** Seattle's ordinance sets the conditions under which deactivation is possible, gives workers the right to challenge it, specifies what the notice of deactivation must include and requires that workers be given access to the records substantiating it. Washington State prohibits passenger transport platforms from enforcing any policy that treats paid sick time as time off the platform that may lead to deactivation.
- **Italy** prohibits deactivating a worker, or reducing their opportunities, because they did not accept work.
- **France** prohibits platforms from suspending or deactivating accounts because workers exercised their freedom to choose periods of activity or inactivity, their work materials or their itinerary, or to work for more than one platform at once; non-acceptance of work cannot be a reason for deactivation or suspension. Collective agreements add guarantees: a 2023 agreement in the delivery sector obliges platforms to inform workers of their deactivation procedures and requires human control over decisions to terminate contracts, and a 2023 agreement for chauffeured passenger transport (VTC) contains similar guarantees plus compensation for drivers whose accounts have been unfairly suspended.
- **Chile** requires platform contracts to state the grounds, form of communication, notice periods and mechanisms for termination, and requires platforms to give at least 30 days' written notice of termination to independent workers who have provided services for at least six months, except for serious non-compliance by the worker.
- **Malta** entitles workers to a written communication, with reasons, before being deactivated, unless the deactivation is for non-compliance by the worker.
- **Kenya** requires transport network companies to have a deactivation policy and, before deactivating a vehicle owner, to give adequate written notice, an opportunity to respond and written reasons.

The questionnaire appended to the report asked governments whether a new standard should prohibit suspending or deactivating a platform worker's account, or ending their work relationship, on "discriminatory, arbitrary or otherwise unjustified grounds", and whether workers should have effective access, without undue delay, to a human review of any algorithmic decision affecting their working conditions, in particular one resulting in suspension, deactivation or termination.
