The statutory pension age is the legal age at retirement set by a country's pension system, at which a person becomes eligible for a statutory old-age pension. Most EU countries also allow early retirement, typically one to five years before the legal age, which is often subject to a reduction in monthly pension payments, and deferred retirement beyond the legal age, which may result in a bonus. The average age at which people actually first receive an old-age pension may therefore differ from the statutory age, because of early retirement, flexible pension ages and pension reforms.

## Usage

Over the past 10–15 years many EU countries have reformed their pensions, with two main trends in the statutory pension age: gradual increases and the narrowing of differences between men and women. Between 2012 and 2023, 21 countries raised their statutory pension age. The lowest statutory age for men rose from 60 years (France) in 2012 to 62 years (France) in 2023, and for women from 59 years and 4 months (Romania) to 60 years (Austria and Poland). The number of countries with different statutory ages for men and women fell from 11 in 2012 to 6 in 2023, some of which are still in a transition period towards equal ages.

Eurostat attributes the roughly five-year later [[DefinedTerm/transition-from-work-to-retirement]] between 2012 and 2023 mainly to this increase in the eligible pension age. Countries with relatively low pension ages, such as Poland and Slovakia, tend to have higher shares of pension recipients, whereas in Denmark the high pension age means that only one fifth of people aged 60–64 receive a pension.

In 2023, 78.2% of statutory old-age pension recipients in the EU received their pension without reduction or bonus, 19.4% took early retirement with a reduced pension, and 2.4% deferred their pension in return for a bonus. Early retirement exceeded a third of recipients in Cyprus, Portugal and Belgium, while deferment was most common in Czechia, Denmark and Finland. Ireland, the Netherlands and Sweden have no system of early or deferred retirement. People who retired with a reduction were younger at first receipt, and those with a bonus older, than those who received neither.
