Weekend work is work done on a Saturday or a Sunday. In the Insee Références study [[Report/atypical-working-hours-women-less-qualified-and-foreign-born-most-exposed]] (2025), it is measured over the year from two questions of the [[Dataset/french-working-conditions-survey]] — how many Saturdays, and how many Sundays, a year the respondent works at their workplace — so work taken home is excluded. The study concentrates on *regular* weekend work, defined as at least 11 Saturdays and/or 11 Sundays a year, or at least about one weekend day a month once annual leave is taken into account; people who work at least 11 Saturdays and also at least 11 Sundays are counted as working both weekend days. Saturdays and Sundays worked are the "atypical days" in its definition of [[DefinedTerm/atypical-working-hours]], alongside atypical usual hours.

## Usage

In metropolitan France in 2019, 30% of employees worked regularly at weekends (32% of women, 28% of men), and 43% worked at least two weekend days a year. Regular Saturday work (29% of employees) was almost twice as common as regular Sunday work (15%). The two overlapped heavily: 48% of regular Saturday workers also worked Sundays, and 95% of regular Sunday workers also worked Saturdays, so that people rarely worked on Sundays only. Weekend work also went with atypical usual hours: 63% of regular Saturday workers and 77% of regular Sunday workers had atypical hours, against 36% of all employees, and night workers were the most likely to work weekends too (72% on Saturdays, 69% on Sundays). By contrast, only 17% of employees on standard hours worked regularly on Saturdays and 6% on Sundays.

Exposure depends strongly on occupation, even at otherwise comparable characteristics. Retail employees had by far the highest probability of working on Saturdays only (51% for women and 55% for men), and fewer than a third of them did not work regularly at weekends. Among women, 60% of regular Saturday workers were clerical and service employees, retail employees alone making up 15%; Sunday work was likewise done mainly by employees (64%), but more often civil-service employees (29%) and personal-services staff (24%). Among men, Saturday and Sunday workers were more alike, with manual workers making up a little over a third, as in the male workforce as a whole, while police officers and the military were over-represented (9% of Saturday and 14% of Sunday workers).
