A minijob is the common name in Germany for marginally paid employment (geringfügig entlohnte Beschäftigung), a form of atypical employment. German employment statistics distinguish it from employment subject to social insurance contributions (sozialversicherungspflichtige Beschäftigung), and a person may hold a minijob as their only employment or as a second job alongside employment subject to social insurance.

## Usage

The IAB report [[Report/employment-and-living-situations-of-over-60s]] describes minijobs as a particularly common form of employment for older workers and pension recipients. Before the statutory retirement age they make up a significant share of older people's paid work, and after entry into the old-age pension they are, alongside self-employment, almost the only form of employment.

The report also sets out why minijobs were exposed during the COVID-19 pandemic. The risk of becoming unemployed is generally higher in marginal employment than in employment subject to social insurance, and marginal employment was not protected by short-time work allowance (Kurzarbeitergeld). The contact restrictions of the first lockdown closed whole sectors, such as hospitality and retail, in which minijobs are disproportionately common, so the fall in labour demand hit marginally paid employment especially hard. The work involved — often found in agriculture, food production, retail, catering, building cleaning or private households — also meant that people in minijobs were the least likely to be able to work from home, in all the age groups the report examined. According to the report, a slight decline in minijobs had already been observed before the pandemic.
