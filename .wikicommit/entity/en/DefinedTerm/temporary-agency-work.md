Temporary agency work (Swedish *bemanningsanställning*) is a way of organising work in which the worker is employed by a staffing agency while the work itself is carried out at a client organisation that hires the worker's labour. The arrangement is described as a triangular employment relationship: the worker has an employment relationship with the agency and a working relationship with the client, and the agency and the client have a business relationship with each other. Employment terms and pay are set by the agency, while the client directs and allocates the daily work. In Sweden agency workers can be permanently employed by the agency or employed on a more temporary basis, and the arrangement can be flexible in both time and place, since the worker may work at several workplaces.

## Usage

The Swedish knowledge compilation [[Report/organisation-of-work-and-its-significance-for-health-and-well-being]] reviews research on agency work's consequences for health and well-being. The studies it summarises report that agency workers experience more [[DefinedTerm/job-insecurity]] than the client's own employees and have less access to competence development and learning, because the client trains them only as far as the tasks require and the agency avoids training that might jeopardise its business relationship with the client. A Swedish survey of just under 500 agency workers found that agency blue-collar workers ran twice the risk of work-related well-being problems of agency white-collar workers, that agency workers who found their work more physically demanding than the client's employees ran three times the risk of work-related injury, and that they did not receive the same safety and health introduction as the client's employees. The same study suggested that Swedish agency workers have better conditions than in some other countries because they are paid between assignments. Finnish research found agency workers' risk of workplace accidents to be rising and higher than in the other industries studied, although accidents involving agency workers were seldom serious. Studies also report that agency workers may see themselves as visitors rather than members of the work group, with weaker social relations and trust.

The compilation advises employers that use agency workers to have concrete strategies for including them in competence development and to ensure that social relations function, and calls on employers and other actors to address the use of fixed-term and agency employment, which it describes as generally worse for individuals' health and well-being.
