A simultaneous activity, in time-use measurement, is an activity performed at the same time as the main or "primary" activity a respondent records — for example, folding laundry while supervising children and watching television. In the General Social Survey on Time Use, questions on simultaneous activities allow a better understanding of multitasking, which [[Report/time-use-total-work-burden-unpaid-work-and-leisure]] defines, drawing on earlier research, as "the simultaneous performance of several tasks or the rapid alteration between them that allows individuals to squeeze in more tasks and get more things done within a limited amount of time".

## Usage

Paid work is generally done as a primary activity, whereas housework and child care are often done as simultaneous activities, typically alongside leisure. Estimates based on primary activities alone may therefore systematically understate the time spent on [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]], and because women are more likely than men both to do unpaid work and to multitask, they may understate women's [[DefinedTerm/total-work-time]] and give a distorted picture of progress toward gender equality. The General Social Survey on Time Use (the [[Dataset/canadian-time-use-survey]]) first asked in 2010 about participation in, and the duration of, activities done at the same time as the primary activity; the 2015 cycle kept the participation questions but dropped the duration questions.

In Canada, counting simultaneous activities changes the gender comparison markedly. Women and men aged 25 to 54 had an equivalent total work burden in 2015 when only primary activities were counted (7.8 and 7.6 hours a day), but when unpaid work done as a simultaneous activity was added, women's was 1.2 hours a day greater than men's in 2010 (9.1 versus 7.9 hours). In 2015, 60.8% of women did unpaid work as a simultaneous activity, against 40.2% of men, and nearly 20% of women did housework or caregiving while engaged in leisure, against 6.0% of men.
