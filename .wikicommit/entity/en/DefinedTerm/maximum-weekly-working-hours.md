Maximum weekly working hours is a legal limit on how many hours a person may work in a week, measured as an average over a reference period rather than week by week. In the UK, according to the government's GOV.UK guidance, a worker cannot work more than 48 hours a week on average, normally averaged over 17 weeks; the guidance notes that this law is sometimes called the "working time directive" or "working time regulations". Workers under 18 face a stricter limit and cannot work more than 8 hours a day or 40 hours a week.

## Usage

The UK limit is not absolute. An individual worker can choose to work more by opting out of the 48-hour week, and people in certain jobs may have to work more than 48 hours a week on average: jobs where 24-hour staffing is required; the armed forces, emergency services and police; security and surveillance; domestic service in a private household; seafarers, sea-fishermen and workers on vessels on inland waterways; and jobs where working time is not measured and the worker is in control of it, such as a managing executive with control over their own decisions.

Employers need to keep some records of working hours, and the Advisory, Conciliation and Arbitration Service (Acas) provides further advice on working hours and on the records employers must keep.
