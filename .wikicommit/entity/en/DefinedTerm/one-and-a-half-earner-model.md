A one-and-a-half earner couple (Dutch *anderhalfverdieners*) is a couple in which one partner works full time and the other works part time. Statistics Netherlands (CBS) further speaks of "large" one-and-a-half earners when the woman has a part-time job of 20 to 35 hours a week.

## Usage

CBS uses the term in its Emancipation Monitor to describe the working patterns of couples in the Netherlands. In the 2024 edition ([[Report/working-and-caring-emancipation-monitor-2024]]), the combination of a full-time working man and a part-time working woman was the most common pattern among couples with children under 18 (55%, among man–woman couples in which both partners are aged 25 to 50), and among such couples it increasingly took the "large" form. Both partners worked full time in 13% of couples with minor children, against 44% of couples without minor children. Just over half (53%) of mothers with a partner and children under 18 worked 20 to 35 hours a week, while the great majority of fathers worked full time.

The pattern sits alongside a gap between wishes and practice: half of parents with a partner and a child under 18 would prefer to share paid work and childcare equally, but 9% say they do, while 48% say the man works more and the woman does more of the care. CBS links mothers' shorter working weeks to the fall in their income from work after the birth of a first child (the [[DefinedTerm/child-penalty]]).
