Disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) for an illness or health condition are calculated as the sum of the years of life lost due to premature mortality in the population and the years lived with disability (YLD) by people living with the condition or its consequences. They express the gap between current health status and an ideal situation in which individuals live into old age free of disease and disability, using a set value for life expectancy to estimate the years lost to premature death or disability. DALYs thus reflect the effect of diseases on the general population in terms of both quality of life and death, and weigh more heavily on young adults and newborns.

## Usage

In occupational safety and health, DALYs are used to compare the burden of different work-related injuries and illnesses. The EU-OSHA report [[Report/work-related-msds-prevalence-costs-and-demographics-in-the-eu]] draws on EU-OSHA estimates of the costs of work-related injury and illness, based on a project by the ILO, the Finnish Ministry of Social Affairs and Health, the Finnish Institute of Occupational Health, Singapore's WSH Institute, the International Commission on Occupational Health and EU-OSHA that relied mainly on WHO and ILO data. In those estimates for the EU-28 in 2017, cancer accounted for 25 % of DALYs per 100,000 workers lost to work-related illness and [[DefinedTerm/work-related-musculoskeletal-disorders]] for about 15 %. The report notes that DALYs do not express economic values directly, although their impact on productivity can be linked to economic loss.
