Grandparents raising grandchildren are, in the U.S. American Community Survey (ACS), grandparents who are currently responsible for most of the basic needs of a grandchild under 18 who lives with them. The measure comes from survey items rather than time diaries, and it does not indicate whether the grandparent has legal custody. Other family members and friends also raise children whose parents cannot, but the data cover only grandparents.

## Usage

According to [[Report/older-women-and-unpaid-caregiving-in-the-us]], 2.33 million children in the United States — 3.2% of all children under 18 — were being raised by a grandmother in 2021, and 1.32 million women were raising at least one grandchild, against 757,000 men. The arrangement was usually long-term: 84.7% of these grandmothers had been responsible for a grandchild for at least a year and 48.8% for five years or more. Some 46.0% were also employed, combining paid work with raising a minor child, and 21.8% lived in poverty, compared with 13.2% of grandfathers raising grandchildren. About 51.2% of these grandmothers were white, 21.7% Black, 4.4% Asian and Pacific Islander, and 21.5% Hispanic.

The share of children raised by a grandmother varied markedly by state, from 6.4% in West Virginia and New Mexico and 5.8% in Mississippi to about 1.3% in Vermont. The brief notes several interrelated factors that could explain this: many experts point to the opioid epidemic, and financial hardship, maternal mortality, single parenthood and military families may each increase the chance of children being raised by other relatives.
