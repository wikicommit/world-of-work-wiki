Effort–reward imbalance is the situation in which workers do not perceive the level of rewards from their work — comprising earnings, recognition and career advancement — as proportional to the efforts they devote to it. Eurofound describes it as having important detrimental implications for health, including stress and an increased risk of physical and mental health problems, and treats it as part of whether pay is perceived to be fair, alongside the absolute level of earnings.

## Usage

In the [[Dataset/european-working-conditions-survey]], effort–reward imbalance is examined within the earnings dimension of [[DefinedTerm/job-quality]], through the share of workers who disagree that they feel they get paid appropriately. That share has been decreasing for both women and men since 2005, but a gender gap persists: in 2024, 30 % of female workers and 24 % of male workers reported an effort–reward imbalance.
