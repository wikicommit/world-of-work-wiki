The unemployment rate is the number of unemployed persons as a percentage of the labour force, the labour force (formerly called the active population) being all employed and unemployed persons. In EU statistics an unemployed person is defined by Eurostat, according to the guidelines of the International Labour Organization, as someone aged 15 to 74 who was not employed during the reference week, is currently available for work, and is either actively seeking work or has found a job to start within at most three months. An employed person, by contrast, is someone aged 15–89 who did any work for pay, profit or family gain during the reference week — even for just one hour — or who had a job or business from which they were temporarily absent.

## Usage

The youth unemployment rate applies the same calculation to a narrower age band; in Eurostat's migrant integration statistics it covers people aged 15–29, the unemployed in that age group as a percentage of the labour force of the same age. Unlike the labour force participation rate and the employment rate, which are expressed as shares of the total population, the unemployment rate takes the labour force as its denominator.

A long-term unemployed person is someone out of work who has been actively seeking employment for at least a year, the unemployment period being the duration of the job search or the time since the last job was held, whichever is shorter.

In Eurostat's migrant integration statistics, unemployment rates come from the [[Dataset/eu-labour-force-survey]] and are one of three indicators used to compare citizenship and migration-status groups in [[DefinedTerm/labour-market-integration-of-migrants]]. For people aged 20–64, the rate fell continuously from 2013 for nationals, citizens of other EU countries and non-EU citizens alike, apart from a temporary increase in 2020, and by 2025 stood below its pre-pandemic level for all three groups.
