The halo around unemployment (*halo autour du chômage*) is a measure used by Insee, the French national statistics institute, for people without a job who either are looking for one but are not available to start work within two weeks, or have not taken any active step to find a job in the previous month but want to work, whether or not they are available. These people want to work but do not satisfy all the criteria of job search or availability needed to be counted as unemployed in the sense of the International Labour Office (ILO), under which an unemployed person is without a job in a given week, available to start one within two weeks, and has actively looked for one in the past four weeks or found one starting within three months.

## Usage

Insee reports the halo as a share of the population aged 15 to 64, alongside the share of unemployment (the number of unemployed people divided by the whole of the corresponding population, as distinct from the [[DefinedTerm/unemployment-rate]], which divides by the active population). Adding the two gives the share of people who are without a job and want to work. In [[Report/snapshot-of-the-labour-market-in-2024]], measured with the Labour Force Survey (enquête Emploi), the halo covered 4.5% of 15-64-year-olds in 2024, down 0.2 point on 2023 and back at its 2022 level, after jumping to 5.4% in 2020 under the effect of the health crisis; unemployment and the halo together covered 10.1% of 15-64-year-olds.

Women were more often in the halo than men in 2024 (5.1% against 4.0%) although they were less often unemployed (5.3% against 5.9%). Among 15-24-year-olds the halo reached 7.3%, 1.4 points above 2019, an increase carried entirely by young people still in education, which Insee links, with caution, to a now more frequent wish among them to find a work-study job.
