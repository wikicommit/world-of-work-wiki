Migration status is a statistical classification of individuals according to their country of birth and, where relevant, their parents' country of birth. People are first grouped as native-born (born in the reporting country) or foreign-born (born abroad); native-born people are then further classified by their parents' birthplace to reflect a possible migrant background. Eurostat's migrant integration statistics therefore use four categories: native-born with two native-born parents, native-born with one foreign-born parent, native-born with two foreign-born parents, and foreign-born people.

## Usage

Migration status is used alongside a classification by citizenship, which groups people as nationals of the country where they live, citizens of another EU country, or citizens of a non-EU country.

In Eurostat's labour market indicators for 2025 on [[DefinedTerm/labour-market-integration-of-migrants]], drawn from the [[Dataset/eu-labour-force-survey]], native-born people with two native-born parents had the highest employment rates and generally the lowest unemployment rates in most countries, while foreign-born people and native-born people with two foreign-born parents generally had lower employment and often the highest unemployment. Native-born people with one foreign-born parent followed patterns similar to those with two native-born parents in labour force participation.
