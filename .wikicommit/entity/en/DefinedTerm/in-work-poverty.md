In-work poverty refers to people who are in work and yet poor — the so-called "working poor". Poverty is often associated with the absence of paid work, but low wages can also push some workers below the poverty line. Eurostat tracks the share of the working poor aged 18 and over, and treats fair income and social protection, alongside safety at work, as important components of [[DefinedTerm/decent-work]].

## Usage

Eurostat uses in-work poverty to monitor the decent-work strand of the EU's progress towards Sustainable Development Goal 8. It reports that people working part time or on temporary contracts, self-employed workers, low-skilled workers and non-EU-born workers are especially affected. In the EU the share of the working poor peaked in 2016 and has fallen since; by 2024 it had dropped to 8.2%, the lowest level recorded since monitoring began in 2010.

The EU Directive on adequate minimum wages seeks to enhance the adequacy of the [[DefinedTerm/minimum-wage]], helping to improve living and working conditions while reducing in-work poverty.
