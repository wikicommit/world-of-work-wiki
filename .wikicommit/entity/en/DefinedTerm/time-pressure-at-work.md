Time pressure at work is the experience of having to carry out one's work under pressure of time. In the 2019 ad hoc module of the [[Dataset/eu-labour-force-survey]] on work organisation and working time arrangements, Eurostat measures it by how often employed people work under time pressure in their main job — always, often, sometimes or never.

## Usage

In 2019 most employed people in the EU worked under time pressure only sometimes (41.6%) or never (22.2%), while 11.0% always and 25.2% often did. The shares differed widely between countries: Malta had the highest share always working under time pressure (20.9%) and Slovakia the lowest (4.3%); Czechia had the highest share often doing so (38.5%) and Spain the lowest (15.4%), while Spain also had the highest share never experiencing time pressure (38.7%) and Finland the lowest (7.7%).

According to Eurostat, time pressure hardly differs between men and women, and middle-aged workers (35–49) report the most. It differs more by education and professional status: 11.8% of people with a high level of education always and 28.8% often worked under time pressure, against 8.8% and 18.0% of those with a low level, and self-employed people with employees (18.1% always, 33.6% often) did so more than those without employees (9.0% and 22.7%) or employees (10.9% and 25.2%). Workers with atypical working schedules — shifts, evening or night work, or weekend work — were more often under pressure than those with a normal schedule: 40.9% against 33.3% always or often. By activity, people in financial and insurance activities experienced it most (12.8% always, 31.4% often) and those in agriculture, forestry and fishing least; by occupation, managers came top (16.7% always, 34.0% often), while skilled agricultural, forestry and fishery workers and people in elementary occupations experienced it least.

Eurostat also relates time pressure to working hours. In 2019 employed people in the EU usually worked 37.2 hours a week, but those who always or often worked under time pressure and had been contacted several times in their leisure time by their employer or clients, with action required before the next working day, worked 43.1 hours on average — under 40 hours in Lithuania, the Netherlands and Norway, and more than 45 hours in Slovakia, Belgium, Portugal, Austria, Czechia, Greece, the United Kingdom, Iceland and Turkey.
