Work–family conflict is interference between the work and family domains, examined in research in two directions: work interfering with family, abbreviated WFC (also called work-to-family conflict), and family interfering with work, abbreviated FWC (family-to-work conflict). The two directions are measured and analysed separately.

## Usage

The concept is used in occupational health research on how combining paid work with family responsibilities affects mental health. In [[ScholarlyArticle/gender-differences-in-work-family-conflict-and-mental-health-of-swedish-workers]], a study of Swedish workers in the SLOSH cohort from 2014 to 2018, within-person increases in both WFC and FWC were associated with increases in depressive symptoms among both men and women, and the impact was greater for those with more intensive childcare responsibilities — for women caring for children more than 10 hours a week, the estimate for WFC was 2.04, against 1.57 for women without childcare.
