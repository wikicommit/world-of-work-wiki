Job quality is the quality of a job assessed across several dimensions of working conditions rather than by any single one: in Eurofound's words, it is multidimensional — "it is not just one aspect that makes a job a good job". Eurofound's job quality framework defines seven dimensions, each measured by a specific index: physical environment, social environment, working time quality, work intensity, skills and discretion, prospects, and earnings.

## Usage

Eurofound has tracked job quality through the [[Dataset/european-working-conditions-survey]] since its first round in 1990, and describes the concept as having evolved over that time: earlier approaches emphasised wages, working time, and health and safety, while the scope later expanded to include autonomy, work intensity, skills use and psychosocial risks. It presents insights into job quality, including trends over time, as serving to shape EU policies such as the Competitiveness Compass and the Quality Jobs Roadmap.

The dimensions cover, respectively: exposure to physical risks and demands; adverse social behaviour and social support from colleagues and managers; the duration of working hours, atypical hours and workers' control over working time; the pace and emotional demands of work; the use and development of skills, training and participation in work organisation; career prospects and job security; and earnings, including their predictability and whether pay is felt to match effort ([[DefinedTerm/effort-reward-imbalance]]). Because each dimension has its own index, trends can diverge — in the 2024 survey, for example, working time quality improved for both men and women while the work intensity index deteriorated for women.
