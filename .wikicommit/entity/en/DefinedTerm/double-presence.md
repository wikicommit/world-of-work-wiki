Double presence (*doble presencia*) is the mental and emotional pressure a working person experiences from needing to be permanently present — even if only mentally — in the domestic sphere while they are at work. It arises from the unequal division of paid and unpaid activities that has traditionally assigned domestic and care work mainly to women and paid work mainly to men, and it is one of the ways this inequality affects workers' health.

## Usage

Argentina's 2018 national survey of working conditions ([[Dataset/ecetss]]) measured double presence with the question "Are there moments when you would need to be at work and at home at the same time?", counting respondents who answered "always" or "many times". On that measure, 25.3% of workers experienced double presence — 30% of women and 21.4% of men. The gender gap was largest among independent workers, and women professionals were the group with the greatest difficulty in combining home and work. The survey treats it as an indicator of difficulty in reconciling personal, family and working life, alongside the unequal distribution of [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]]: employed women were more likely than employed men to do unpaid domestic work and spent more hours a day on it. Results are reported in [[Report/ecetss-2018]].
