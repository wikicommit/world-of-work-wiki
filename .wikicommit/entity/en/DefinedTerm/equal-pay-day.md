Equal Pay Day is a date that European and American countries set to raise public awareness of equal pay between women and men and to encourage efforts to narrow the [[DefinedTerm/gender-pay-gap]]. It expresses the gap as the number of additional days women would have to work to earn the same pay as men. Taiwan's Ministry of Labor calculates it using the method of the European Commission's European Equal Pay Day (EEPD), multiplying 365 days by the gender pay gap, which Taiwan measures on average hourly earnings including overtime and other irregular pay.

## Usage

Taiwan has published its Equal Pay Day since 2012. According to the Ministry of Labor bulletin [[Report/womens-labour-force-participation-in-taiwan-in-recent-years]], the gap of 14.6% in 2018 meant that women needed to work 54 more days (365 × 14.6%) to earn as much as men, so Equal Pay Day 2019 fell on 23 February. As the gap narrowed from 18.6% in 2008, the number of extra days fell from 69 to 54 and the date moved 15 days earlier over the decade; the dates published for 2012 to 2019 ran from 3 March 2012 to 23 February in each of 2017, 2018 and 2019. The series is revised together with the pay-gap figures when the underlying earnings survey is re-benchmarked.
