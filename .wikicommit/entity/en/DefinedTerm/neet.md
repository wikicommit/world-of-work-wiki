Young people neither in employment nor in education or training (NEETs) are those in a given age group who are not employed — they are either unemployed or outside the labour force — and who have not received any formal or non-formal education or training in the four weeks preceding the survey. In the EU the indicator is produced from the [[Dataset/eu-labour-force-survey]]: its numerator counts people meeting both conditions, and its denominator is the total population of the same age group, excluding those who did not answer the question on participation in regular education and training. NEETs are commonly subdivided into the unemployed, who are without work but actively seeking it, and those outside the labour force, who neither have a job nor are looking for one. Eurostat presents the indicator as a complement to employment and unemployment statistics, which describe young people's labour market participation less well because many of them are still in education and another group is disengaged from both education and work.

## Usage

The EU analyses the indicator mainly for people aged 15 to 29, and under the European Pillar of Social Rights has set an EU-level target of reducing their NEET rate to 9 % by 2030. In 2025 the EU rate for this age group was 11.0 %, down 4.2 percentage points from 2015, after a rise at the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020; it ranged from about 5 % in the Netherlands to 19 % in Romania, and nine Member States were already below the 2030 target. Eurostat links the rate closely to economic performance and the business cycle.

The rate rises with age: in 2025 it was 5.3 % among 15-19-year-olds, most of whom are still in education, 12.8 % at 20-24 and 14.7 % at 25-29. It is higher for young people with a low level of education (12.8 %) than for those with a medium (11.0 %) or high level (8.0 %), and higher in rural areas (12.2 %) than in towns and suburbs (11.0 %) or cities (10.3 %). Young women were more likely to be NEET than young men (12.0 % against 9.9 % in 2025), a gap that widens with age to 6.0 percentage points at 25-29. Female NEETs were more often outside the labour force, while male NEETs were more often unemployed. Eurostat suggests that social conventions placing more weight on women's role within the family may help explain the gender gap, and attributes the higher share of young women outside the labour force in part to family structures, as more young women than men may spend time caring for children or other family members.

Eurostat lists factors that may contribute to becoming NEET: a low or medium level of educational attainment, a low household income, a parent who experienced unemployment, being raised by a single parent, living in a rural area, having been born outside the EU and having a disability. It notes that young people who spend a long time as NEETs are often affected by poverty and social exclusion, insecurity, crime and health problems, and places the indicator in the context of a less linear transition from education to work, in which more students also work and more young workers return to education or training.
