A micro-entrepreneur is a French individual entrepreneur who has opted into the regime of the same name, which offers simplified formalities for creating a business and a simplified method of calculating and paying social contributions and income tax. The regime applies, under certain conditions, to individual entrepreneurs who ask for it. It was introduced on 1 January 2009 as the auto-entrepreneur regime and took its present name on 19 December 2014. In French statistics, self-employed people who do not use the regime are referred to as "classic" self-employed (*indépendants classiques*) ([[DefinedTerm/self-employment]]).

## Usage

Insee's statistics on the self-employed separate micro-entrepreneurs from classic self-employed people, a distinction available in the Labour Force Survey from 2014. In [[Report/working-time-of-the-self-employed-2025]], micro-entrepreneurs worked on average 1,421 hours a year in their main job in 2023, against 2,151 hours for classic self-employed people, with fewer days worked (213 against 253) and shorter working days (6.7 hours against 8.5); their usual weekly working time was 32.1 hours against 45.9. Insee notes that micro-entrepreneurs more often combine their self-employed activity with salaried work than classic self-employed people do, which partly explains their shorter working time in their main job. Insee attributes about half of the 18% fall in the self-employed's average annual working time between 2008 and 2023 to the changing composition of self-employment, linked above all to the arrival of the regime and the strong growth in the share of micro-entrepreneurs since then.
