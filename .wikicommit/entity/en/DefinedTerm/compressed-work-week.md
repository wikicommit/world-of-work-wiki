A compressed work week is a work schedule in which the same weekly working hours are achieved by working longer but fewer days. It is a well-established type of work schedule, used for example in the US Federal Government.

## Usage

An editorial in the *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* ([[ScholarlyArticle/importance-of-extended-working-hours-for-work-related-injuries]]) describes compressed work weeks as advantageous in several ways: the lower number of work days gives employees more days without work, and it decreases total commuting time, together with the risks and environmental exposures that commuting brings. It has also been suggested as a means of reducing traffic congestion, although those effects are debated.

The schedule matters for research on working time and safety because long work days do not necessarily imply long weekly working hours. Since a compressed week combines long days with ordinary weekly hours, the editorial argues that the risks of long daily and long weekly hours need to be disentangled by studying specific combinations of working hours.
