Employee voice (or simply "voice") is defined by Detert and Burris as "the discretionary provision of information intended to improve organizational functioning to someone inside an organization with the perceived authority to act, even though such information may challenge and upset the status quo of the organization and its power holders". In working conditions research it describes how far workers have a say in the organisation of their own work.

## Usage

The EU-OSHA report [[Report/work-related-msds-prevalence-costs-and-demographics-in-the-eu]] measures employee voice with a scale built from five questions in the 2015 [[Dataset/european-working-conditions-survey]], focusing on the perceived authority to act on the information: whether workers are consulted before objectives are set for their work, are involved in improving the organisation or its processes, have a say in the choice of their colleagues, are able to apply their own ideas in their work, and can influence decisions that are important for their work (Cronbach's alpha 0.83). The share of workers reporting a lack of employee voice remained stable between 2005 and 2015.

In the report's exploratory regressions, employee voice is one of the organisational and [[DefinedTerm/psychosocial-risks]] factors significantly related to MSDs, but only to those in the upper limbs, and the strength of the relationship is small; it differs between age groups and education levels. The report lists a lack of employee voice among the factors associated with upper-limb [[DefinedTerm/work-related-musculoskeletal-disorders]].
