Karoshi (過労死, "death from overwork") is the Japanese term for death or serious illness brought on by overwork. In Japanese administrative usage the broader expression karōshi-tō (過労死等, "karoshi and related harm") groups two kinds of outcome: cerebrovascular and heart diseases, whose risk long working hours and other excessive work are medically known to raise, and mental disorders, to which the stress of excessive work contributes. When such a disease is recognised as caused by excessive work, it is treated as an occupational injury and covered by workers' accident compensation insurance ([[DefinedTerm/occupational-disease]]).

Since 2014 the term has had a statutory definition. Article 2 of the [[Legislation/act-promoting-measures-to-prevent-death-and-injury-from-overwork]] defines karōshi-tō as death from cerebrovascular or heart disease caused by an excessive workload; death by suicide due to a mental disorder caused by strong psychological stress at work; and those cerebrovascular and heart diseases and mental disorders where they have not led to death.

## Usage

A fiscal year 2015 seminar pamphlet produced under a commission from the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare uses the term to frame the case for preventing overwork ([[DefinedTerm/long-working-hours]]). It presents the chain from illegal long working hours to karoshi, and from there to consequences for the company: recognition as an occupational injury by the Labour Standards Inspection Office, referral for prosecution with criminal penalties and publication of the workplace's name, civil lawsuits over breach of the employer's duty of care for safety with large damages, loss of social credibility and difficulty in recruiting.

**Compensation statistics.** Citing the ministry's figures for fiscal year 2014, the pamphlet reports:

- **Cerebrovascular and heart diseases**: 763 claims and 277 decisions to pay compensation, both down on fiscal 2013 (784 and 306) but still high; 121 of the paid cases were deaths. Payments were most numerous in transport and postal activities, followed by wholesale and retail trade, manufacturing and construction. Of the cases recognised on the grounds of excessive work over a long period, about 90% had averaged 80 hours or more of overtime a month, as in the previous year.
- **Mental disorders**: 1,456 claims and 497 decisions to pay, both the highest on record; 99 of the paid cases involved suicide, including attempted suicide. Payments were most numerous in manufacturing, followed by wholesale and retail trade and transport and postal activities.

**National framework.** The Act on Promotion of Measures to Prevent Karoshi etc. (過労死等防止対策推進法) came into force on 1 November 2014, and on 24 July 2015 the Cabinet adopted an outline of measures under it. The outline sets out priority measures for the state — research and analysis of cases and epidemiological study, awareness-raising (including on reducing long working hours, promoting annual paid leave, mental health care and preventing workplace power harassment), consultation services and training, and support for private organisations' activities — and for employers: top management leading the effort, also in view of karoshi occurring among young workers as well as those in their prime working years, investigating causes and preventing recurrence when a case occurs, and making use of occupational health staff.

**Recognition criteria and prevention policy.** A timeline of overwork-related policy on the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's workers' mental health portal *Kokoro no Mimi* traces how the recognition of such cases developed. The first criteria for recognising diseases of the central nervous and circulatory systems (stroke, acute cardiac death and the like) as work-related were set in February 1961. They were revised in October 1987 as criteria for cerebrovascular and ischaemic heart diseases, partially amended twice in the mid-1990s, fundamentally revised in December 2001 and revised again in August 2020. Alongside compensation, the timeline lists prevention measures: administrative guidance on comprehensive measures to prevent health impairment from overwork, first issued in February 2002 and replaced by new versions in 2006 and 2016; the 2005 amendment of the Industrial Safety and Health Act that made face-to-face guidance by a physician mandatory ([[DefinedTerm/physician-interview-guidance-for-long-working-hours]]); and the 2018 work style reform legislation, which set the upper limit on overtime at, in principle, 45 hours a month and 360 hours a year ([[DefinedTerm/overtime]]) and strengthened physician guidance for employees working long hours.
