Temporary employment covers employees with fixed-term or otherwise limited-duration contracts, as distinct from permanent employment, in which the contract is of unlimited duration. In the [[Dataset/eu-labour-force-survey]], an employee's job is deemed temporary if employer and employee agree that its end is determined by objective conditions, such as a specific date, the completion of an assignment or the return of an employee who is temporarily absent. Typical cases are seasonal workers, people substituting for employees on maternity leave, workers on project-based contracts, people with training contracts of specified duration, and people engaged by an agency or employment exchange and hired out to a third party for specific tasks, unless they have a written contract of unlimited duration.

## Usage

Eurostat applies the distinction only to employees and only to their main job; the self-employed (with or without employees) and unpaid family workers are outside it, and on-call or casual workers are classified as employees or self-employed according to their employment relationship. Both temporary and permanent jobs can be full-time or part-time.

Between 2015 and 2024, the EU share of temporary employment was highest in 2017 (13.8%) and lowest in 2024 (11.0%), with women's share consistently about 2 percentage points above men's (12.2% against 10.0% in 2024). Temporary employment fluctuates more than other employment: in 2020 it fell by 11.3%, against a 0.4% fall in non-temporary employment, and it fell again by 3.7% in both 2023 and 2024 while other employment kept growing. The 11.0% in 2024 corresponded to 22.1 million employees, about 80% of them in six countries (Germany, France, Spain, Italy, Poland and the Netherlands). Shares ranged from 1.4% in Lithuania and 1.5% in Romania to 22.2% in the Netherlands.

Temporary contracts are concentrated among young people: in 2024, 31.1% of employees aged 15 to 29 were on one, against 7.4% of those aged 30 to 54 and 4.6% of those aged 55 to 64, with young people's shares reaching 48.8% in the Netherlands. The reasons differ by age. Among 15- to 29-year-olds the most common reason was being in education or training (30.2%), followed by not finding a permanent job (18.6%) and the job being offered only on a temporary contract (16.1%); among 25- to 64-year-olds, not finding a permanent job (32.7%) and availability only on a temporary contract (28.1%) together accounted for 60.8%, while 8.2% did not want a permanent job.

Temporary employment also varies by occupation, education and family situation. Service and sales workers (21.5% of temporary employment, against 15.5% of other employment) and elementary occupations (15.3% against 7.4%) are over-represented, and managers under-represented. Among people aged 25 to 64, the share falls as the level of education rises; women have higher shares than men; and the presence of children is associated with lower shares for men at every education level, whereas for women with low or medium education it is associated with higher shares.

[[DefinedTerm/temporary-agency-work]] is one form of temporary employment, in which workers are employed by an agency and assigned to a company temporarily, the contract typically being between the worker and the agency rather than the company.
