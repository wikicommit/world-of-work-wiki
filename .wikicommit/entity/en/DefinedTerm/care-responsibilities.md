In the EU Labour Force Survey's [[Dataset/eu-lfs-ad-hoc-module-on-reconciliation-between-work-and-family-life]], care responsibilities are of two kinds. Childcare responsibilities cover one's own or one's partner's children up to the age of 14, and anyone whose own or partner's children live in their household is automatically counted as having them. Care responsibilities for incapacitated relatives cover looking after or helping a partner or relative aged 15 or over — including the spouse's or partner's relatives, and disabled children from the age of 15 — who needs care because they are sick, elderly or disabled, whether or not they live in the same household.

## Usage

Eurostat uses the concept to measure how many people combine work with care and how care affects their employment. In 2018, 34.4% of people aged 18-64 in the EU-28 had care responsibilities: 28.9% for children under 15 only, 4.1% for incapacitated relatives only, and under 2% for both — almost 90 million people with care responsibilities for children only and more than 12 million for incapacitated relatives only. The share without any ranged from 72.4% in Germany to 55.2% in Ireland. Childcare responsibilities are most common among people aged 35-44, the employed, the highly educated and couples with children; women make up 52.2% of those caring only for children but 63.0% of those caring only for incapacitated relatives, who are mostly aged 45 or over and live more often in rural areas.

The statistics show a strong gender divide in how care affects work. In 2018, 27.1% of employed people aged 18-64 adapted their work to facilitate childcare responsibilities, more often those with a high level of education (34.3%) than a low one (17.1%). Among those with childcare responsibilities, 83% of men but 61% of women said it had no effect on their current employment; 18% of women and 3% of men had reduced their working time, and 82% of all those who reduced their hours for childcare were women — with lower shares in Finland (58.1%), Estonia, Denmark and Sweden. A third of employed women but 1.3% of men had interrupted work for more than six months for childcare. Care for incapacitated relatives affects employment much less: 4.2% of people in or with previous employment had reduced their working time or interrupted work for at least a month for it, 5.9% of women against 2.5% of men.

How far work can be fitted around care also varies. In 2018, 29.4% of employees with childcare responsibilities in the EU-28 said they could generally both vary the start or end of their working day and take whole days off for care, and 70.2% could do at least one of these, while 25.2% could do neither. Of employed people with care responsibilities, 63.4% reported no obstacle at work to reconciling work and care; the most common obstacles were long working hours (9.9%) and unpredictable or difficult work schedules (9.4%).
