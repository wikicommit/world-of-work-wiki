Rest breaks at work are periods during or between working days in which a worker is entitled to be free from work. In the UK, according to GOV.UK's guidance, workers over 18 are usually entitled to three types of break: rest breaks at work, daily rest and weekly rest. A worker's employment contract may give them more or different rights to breaks.

## Usage

**Rest breaks during the working day.** Workers have the right to one uninterrupted 20-minute rest break during their working day if they work more than 6 hours a day; this could be a tea or lunch break. Whether the break is paid depends on the employment contract.

**Daily rest.** Workers have the right to 11 hours' rest between working days — for example, a worker who finishes at 8pm should not start again until 7am the next day.

**Weekly rest.** Workers have the right to either an uninterrupted 24 hours without any work each week, or an uninterrupted 48 hours without any work each fortnight.

Where work is monotonous, such as work on a production line, and so puts health and safety at risk, an employer should give an employee enough breaks to make sure their health and safety is not at risk. Domestic workers in a private house, such as a cleaner or au pair, are not entitled to rest breaks for health and safety reasons.
