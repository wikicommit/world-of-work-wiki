A caregiving penalty is the cost that providing care, especially unpaid care, imposes on the caregiver. In the short term, leaving the labour force or reducing working hours immediately lowers pay; in the long term, it can lower future earnings through missed promotions or being relegated to lower-paid jobs, and reduced labour force participation and earnings in turn mean lower Social Security and employment-based retirement benefits.

## Usage

The U.S. Department of Labor's [[Report/older-women-and-unpaid-caregiving-in-the-us]] describes women in general as hard-hit by caregiving penalties, and notes elsewhere that women are most often responsible for family caregiving. It cites recent estimates that, as a result of a lifetime of caregiving, mothers earn 15 percent less over their lifetimes than they otherwise would. Older women, it argues, have fewer years left to make up for earnings or work hours given up to care for others; they are especially likely to live in poverty, with caregiving responsibilities a significant contributing factor. The brief also cites Women's Bureau analyses indicating that up to 13% of women aged 55 or older who left the workforce during the first year of the pandemic did so to provide care.
