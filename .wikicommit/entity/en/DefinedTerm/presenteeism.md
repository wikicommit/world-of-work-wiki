Presenteeism is the fact that people sometimes work while they consider themselves to be ill. In the EU-OSHA report on musculoskeletal disorders it is treated, alongside restrictions in daily activities and absence from work, as one of the ways health problems affect workers' productivity: rather than reducing the hours a worker can put in, as absenteeism does, it points to health problems affecting how productive a worker is while at work.

## Usage

The EU-OSHA report [[Report/work-related-msds-prevalence-costs-and-demographics-in-the-eu]] measures presenteeism with a question in the 2015 [[Dataset/european-working-conditions-survey]] on how many days in the past 12 months respondents had worked while sick. Workers with [[DefinedTerm/work-related-musculoskeletal-disorders]] and other health problems worked while sick more often than workers with no health problems: 13 % had done so for 1 to 3 days (against 8 %) and 29 % for 4 to 20 days (against 9 %). Workers whose only health problem was an MSD did not differ significantly from workers with only other health problems.
