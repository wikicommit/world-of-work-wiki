Paid domestic work (*trabajo doméstico remunerado*) is work done for a private household in exchange for pay. Mexico's National Survey of Occupation and Employment (ENOE) defines people employed in paid domestic work as those who provide their services in cleaning, assistance and other tasks related to the home of a person or a family in exchange for economic remuneration. The category includes domestic employees, cooks, chauffeurs, carers of people, watchmen, launderers, ironers and those who work as gardeners in private homes. INEGI, citing UN Women, describes domestic or household work in general as the many tasks of maintaining a home — cooking, cleaning, looking after the garden or pets, caring for people and others — and distinguishes the paid form from unpaid domestic work (see [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]] and [[DefinedTerm/housework]]).

## Usage

INEGI uses the measure to profile domestic workers for the International Day of Domestic Workers on 30 March. In [[Report/statistics-for-international-domestic-workers-day-2024]], it counts 2.5 million people aged 15 and over in paid domestic work in Mexico in the third quarter of 2023, 4.2% of all employed people, nine in ten of them women. The bulk of them (82%) are domestic employees, the group that Mexico's National Occupation Classification System defines as people who mainly clean private homes but may also prepare food, wash and iron clothes and care for people.

INEGI describes paid domestic work as one of the occupations with the highest rate of [[DefinedTerm/informal-employment]]: 95.0% of paid domestic workers worked informally, 97.1% had no written contract and 69.5% received no employment benefits, and it presents access to benefits as an essential element in formalising the work. Most earned little — 67.8% had incomes of up to one [[DefinedTerm/minimum-wage]] — and conditions differed by sex, women being more often informal, on lower pay and without benefits than men.
