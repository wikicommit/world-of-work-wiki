The Health and Safety Executive's Management Standards for work-related stress describe six areas of work that may affect employee well-being when poorly managed. The standards set out desired conditions for each area, and HSE frames them as a way for managers, employees and representatives to improve the organization of work together. The accompanying leaflet [[Report/how-to-tackle-work-related-stress]] applies them through a five-step process.

## Usage

- **Demands** concern workload, work patterns and the work environment. HSE calls for achievable demands in agreed working hours, jobs matched to people's capabilities, and ways to address concerns.
- **Control** concerns how much say people have over their work, including pace, breaks and work patterns where possible.
- **Support** concerns information, resources, constructive feedback and help from the organization, managers and colleagues.
- **Relationships** concern positive working, conflict, bullying and ways to report or address unacceptable behavior.
- **Role** concerns clear responsibilities and avoiding conflicting requirements.
- **Change** concerns communication, consultation, likely effects on jobs and support during organizational change.

HSE presents the standards as an organizational approach, aimed at improving conditions for groups of employees. Its [[HowTo/apply-the-hse-management-standards-for-work-related-stress]] process combines worker input with surveys and existing workplace data to identify priorities.
