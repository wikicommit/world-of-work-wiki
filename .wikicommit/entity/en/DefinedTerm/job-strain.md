Job strain is a component of work stress defined as high psychological demands at work coupled with low decision-making latitude — that is, a job that is hectic and subject to conflicting demands while giving the worker little use of their skills and little say over how the work is done. Decision latitude combines two elements: skill discretion (whether the job requires learning new things and a high level of skill, and whether it involves doing things over and over) and decision authority (the freedom to decide how to do the job and how much say the worker has in what happens in it).

## Usage

In Statistics Canada's [[Dataset/national-population-health-survey]], as used in the [[ScholarlyArticle/shift-work-and-health]] study, job strain is one of five components of work stress derived from twelve statements that respondents rated on a five-point agreement scale, alongside physical demands, supervisor support, co-worker support and job insecurity. Two items measure psychological demands and five measure decision latitude; so that each item contributes equally, the summed scores are divided by 2 and 5 respectively, and the psychological-demand score is then divided by the decision-latitude score. Respondents whose ratio fell in the upper quartile of the 1994/95 distribution (1.18 or more) were classed as having high job strain. The internal consistency of the scale was modest (Cronbach's alpha of 0.61 for decision latitude and 0.34 for psychological demands).

The article notes that work stress has been linked to health problems such as depression, anxiety, migraine, high blood pressure and coronary heart disease, and that failing to control for it is a limitation often cited in studies of [[DefinedTerm/shift-work]] and health. In its 1994/95 data, high job strain was reported by 17% of men and 29% of women with regular daytime schedules, compared with 30% and 29% of men on evening and rotating shifts and 40% and 45% of women on those shifts. In its cross-sectional models, job strain was significantly associated with higher psychological distress among men.
