Informal employment (labour informality, or non-registration), as measured in Argentine labour statistics, is work that takes place outside the social security system: an employee is informal when the employer does not register them with social security, and an independent worker is informal when they make no contribution to a pension scheme. It is associated with low incomes and often with poverty, and with precarious access to labour rights such as paid holidays, health and unemployment insurance, disability pensions and a future retirement pension — which is why Argentina's national survey of working conditions treats it as a dimension of workers' health and safety as well as of employment.

## Usage

In the [[Dataset/ecetss]], an employee counts as informal when the employer makes no pension deduction from their pay, the same operational definition Argentina's Permanent Household Survey uses to calculate the rate of unregistered employment. For independent workers, informality is determined by the absence of any pension payment under any of its forms (the simplified *monotributo* or social *monotributo*, the self-employed scheme, or a provincial or professional fund). A productive unit is in turn classed as formal when at least one of its employees is registered, and informal when none is; an unregistered employee working in an informal unit is described as being in "double informality", which further reduces their prospects of improving their income and social security coverage.

On this basis the 2018 survey found informality among 42.8% of workers in urban Argentina, with very different levels by category: 28.5% of employees in productive units, 76.8% of domestic workers in private homes, 68.3% of own-account workers and 23% of employers. Among employees it fell as education and establishment size rose and was highest among young people, and unregistered employees had low coverage of rights such as health insurance (*obra social*), the year-end bonus, paid holidays and accident insurance; the survey notes that lacking cover by an occupational risk insurer (ART) also means lacking the prevention and health and safety care that such cover involves. The survey authors stress that the category is highly heterogeneous, spanning situations as different as professionals and street vendors. Detailed results are in [[Report/ecetss-2018]].
