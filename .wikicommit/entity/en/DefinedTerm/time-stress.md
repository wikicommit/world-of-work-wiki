Time stress is the experience of always or often feeling rushed or pressed for time. The [[Dataset/abs-time-use-survey]] measures it by asking people aged 15 and over how often they feel this way, and reports the proportion who answer always or often. Statistics Canada, in [[Report/time-use-total-work-burden-unpaid-work-and-leisure]], uses the closely related notion of time pressure, defined as "both a cognitive awareness of not having enough time and the emotional experience of hectic pace, harriedness, and rushing, accompanied by apprehension and frustration".

## Usage

In 2024, a third of people (33%) always or often felt rushed or pressed for time, more females (36%) than males (30%), according to [[Report/how-australians-use-their-time-2024]]. Balancing work and family was the most common reason given (42%). It also varied with age and family situation: almost half of females aged 35 to 44 (49%) reported it, and people in couple families with children under 15 were more likely to feel rushed than couple families with no children (51% compared with 26%).

In Canada, Statistics Canada reads women's heightened time pressure as support for the view that they perform a disproportionate share of [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]]. In 2015, among people aged 25 to 54, 48.9% of women often felt at the end of the day that they had not accomplished what they set out to do, against 43.4% of men; 68.6% of women felt under stress when they did not have enough time, against 60.0% of men; and 46.3% of women felt constantly under stress trying to accomplish more than they could handle, against 39.8% of men.
