The gender leadership gap is the under-representation of women in management positions. In the IAB's work on private-sector establishments in Germany it is expressed through a representation measure: the share of women at a given management level divided by their share of all employees. A value of 1 means women hold management positions in line with their share of employment; values below 1 mean they are under-represented, and values above 1 that they are over-represented.

## Usage

The IAB has analysed and documented the gender leadership gap in private-sector establishments in Germany for 20 years using the [[Dataset/iab-establishment-panel]], distinguishing the top and the second management level. In [[Report/women-hold-less-than-a-third-of-top-management-positions]] it reports that women held 29% of top-level and 42% of second-level positions in 2024 against a 45% share of employees — measures of 0.64 and 0.93 — and that although women's share of top positions has risen over 20 years, the gap has hardly changed because their share of employees has risen too. Measuring against the employment share separates the two: an industry with many women in management can still have a large gap if even more of its employees are women (health and social work and education: 55% of top positions, 76% of employees), while one with few can have a smaller gap if few women work there at all (construction).

Citing earlier research, the report attributes the limits on women's career advancement to traditional role models, structural barriers and inadequate reconciliation of work and family, and it examines whether establishments' reconciliation measures are linked to women's advancement into management.
