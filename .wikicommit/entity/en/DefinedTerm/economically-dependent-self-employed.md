The economically dependent self-employed are self-employed people who sit at the boundary between [[DefinedTerm/self-employment]] and employment because they depend on a client both economically and in how their work is organised. In the operational definition Eurostat adopted for the [[Dataset/eu-lfs-ad-hoc-module-on-self-employment]], they are self-employed persons without employees who, during the last 12 months, worked for only one client or for a dominant client (in terms of income), and whose client decided their working hours. The definition combines three criteria: having no employees, economic dependency (the number and importance of clients) and organisational dependency (who decides the start and end of the working day).

## Usage

Under this definition there were about 1 million economically dependent self-employed in the EU-28 in 2017, or 3.4% of the 33 million self-employed. Of the self-employed, 71.8% had no employees; 20.2% of those had one or a dominant client, and 23.4% of that group said the client controlled their working time. The share was highest in Slovakia (12.8% of the self-employed without employees) and Cyprus (8.9%) and close to zero in several countries, including Greece, Bulgaria, Estonia and Croatia.

Compared with the independent self-employed without employees, the dependent self-employed were concentrated in construction (16.8%) and human health and social work (11.1%) rather than agriculture and trade, were rarely managers, clerical workers or skilled agricultural workers, and tended to have a higher level of education. They were also the group most likely to want to change status: 32.6% wished to work as employees, against 17.4% of the independent self-employed without employees and 10.5% of the self-employed with employees.

Eurostat presents the definition as a first approach to the concept rather than a settled standard. It describes the group as much debated over the past decade but lacking internationally comparable data, and calls the 2017 module the first large-scale attempt to identify and classify it. At the time, discussions on the definition were ongoing at international level with the International Labour Organization. Eurostat suggested two possible refinements: extending the definition to the self-employed *with* employees who have a dominant client deciding their working time (about 0.1 million people in 2017), and drawing on further indicators of dependency such as [[DefinedTerm/job-autonomy]] — whether the person can influence the content and order of their tasks — and whether they work with business partners. In some countries a large share of the self-employed without employees could not be classified because they did not answer the questions on clients and working time.
