In the EU Labour Force Survey's [[Dataset/eu-lfs-ad-hoc-module-on-reconciliation-between-work-and-family-life]], professional childcare services comprise all forms of care organised by private or public structures — centre-based pre-schools and nursery schools (normally ISCED level 0), day care centres, crèches and after-school centres — as well as organised family care and care by professional childminders, whether arranged through an intermediary service or employed directly by the family. The care can take place in the household, at the carer's home or in a facility, and organised services count whether or not parents pay for them directly, since they may be subsidised by the state or the employer. A childminder engaged directly by the parents counts only if paid and working at it as a real profession, with a registered business of their own, rather than as a side activity for extra money.

## Usage

Eurostat uses the concept to ask whether people with childcare responsibilities rely on formal care. In 2018, 62.3% of people with childcare responsibilities in the EU-28 did not use professional childcare services at all, while 37.7% used them for some or all children. Use was higher among employed parents (around 40%) than among the unemployed or people outside the labour force (around a quarter), and among highly educated parents (45.7%) than among those with medium (36.8%) or low (24.5%) education; people born in a country other than the reporting country used them less. Among those not using them, the main reason given was that care was arranged alone or with a partner (47.0%), followed by children being able to look after themselves (18.1%) and informal help such as grandparents (14.9%); around 7% mentioned costs or other personal reasons and 2.9% service-related issues. Cost was a more prominent reason among carers aged 18-24 (14.3%).
