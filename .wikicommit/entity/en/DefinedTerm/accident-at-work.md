An accident at work (*accident du travail*, AT) is, as defined in the French official statistics Insee publishes, an accident causing a bodily or psychological injury, whatever its cause, that happens through or in the course of work. It concerns anyone who is an employee or who works, in any capacity and in any place, for one or more employers or heads of business. The count Insee reports includes only accidents at work that were declared to and recognised by the national health insurance fund (Cnam), the agricultural mutual insurance scheme (Mutualité sociale agricole) or the pension fund for local government employees (CNRACL), and that led to at least one day off work.

## Usage

In 2022, the general scheme, the agricultural scheme and the CNRACL recognised 679,200 accidents at work with time off. Men accounted for 61% of victims and 61% of victims were under 45; 41,000 accidents led to a permanent partial incapacity for work and 789 were fatal. The construction sector recorded 75,140 of these accidents, and health and social work together accounted for 40% of women's accidents at work. L’Assurance Maladie – Risques professionnels observed a break in its series from 2022, so these figures are not comparable with those Insee published in earlier editions.

Insee reports these figures alongside those for [[DefinedTerm/occupational-disease]], which it defines as harm to health contracted in the course of work resulting from a slowly developing series of events to which no certain origin or date can be assigned. Occupational diseases arise later in life than accidents at work.
