An accident at work is an accident that happens through or in the course of work and causes physical or mental (psychological) harm to the person working. Statistical systems agree on that core but differ in which accidents they count. In the EU's harmonised [[Dataset/european-statistics-on-accidents-at-work]] (ESAW), an accident at work is a discrete occurrence during the course of work which leads to physical or mental harm; it generally includes road traffic accidents in the course of work but excludes accidents on the journey between home and work. ESAW counts fatal accidents (death within one year) and non-fatal accidents causing at least four full calendar days of absence. In French official statistics published by Insee, an accident at work (*accident du travail*, AT) is an accident causing a bodily or psychological injury, whatever its cause, that happens through or in the course of work, to anyone who is an employee or who works, in any capacity and in any place, for one or more employers or heads of business; the count Insee reports includes only accidents declared to and recognised by the national health insurance fund (Cnam), the agricultural mutual insurance scheme (Mutualité sociale agricole) or the pension fund for local government employees (CNRACL) that led to at least one day off work.

## Usage

Accident counts are often expressed as incidence rates per 100,000 employed people. In the EU in 2024, ESAW recorded 2.78 million non-fatal and 3,367 fatal accidents at work, or 1,359 non-fatal and 1.65 fatal accidents per 100,000 employed people. Men were involved in about two-thirds of non-fatal accidents, which Eurostat links to the share of men and women in employment, the kinds of work they do, women's greater likelihood of working part time, and the concentration of accidents in male-dominated sectors such as mining, manufacturing and construction. Cross-country comparison is limited by under-reporting and by differences between insurance-based and legal-obligation reporting systems.

In France in 2022, the general scheme, the agricultural scheme and the CNRACL recognised 679,200 accidents at work with time off. Men accounted for 61% of victims and 61% of victims were under 45; 41,000 accidents led to a permanent partial incapacity for work and 789 were fatal. The construction sector recorded 75,140 of these accidents, and health and social work together accounted for 40% of women's accidents at work. L’Assurance Maladie – Risques professionnels observed a break in its series from 2022, so these figures are not comparable with those Insee published in earlier editions.

Insee reports these figures alongside those for [[DefinedTerm/occupational-disease]], which it defines as harm to health contracted in the course of work resulting from a slowly developing series of events to which no certain origin or date can be assigned. Occupational diseases arise later in life than accidents at work.
