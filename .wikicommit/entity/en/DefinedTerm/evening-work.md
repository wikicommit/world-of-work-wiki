Evening work is work done after typical daytime hours but before usual sleeping hours. Statistics Canada notes that European Union standards for the Labour Force Survey place it after daytime hours such as 6:00 AM to 6:00 PM and before usual sleeping hours such as 12:00 AM. Its evening work indicator counts the workers whose usual schedule in their main job is a regular evening shift or regular evening hours, as a percentage of all employed persons.

## Usage

In Canada the indicator is one of the measures in Statistics Canada's *Quality of Employment in Canada* series, reported in [[Report/evening-work-2022]] from a supplement to the [[Dataset/canadian-labour-force-survey]]. In April 2022, 918,000 people, or 4.8% of workers aged 15 to 69, usually worked regular evening hours. Earlier surveys taken at other times of year gave 6.5% in March 2020, just before the pandemic, and 5.4% in fall 2016. Statistics Canada suggests the lower 2022 rate may partly reflect the fall in employment in accommodation and food services, the industry where evening work is most common.

Statistics Canada links evening work to the demand for services such as dining and entertainment. An evening schedule can leave students and other workers free to meet other responsibilities during the day, but it may conflict with childcare and family responsibilities. In April 2022:

- **Industry.** 21.8% of workers in accommodation and food services usually worked in the evenings, and the industry made up nearly a quarter (24.4%) of all evening workers. Rates were also above average in business, building and other support services; arts and entertainment; retail trade; manufacturing; and health care and social assistance.
- **Age and sex.** Youth aged 15 to 24 had a rate of 14.2%, against 3.3% for workers aged 25 to 54 and 3.7% for those aged 55 to 69. Among youth, students (19.7%) were more likely to work evenings than non-students (8.3%). Men (4.8%) and women (4.9%) had similar rates.
- **Hours.** Evening work was more than three times as common among part-time workers (those usually working less than 30 hours a week, 11.3%; see [[DefinedTerm/part-time-employment]]) as among full-time workers (3.3%). This partly reflects how public-facing industries organize shifts to meet peaks in demand, and contrasts with [[DefinedTerm/night-work]], whose share was similar for full-time and part-time workers.
- **Racialized groups.** Rates were highest among Filipino (11.8%) and Korean (10.0%) Canadians, both groups in which employment in accommodation and food services was about double the national average.
- **Flexibility and location.** Evening workers were less likely to have flexible scheduling: 23.4% could vary when their workday started and ended, compared with 37.2% of workers with regular daytime hours, and 54.8% said it was easy or very easy to take an hour or two off for personal reasons, against 73.2%. Almost all (94.1%) worked only at locations other than their home, compared with 72.2% of daytime workers.
