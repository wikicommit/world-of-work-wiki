The Sumer survey (*Surveillance médicale des expositions des salariés aux risques professionnels*, medical surveillance of employees' exposure to occupational risks) maps the exposure of employees in France to the main occupational risks. It is managed jointly by Dares, the research and statistics directorate of the French Ministry of Labour, and the medical labour inspectorate.

## Details

The 2017 edition was administered by occupational physicians between June 2016 and September 2017; 26,494 employees responded, representing 24.8 million employees. A question on whether and how often the respondent teleworks — a few days or half-days a month, one day, two days, or three or more days a week, or never — was introduced for the first time in 2016.

When a respondent teleworks, it is not possible to tell how far their answers describe their working conditions on days at the usual workplace or on telework days. The Insee dossier [[Report/can-telework-improve-managers-working-conditions]] combined it with the [[Dataset/reponse-survey]] to study how telework relates to managers' working hours, autonomy and work intensity, social support, training, exposure to organisational change, and health.
