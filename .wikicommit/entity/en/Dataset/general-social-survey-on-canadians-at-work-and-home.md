The General Social Survey (GSS) on Canadians at Work and Home is the 2016 cycle of Statistics Canada's General Social Survey, a voluntary annual cross-sectional survey begun in 1985 in which each cycle has a core topic and a standard set of sociodemographic questions. The 2016 cycle took a comprehensive look at the way Canadians live, covering work, home, leisure and overall well-being, with questions on purpose in life, opportunities, life aspirations, outlook and resilience.

## Details

Its target population was non-institutionalized people aged 15 and over living in the ten provinces. It was conducted from August to December 2016, with a total sample of 19,609 and an overall response rate of 50.8%.

The survey asked about five types of workplace harassment in the past 12 months — verbal abuse, humiliating behaviour, threats, physical violence, and unwanted sexual attention or sexual harassment — and about who was responsible. It also asked about the work environment, including input into decisions, support from managers or supervisors, workload manageability, choice in the sequencing of tasks, competition with colleagues, conflicts with managers or supervisors and friends at work, and about job satisfaction, motivation, intention to leave and sense of belonging to the organization, alongside personal measures such as stress, self-rated general and mental health, and outlook on the future. Statistics Canada's study [[Report/harassment-in-canadian-workplaces]] used it to analyse [[DefinedTerm/workplace-harassment]] among about 9,000 respondents aged 15 to 64 who had worked for pay in the past year. The survey did not ask the gender of the harasser, and because the reference period is the previous year, a respondent's current job is not necessarily the one in which harassment occurred.
