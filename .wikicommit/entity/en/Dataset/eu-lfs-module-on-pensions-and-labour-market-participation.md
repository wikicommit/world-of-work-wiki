The EU-LFS module on pensions and labour market participation is a set of additional questions in the [[Dataset/eu-labour-force-survey]] (EU-LFS) on the [[DefinedTerm/transition-from-work-to-retirement]]. It covers the types of pension people receive, the age at which they start receiving one, and whether they continue or stop working after their first old-age pension and why.

## Details

The module was carried out in 2023 as one of the EU-LFS's 8-yearly modules, and an earlier survey on the subject was carried out in 2012; the next round is planned for 2031. Modules have been an inherent part of the EU-LFS since 1999: they were called "ad hoc modules" until 2020, and from 2021 those whose variables recur every eight years are called "8-yearly modules".

The 2023 module covers only people aged 50–74. It covers all EU countries and the EFTA countries Norway and Switzerland; in Cyprus it covers only the areas controlled by the Government of the Republic of Cyprus.

In the module, an old-age pension covers statutory, occupational and personal pensions. Statutory pensions provide social protection against the risks linked to old age; occupational pensions are private supplementary plans linked to an employment relationship or professional activity, often established in employment contracts or collective agreements; and personal pensions are private voluntary plans invested in an individual account. A disability pension is a periodic payment supporting the income of someone below the standard retirement age whose disability impairs their ability to work or earn. Eurostat's published percentages are calculated on totals that exclude non-response.

Results for 2023 and 2012 are not always directly comparable: the question on reasons for continuing to work was asked in 2012 of people employed in the reference week, but in 2023 about the situation at first old-age pension receipt, and the answer categories differed.
