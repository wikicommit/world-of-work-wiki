The EU-LFS ad hoc module on reconciliation between work and family life is a set of additional questions in the [[Dataset/eu-labour-force-survey]] (EU-LFS) that examines who in Europe has [[DefinedTerm/care-responsibilities]] for young children or incapacitated relatives, and what they did or have done to reconcile their job with those responsibilities. Eurostat presents it as a tool for monitoring progress on the EU's long-standing commitment to work-life balance, including targets to improve the provision of childcare.

## Details

The subject was covered in 2005 and 2010, and the 2018 module was developed to give insight into how people aged 18 to 64 reconcile work and family life. Its legal basis was Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2236/2016 of 12 December 2016, which obliged EU Member States and EFTA countries to carry out the survey and send microdata to Eurostat; Turkey also implemented it. Under the Integrated European Social Statistics (IESS) Regulation, which governs the LFS from 2021, the module is to be repeated every eight years from 2025.

For the 2025 round, the module's variables were laid down in Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2022/2312 of 25 November 2022, with the aim of providing harmonised information on reconciling work and family life at European level. Spain's National Statistics Institute (INE) ran it, in collaboration with Eurostat and with costs co-financed by the European Commission, as the 2025 module of its Labour Force Survey (EPA), covering people aged 18 to 74 in private households. The Spanish module asked members of the survey subsample at their sixth interview about their usual situation, and its published results cover care responsibilities for children, grandchildren and dependent relatives, the use of professional childcare and the main reason for not using it, weekly hours spent caring for dependent relatives, the main change made at work and the main difficulty at work in reconciling work and care, and the use of birth leave and childcare leave of absence; they are summarised in [[Report/epa-module-on-reconciliation-between-work-and-family-life-2025]].

The 2018 module asks who has care responsibilities and who does not; the characteristics of carers; whether people with childcare responsibilities use [[DefinedTerm/professional-childcare-services]] and, if not, why; the effects of childcare responsibilities on employment; how much flexibility people have at work to reconcile work and family; and how far they have had career breaks for care. Flexibility is measured by combining two questions — whether employees can vary the start or end of their working day for care reasons, and whether they can arrange their working time to take at least one full day off for care without using annual leave. Questions on reducing working time or interrupting work for at least a month to care for ill, elderly or disabled relatives were put to people in employment or with previous work experience.

Eurostat describes the target population of the module as persons in employment who have care responsibilities for children and/or incapacitated relatives, while its results also cover people with previous employment and people outside the labour force. The EU-LFS concepts follow International Labour Organization guidelines.
