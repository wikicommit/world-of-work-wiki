The Annual Population Survey (APS) is a household survey of the UK Office for National Statistics (ONS) that collects information on individuals' labour market activities together with other personal characteristics. The ONS describes it as a large and detailed source.

## Details

Because it records personal characteristics alongside working hours, the survey makes it possible to examine which characteristics are associated with the hours individuals work. In [[Report/average-hours-worked-and-economic-growth-uk-1998-to-2022]], the ONS identified nearly 74,000 respondents in the 2022 survey (73,977) who were over 16, in employment and working a positive number of hours in the reference week, and regressed their total weekly hours on age, sex, nationality, marital status, self-employment, housing tenure, graduate status and occupation. Combining the 2019 and 2022 surveys (184,424 observations) allowed it to estimate how the effect of each characteristic on hours had changed since before the COVID-19 pandemic.

The ONS also used the survey, with newly reweighted earnings data for 2018, in its experimental analysis [[Report/job-quality-indicators-in-the-uk-hours-pay-and-contracts-2018]]. It explains there that although the Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings is the primary UK source for earnings and hours, the APS provides employment indicators and more demographic information, such as ethnicity and education, needed for its detailed analysis. The APS collects self-reported data: despite validation and imputation, responses may not be fully accurate, may be incomplete and may not match what businesses report, and sample sizes at local authority level are small.
