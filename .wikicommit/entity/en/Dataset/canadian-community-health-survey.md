The Canadian Community Health Survey (CCHS) is a Statistics Canada survey that collects health information at the health region level. It covers the household population aged 12 or older in all provinces and territories, except people living on Indian reserves, on Canadian Forces bases and in some remote areas. Data collection for its first cycle, cycle 1.1, began in September 2000 and ran for 14 months.

## Details

The survey uses the area frame designed for the Labour Force Survey as its primary sampling frame, with a multistage stratified cluster design to sample dwellings. In cycle 1.1, 83% of the sampled households came from the area frame and were interviewed face to face, one person being randomly selected in about 82% of these households and two in the rest; in some health regions, random digit dialling or list frames of telephone numbers supplied the remaining 17% of the targeted sample, who were interviewed by telephone. The response rate was 84.7%, the responding sample was 131,535, and 6.3% of interviews were obtained by proxy.

Respondents report the work schedule of their main job, which allows evening, night, rotating and irregular shifts to be distinguished from a regular daytime schedule, and those with non-standard hours give their main reason for working them; the survey also asks about sleep problems. Some questions asked in the [[Dataset/national-population-health-survey]], such as those on work stress, were not asked in all provinces in the CCHS.

Statistics Canada's [[ScholarlyArticle/shift-work-and-health]] used cycle 1.1 to profile [[DefinedTerm/shift-work]] in 2000/01 among 24,364 men and 22,398 women aged 18 to 54 in the 10 provinces who had worked throughout the previous year, finding that 30% of men and 26% of women in that group had non-standard schedules.
