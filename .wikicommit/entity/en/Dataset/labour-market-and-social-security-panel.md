The Panel Study Labour Market and Social Security (German: Panel „Arbeitsmarkt und soziale Sicherung“, PASS) is a panel survey in which around 15,000 people in 10,000 households are interviewed every year, representative of the resident population of Germany. Because of its broad range of questions and its sampling design, it is regarded as an important data basis for research on the labour market, the welfare state and poverty in Germany. The Institut für Arbeitsmarkt- und Berufsforschung (IAB) has a research department of the same name.

## Details

Actual weekly working hours are recorded for the current job of employees subject to social insurance contributions, the self-employed and people in marginal employment, with hours summed across several current jobs.

Wave 5 (2011) is the only wave that, alongside actual hours, also asked about desired working hours and attitudes to mothers' employment and to childcare outside the family. All respondents, whatever their employment status, were asked how many hours a week they would most like to work, taking into account the income they need and, where relevant, their partner's working hours; the question did not mention whether a job or a childcare place is available. They were also asked from what age of a child a mother can work again — first for at least 15 hours a week, then full-time for at least 30 — answering as a matter of principle regardless of whether childcare or a job is available, and from what age a child can be cared for regularly outside the family, for a few hours a day or full-day for at least 35 hours a week, regardless of whether they have children themselves. [[Report/working-time-preferences-and-employment-of-mothers]] uses this wave to compare desired and actual hours and these attitudes by household situation.
