The Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) is an annual, random-digit-dialed telephone survey of noninstitutionalized US civilian residents aged 18 years and over. It is conducted by US states and territories to gather data on health-related risk behaviours, chronic illnesses and conditions, and the use of health-related services.

## Details

The BRFSS questionnaire consists of a set of core questions asked by all states; states may also choose optional modules on specific subjects or add questions of their own. One such optional module covers industry and occupation: participants who are employed for wages, self-employed, or out of work for less than a year are asked "What kind of work do you do?", and their free-text answers are later coded to US Census occupation codes. Twenty-nine states administered this module in 2013 or 2014. The survey also asks respondents how many hours of sleep they get on average in a 24-hour period.

Response rates are calculated following American Association of Public Opinion Research guidelines. The median response rate across all states, territories and the District of Columbia was 46.4% in 2013 and 47.0% in 2014. BRFSS data are cross-sectional and self-reported, so they are subject to recall and social desirability bias and cannot establish temporal relationships.

[[ScholarlyArticle/short-sleep-duration-by-occupation-group-29-states-2013-2014]] used the 2013 and 2014 data from the 29 states with the occupation module — of 412,829 BRFSS participants in those years, 179,621 employed respondents formed its final analytical sample — to compare [[DefinedTerm/short-sleep-duration]] across occupation groups.
