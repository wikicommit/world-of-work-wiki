The General Social Survey on Caregiving and Care Receiving is a cycle of Statistics Canada's General Social Survey, conducted in 2012. It defines caregivers as people who provided help or care in the past 12 months to someone with problems related to one or more chronic conditions, or to someone with problems related to aging, and collects information on the condition of the person cared for, the tasks the caregiver helped with, the time spent and the caregiver's relationship to the primary-care recipient.

## Details

Because it asks about care over a 12-month period rather than on a single diary day, the survey captures caregiving that is given intermittently, which a time diary such as the [[Dataset/canadian-time-use-survey]] is likely to underestimate. [[Report/time-use-total-work-burden-unpaid-work-and-leisure]] uses it for that reason: 28.8% of Canadians aged 25 to 54 were caregivers in 2012, but only 11.8% of these saw their primary-care recipient daily. In the same report, the survey shows that 62.3% of women aged 25 to 54 had a child under 13 in the household and/or cared for a family member or friend, against 56.7% of men; that female caregivers were more likely than male caregivers to help with personal care, housework, scheduling or coordinating care, medical treatments and transportation, and less likely to help with household maintenance; and that female caregivers spent 11.8 hours in a typical week on these tasks against 7.4 hours for male caregivers. For most caregivers of both sexes the primary-care recipient was a member of their immediate family.
