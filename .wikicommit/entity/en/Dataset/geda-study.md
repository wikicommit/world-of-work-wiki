The German Health Update (*Gesundheit in Deutschland aktuell*, GEDA) is an interview survey conducted by the Robert Koch Institute as part of its health monitoring. The Robert Koch Institute is its data holder, and its stated aims are to provide current data on health-related topics and to analyse developments and trends over time. Its population is the resident population of Germany aged 18 and over.

## Details

The 2010 wave was carried out between September 2009 and July 2010 by computer-assisted telephone interviewing, with a sample of 22,050 women and men and a cooperation rate of 55.8%. For the 2010 wave, a module of nine questions on working conditions was developed that covers environmental, physical and psychosocial strains with two indicators each: lifting and carrying heavy loads (for men from 20 kg, for women from 10 kg), working in awkward postures, exposure to noise, cold, heat or similar, impairments in the working climate, strain from job insecurity, time strain from work (overtime, long working hours and commuting), [[DefinedTerm/shift-work]], working under performance or time pressure, and working under strict requirements on how the work is done. Respondents said whether they were exposed to each condition "often", "sometimes", "rarely" or "never", and a further question asked whether they believed their health was endangered by their work, answered "not at all", "moderately", "strongly" or "very strongly".

The Robert Koch Institute's report [[Report/work-strain-and-health]] used these questions for 13,767 respondents aged 18 to 64 who were in employment at the time of the interview. An earlier wave, GEDA 2009, provided data on accidents among adults, about one in five of which occurred at the workplace.
