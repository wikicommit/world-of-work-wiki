The Time Use Survey (TUS) is conducted by the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS) to show how Australians aged 15 and over spend their time across a day: the activities they undertake, the proportion of people who take part in each, the average time spent on them, and the household and personal circumstances of the people doing them. Results are reported for a person's diary day and grouped into four broad types of time use: personal care for self, employment and education, [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]], and free time.

## Details

The ABS has run the survey in 1992, 1997, 2006, 2020-21 and 2024; the 2024 cycle was conducted between August and October 2024. For that cycle the ABS changed the survey to improve the respondent experience, reduce reporting burden and improve the quality of critical data items, including the measurement of unpaid work and care. These changes give a more complete picture of time use in 2024 but reduce comparability with 2020-21.

Besides the main activity, the survey records [[DefinedTerm/supervisory-care-of-children]] undertaken at the same time as another activity, and it asks how often people feel rushed or pressed for time ([[DefinedTerm/time-stress]]). The 2024 results are published with tables by sex, age, selected demographics, weekday or weekend, parents' labour force status and age of youngest child, workday and working from home, disability, carer and volunteer status, and the time of day of paid work. The headline findings are in [[Report/how-australians-use-their-time-2024]].
