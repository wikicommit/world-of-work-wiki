The Household, Income and Labour Dynamics in Australia (HILDA) Survey is an Australian household survey whose unit record data are used to study work, family life and wellbeing. The HILDA Project was initiated and is funded by the Australian Government Department of Social Services and is managed by the Melbourne Institute of Applied Economic and Social Research. Data are collected in successive waves; wave 19 relates to 2019.

## Details

The survey records whether employees have a formal agreement with their employer to work from home, and how much they work at home. In 2019 about 8% of employees reported such an arrangement, working a median of one day a week at home. Because the same survey also asks about health, relationships and household work, the Productivity Commission used wave 19 in [[Report/working-from-home-research-paper]] to compare employees who did and did not work from home ([[DefinedTerm/working-from-home]]) before the COVID-19 pandemic. The measures it drew on include self-reported loneliness on a 7-point scale, the Mental Component Summary of the SF-36 health questionnaire, physical activity measured in metabolic-equivalent minutes and grouped into low, moderate and high levels, satisfaction with the flexibility to balance work and non-work commitments on a 0–10 scale, and partners' ratings of the respondent's relationships and of whether they do a fair share of household and childcare work. The data also carry an occupational status score (from the Australian Socioeconomic Index 2006) reflecting the education requirements and earnings potential of each occupation.

HILDA measures working from home as a formal arrangement with the employer, which differs from other Australian sources. The Census records whether a person worked from home rather than commuting on census day, and the Australian Bureau of Statistics' Household Impacts of COVID-19 Survey asked whether people had worked from home one or more times a week in the four weeks before the survey.
