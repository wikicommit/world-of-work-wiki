The EU-LFS ad hoc module on self-employment is a set of additional questions run in the [[Dataset/eu-labour-force-survey]] (EU-LFS) in 2017 to add to the information on the self-employed that the core survey provides. Its main aim was to identify and categorise people at the boundary between employment and [[DefinedTerm/self-employment]], and to define the [[DefinedTerm/economically-dependent-self-employed]] according to their economic and organisational dependency.

## Details

The module covered all EU Member States (then 28), as well as Iceland, Norway, Switzerland and Turkey. Its target population was persons in employment — employees as well as the self-employed — so that the self-employed could be compared with employees and family workers. It measured economic dependency by the number of clients and the share of income coming from a client, and organisational dependency by who decides the start and end of the working day; it also collected data on job autonomy, business partners, reasons for becoming self-employed, difficulties faced, the wish to change professional status and job satisfaction. Percentage calculations exclude item non-response.

Eurostat describes the module as the first large-scale attempt to find and classify the economically dependent self-employed, a much debated group for which internationally comparable quantitative data had been lacking. It responds to data needs linked to the "Small Business Act" for Europe and the Commission's 2012 Communication "Towards a job-rich recovery", which encourages job creation through promoting and supporting self-employment.
