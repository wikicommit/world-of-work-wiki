The Encuesta Nacional sobre Uso del Tiempo (ENUT) is the national time-use survey of Mexico's Instituto Nacional de Estadística y Geografía (INEGI). It records the activities that people aged 12 and over carried out during a reference week and how much time they gave them, distinguishing productive activities — paid work for the market and [[DefinedTerm/unpaid-work]] — from personal activities such as study, personal care and leisure. Its results are used to analyse differences in the distribution of time between women and men, age groups, places and ethnic groups, and to design and monitor public policies on equality and the sharing of domestic and care work. Headline measures include [[DefinedTerm/total-work-time]] and the [[DefinedTerm/time-use-participation-rate]].

## Details

INEGI has carried out six earlier time-use surveys: the ENTAUT in 1996 and ENUT editions in 1998, 2002, 2009, 2014 and 2019. The 1996 and 1998 surveys were modules of the national household income and expenditure survey (ENIGH), while the 2009, 2014 and 2019 editions were stand-alone surveys; these three are the most comparable with one another because they share a questionnaire of stylised questions based on INEGI's Mexican classification of time-use activities, which is harmonised with the United Nations' international classification (ICATUS). Its methodology follows United Nations and Economic Commission for Latin America and the Caribbean (ECLAC) guidance on time-use statistics. In November 2022 INEGI's governing board declared the ENUT to be Information of National Interest, which means it must be produced regularly and periodically.

The 2024 edition was preceded by a public consultation in 2023 and a statistical test at the end of that year. Fieldwork ran from 7 October to 29 November 2024, with a questionnaire of 83 questions in seven sections, and the survey is repeated every five years. The sample of 32,048 dwellings supports estimates for the country, for each state, and for localities with fewer than 10,000 and with 10,000 or more inhabitants. The 2024 edition kept comparability with 2014 and 2019 but added questions on the type of occupation and whether work was done in person or remotely, on Afro-descendant identity, disability and chronic or temporary illness, and on hired domestic workers, and it newly measured emotional care, refined the concept of [[DefinedTerm/passive-care]] and separated care for children aged 0 to 5 from care for those aged 6 to 14. Because emotional care was captured only in 2024, comparisons with earlier editions exclude it.

The survey supplies indicators for the UN Sustainable Development Goals, ECLAC's Gender Equality Observatory for Latin America and the Caribbean and Mexico's national catalogue of indicators, and is one of the main inputs to the satellite account of unpaid household work in Mexico's national accounts. The main 2024 results are in [[Report/enut-2024-results-report]].
