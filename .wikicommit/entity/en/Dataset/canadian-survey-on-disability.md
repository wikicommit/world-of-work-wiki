The Canadian Survey on Disability (CSD) is a national survey run by Statistics Canada of Canadians aged 15 and over whose everyday activities are limited because of a long-term condition or health-related problem. It provides comprehensive data on persons with disabilities for each province and territory, and collects information on disability types and severity, supports for persons with disabilities, employment profiles, income, education and other disability-specific topics. Its 2022 edition is the data source of [[Report/examining-work-potential-and-overqualification-among-persons-with-disabilities]], which uses it to measure [[DefinedTerm/work-potential]] and [[DefinedTerm/over-qualification]] among persons with disabilities.

## Details

**Population.** The 2022 survey covered Canadians aged 15 and over, as of the date of the 2021 Census of Population (May 2021), who were living in private dwellings. It excludes people living in institutions, on Canadian Armed Forces bases or on First Nations reserves, and those living in collective dwellings such as hospitals, seniors' residences, residential care facilities, shelters and correctional facilities, so its data should be interpreted with the institutionalized population left out.

**Identifying disability.** The CSD uses the Disability Screening Questions (DSQ), which are based on the social model of disability: a limitation in daily activities must be reported for a disability to be identified, and the presence of a difficulty alone is not sufficient. The questions first measure the degree of difficulty experienced across domains of functioning and then ask how often daily activities are limited by those difficulties. Anyone who reports being "sometimes", "often" or "always" limited in daily activities because of a long-term condition or health problem is counted as having a disability, as is anyone "rarely" limited who is also unable to do certain tasks or can do them only with a lot of difficulty. The exception is developmental disability, which is identified whenever the respondent has been diagnosed with a developmental condition, regardless of the difficulty or frequency of limitation reported.

**Variables.** Analyses distinguish disability types — seeing, hearing, mobility, flexibility, dexterity, pain-related, mental health-related, developmental, learning and memory — with people able to report several; severity, from mild through moderate and severe to very severe; the number of disability types; and whether a limitation is continuous or dynamic (progressive, recurrent or fluctuating). The survey also asks about needs for assistive aids or devices, barriers to accessibility in the physical environment, communication, Internet use and others' behaviours or assumptions, and needs for workplace accommodations. Employment questions refer to the job held in the survey's reference week or, failing that, the longest-held job since 1 January 2020, and include a self-assessment of how qualified respondents feel for their job.

**Limits.** Sample size constraints restrict how finely some groups can be analysed; for example, overqualification among immigrant and racialized persons with disabilities cannot be broken down further by characteristics such as location or field of study or period since immigration.
