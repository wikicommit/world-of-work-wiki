The American Time Use Survey (ATUS) is conducted by the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics and collects detailed time diary data on all the activities respondents took part in on the prior day. Analyses of unpaid care based on it, such as [[Report/older-women-and-unpaid-caregiving-in-the-us]], can pool several years of data to obtain sample sizes large enough to report on narrower groups; that brief combines 2017, 2018, 2019, 2021 and 2022.

## Details

Care measured as a primary activity — helping someone get dressed, feeding someone, or accompanying someone to a doctor's appointment — undercounts the care people give, because it leaves out secondary care: caring while doing something else, such as household chores, watching TV or pet care. The ATUS has separate items for measuring [[DefinedTerm/eldercare]], which it defines as assisting or caring for an adult who needed help because of a condition related to aging — an ongoing ailment or physical or emotional limitation that typically affects older people — without limiting it to care recipients of a particular age. These eldercare measures capture secondary as well as primary care, for example keeping an eye on an older relative with dementia while also watching TV.
