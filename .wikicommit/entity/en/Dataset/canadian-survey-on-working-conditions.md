The Canadian Survey on Working Conditions (CSWC) is a Statistics Canada survey that aims to better understand the working conditions of Canadians. It is designed to measure the core dimensions of quality of employment — including working time and work-life balance, skills development and training, and physical and psychosocial risks — and how different job characteristics are distributed across the employed population. Statistics Canada presents it as complementing the [[Dataset/canadian-labour-force-survey]] and that survey's supplements, to give a fuller picture of [[DefinedTerm/job-quality]] in Canada.

## Details

The first round was collected from September 2024 to July 2025 in four collection waves (September–October 2024, December 2024–January 2025, March–April 2025 and June–July 2025), and its estimates reflect an average over the reference periods of the four waves. The data represent the employed population aged 15 to 69 living in the provinces; people living on Indigenous reserves and other settlements, full-time members of the armed forces and unpaid family workers are excluded. Most questions refer to the respondent's experience in the 12 months before the survey or to their usual patterns of work. Estimates are based on a sample and subject to sampling variability.

Its measures of exposure to physical risk factors are based on concepts developed by Eurofound, grouped as ambient risks (vibrations, loud noise, high or low temperatures), biological and chemical risks (such as breathing in smoke, fumes or vapours, or handling chemicals or infectious materials) and ergonomic risks (such as lifting or moving people, carrying heavy loads, repetitive hand or arm movements and awkward or painful positions). Working at night is defined as working at least 2 hours between 10:00 p.m. and 5:00 a.m. ([[DefinedTerm/night-work]]).

The first results were published as [[Report/canadian-survey-on-working-conditions-2024-2025]].
