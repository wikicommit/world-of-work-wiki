The EU-LFS module on the labour market situation of migrants and their immediate descendants is a set of additional questions in the [[Dataset/eu-labour-force-survey]] (EU-LFS) that Eurostat uses to study the [[DefinedTerm/labour-market-integration-of-migrants]]. Its reference age group is 15–74. Among other things, it asks foreign-born people whether they have faced obstacles in getting a suitable job in their current host country and what the main obstacle was, why they migrated, how well they spoke the host country's language before migrating, and whether they took a course in that language afterwards.

## Details

The module was carried out in 2021, and from that year it is to be conducted regularly every eight years under Regulation (EU) 2019/1700. Modules have been an inherent part of the EU-LFS since 1999: they were called "ad hoc modules" until 2020, and from 2021 they are called "regular modules" when their variables have an 8-yearly periodicity, or "modules on an ad hoc subject" otherwise.

The 2021 module covers all EU Member States and the EFTA countries Norway and Switzerland; in Cyprus it covers only the areas controlled by the Government of the Republic of Cyprus. Like the rest of the EU-LFS, it covers people usually residing in private households.

In Eurostat's analysis of the module, a migrant is a person residing in an EU Member State who was born in another country, inside or outside the EU, so people who returned to their country of birth after living abroad for at least 12 months are excluded. A "suitable job" is one that matches the person's educational attainment level in the respondent's own assessment, and no reference period is set for when the obstacles occurred. The main obstacles distinguished include lack of skills in the host country's language, no suitable job available, lack of recognition of education obtained abroad, restricted rights to work due to citizenship or residence permit, discrimination due to foreign origin, and other obstacles. Eurostat's published percentages are calculated on totals that exclude non-response.

In 2021, 23.5% of foreign-born people aged 15–74 in the EU said they had faced obstacles to getting a suitable job, 64.5% said they had not, and 12.0% had never worked or sought work in their host country. Lack of host-language skills was the most commonly cited obstacle in 13 of the 26 countries with data. Because the module was carried out in 2021, its results do not include people who fled the war in Ukraine from 2022.

In Italy, foreign-born people who had worked or looked for work but had never looked for a job suited to their education were classified, as the survey methodology requires, under "other" rather than "no suitable job available", which Eurostat gives as the reason for the high share of "other" obstacles there.
