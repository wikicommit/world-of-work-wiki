The Public Service Employee Survey (PSES) is a survey of active employees in organizations of Canada's federal public service and in participating separate agencies. It is a census with voluntary participation: every employee is invited, in the official language of their choice, to report how they perceive their leadership, workforce, workplace, workplace well-being and compensation. Its stated purpose is to inform the continuous improvement of people management practices in the federal government. The survey is led by the Office of the Chief Human Resources Officer.

## Details

The PSES was conducted every three years from 1999 to 2017 and has been run annually since 2018. Each administration is cross-sectional, and data are collected mainly electronically. Respondents rate the large majority of items on a five-point scale running from "strongly agree" or "always" to "strongly disagree" or "never".

Two recent cycles illustrate its reach. The 2017 survey, with 119 items, was run with Statistics Canada and open from 21 August to 29 September; 174,544 employees in 86 federal departments and agencies responded, a response rate of 61%. The 2019 survey, with 112 items, was administered by Advanis on behalf of the Office of the Chief Human Resources Officer from 22 July to 6 September; 182,306 employees in 86 departments and agencies responded, a response rate of 62.3%. In both years, a little under three in five respondents were women, about three in five were aged 40 or older, and roughly two-thirds chose English as their first official language.

Although the PSES was not designed to measure psychological health and safety, its breadth and coverage have made it a candidate for doing so. In [[ScholarlyArticle/measuring-workplace-psychosocial-factors-in-the-federal-government]], Statistics Canada researchers identified 43 items common to the 2017 and 2019 surveys — the Public Service Psychosocial Questionnaire — that measure 10 of the 13 psychosocial factors of the [[DefinedTerm/national-standard-for-psychological-health-and-safety-in-the-workplace]], plus diversity and inclusion, consistently across both cycles. The survey also carries a single overall [[DefinedTerm/job-satisfaction]] item, "Overall, I like my job".
