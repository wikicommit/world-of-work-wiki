"Night-shift work and susceptibility to infectious diseases: a systematic review and meta-analysis" is a review by Loef, Bosma, van Kerkhof, Proper, van Baarle and Dollé, published online on 6 April 2025 in the *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* (volume 51, issue 4, pages 298–311; issue date 1 July 2025). The authors observe that a growing body of research on infection susceptibility among night-shift workers had emerged, particularly since the COVID-19 pandemic, without a comprehensive overview, and set out to synthesise the evidence on the association between night-shift work and susceptibility to infectious diseases (see [[DefinedTerm/night-work]]).

Embase and PsycINFO were searched systematically for studies published up to September 2024. Studies were included if they covered a working population, compared night-shift workers with non-shift workers, and had an infectious disease as the outcome. Results were synthesised descriptively for common respiratory infections (flu and common cold), SARS-CoV-2 infection and other infections, and pooled effect estimates were calculated with random-effects meta-analysis.

## Key Points

- The review included 16 articles describing 14 studies among 191 320 workers.
- Based on 4 studies, night-shift work was not associated with a significantly increased risk of common respiratory infections (odds ratio 1.11, 95% CI 0.97–1.27, I² = 65.8%).
- Night-shift workers had a higher risk of SARS-CoV-2 infection than non-shift workers (odds ratio 1.31, 95% CI 1.09–1.58, I² = 92.2%, 10 studies).
- The SARS-CoV-2 association was stronger in higher-quality studies and in studies conducted in the first year of the COVID-19 pandemic.
- Too few studies of other infections were available for a meta-analysis.
- The certainty of the evidence was graded very low because of the limited number of (prospective cohort) studies and high inconsistency between the available studies.

## Notes

To address the lack of high-certainty evidence, the authors call for more studies that use a prospective design with appropriate adjustment for confounding factors and more extensive information on exposure to night-shift work.
