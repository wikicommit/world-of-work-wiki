"Office design, telework from home, and self-certified sickness absence" is an original article published online on 16 January 2023 in the *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* (volume 49, issue 3, pages 222–230). It investigates two questions: the main effects of office design and of access to telework from home ([[DefinedTerm/working-from-home]]) on self-certified sickness absence, and whether access to telework from home moderates the relationship between office design and such absence.

The study used cross-sectional survey data from a nationally representative sample of 4329 people in Norway. It analysed the data with negative binomial hurdle models, adjusting for age, gender, education level, leadership responsibility and time spent on office work, and evaluated moderating effects with pairwise comparisons and plots of estimated marginal means.

## Key Points

- In adjusted models, employees in conventional open-plan offices had significantly higher odds of sickness absence than employees in private offices (odds ratio 1.32, 95% CI 1.13–1.54).
- Employees with access to telework from home had significantly lower odds of sickness absence than employees without access (odds ratio 0.86, 95% CI 0.74–0.99).
- Among employees with access to telework from home, those in conventional open-plan offices had a significantly higher predicted probability of self-certified sickness absence than those in private offices; among employees without such access, there were no significant differences between office designs.
- There were no significant main or moderating effects on the number of sickness absence episodes in adjusted models.
- The authors identify conventional open-plan offices as a potential risk factor for sickness absence, and conclude that while access to telework from home may be a protective factor overall, it amplified rather than attenuated the differences in sickness absence between employees in private and conventional open-plan offices.

## Notes

The findings rest on a single cross-sectional survey in one country.
