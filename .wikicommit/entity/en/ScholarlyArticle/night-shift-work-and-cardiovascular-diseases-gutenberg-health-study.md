"Night shift work and cardiovascular diseases among employees in Germany: five-year follow-up of the Gutenberg Health Study" is an original article published online on 23 January 2024 in the *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* (volume 50, issue 3, pages 142–151). It asks whether cumulative night shift work ([[DefinedTerm/night-work]]) increases the risk of incident cardiovascular disease (CVD) in the German population-based Gutenberg Health Study (GHS).

The study examined working participants of the GHS at baseline and again after five years. Cumulative night shift work in the ten years before baseline was categorised as low (1–220 nights, up to 1 year), middle (221–660 nights, 1–3 years) or high (more than 660 nights, more than 3 years), and hazard ratios for incident "quality-assured CVD events" were estimated with Cox proportional hazard models.

## Key Points

- At baseline, 1092 of 8167 working participants performed night shift work; 202 incident cardiovascular events occurred during follow-up.
- Crude incidence rates of CVD per 1000 person-years were 6.88 (95% CI 4.80–9.55) for night shift workers and 5.19 (95% CI 4.44–6.04) for day workers, and cumulative incidence curves showed a higher cumulative incidence among workers exposed to night shift work after five years.
- Compared with employees without night shift work, the adjusted hazard ratios for incident CVD events were 1.26 (95% CI 0.68–2.33), 1.37 (95% CI 0.74–2.53) and 1.19 (95% CI 0.67–2.12) for the low, middle and high night shift categories respectively.
- The authors conclude that the observed tendencies indicate night shift work might be negatively associated with cardiovascular health, and expect continued follow-up to clarify its long-term impact.

## Notes

All three adjusted hazard ratios have 95% confidence intervals that include one.
