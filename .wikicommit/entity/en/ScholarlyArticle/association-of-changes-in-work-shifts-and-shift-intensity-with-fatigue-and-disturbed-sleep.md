"Association of changes in work shifts and shift intensity with change in fatigue and disturbed sleep: a within-subject study" is an original article published online on 11 April 2018 in the *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* (volume 44, issue 4, pages 394–402). It examines whether changes in an individual's work shifts and shift intensity are related to changes in their difficulty falling asleep, fatigue and sleep length.

The study linked questionnaire responses of hospital employees (N=7727, 93% women) in 2008, 2012, 2014 and 2015 to daily records of their working hours during the three months before each survey. Using conditional logistic regression and longitudinal fixed-effects analyses, it estimated odds ratios for each 25% within-individual change in the proportion of a working-hour characteristic — so each worker is compared with themselves over time rather than with other workers.

## Key Points

- Changes in the proportion of night shifts ([[DefinedTerm/night-work]]), but not of morning or evening shifts, were associated with parallel changes in the odds of longer sleep length (OR 1.45, 95% CI 1.28–1.64) and of fatigue during free days (OR 1.38, 95% CI 1.16–1.64).
- Short shift intervals and having more than 2 (but not more than 4) consecutive night shifts were associated with increased odds of fatigue during work and of difficulty falling asleep (OR 1.42, 95% CI 1.19–1.72 and OR 1.10, 95% CI 1.05–1.19 respectively).
- Among workers aged 50 or over, the strongest associations were between night shifts and longer sleep (OR 2.24, 95% CI 1.52–3.81) and between a higher proportion of short shift intervals and fatigue during free days (OR 1.68, 95% CI 1.10–2.54).
- The authors conclude that, among shift workers with fatigue or sleep problems, decreasing the proportion of night shifts and quick returns and giving preference to quickly forward-rotating shift systems may reduce fatigue.

## Notes

The study population is hospital employees, 93% of them women, and the working-hour exposures come from daily-based records rather than the questionnaires.
