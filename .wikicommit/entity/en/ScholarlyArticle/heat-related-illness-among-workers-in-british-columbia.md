"Heat-related illness among workers in British Columbia, Canada: Extreme hot weather in 2021 compared to 2001–2020" is an original article by Guo, Weinberger, Tamburic, Peters and McLeod, published online on 31 July 2024 in the *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* (volume 50, issue 7, pages 545–554; issue date 1 October 2024). British Columbia experienced an unprecedented summer with record-breaking high temperatures in 2021, and the authors note that its health impact had not been examined in occupational settings. For the wider topic, see [[DefinedTerm/climate-related-occupational-risks]].

The study set out to characterise occupational heat-related illness (HRI) among British Columbia workers through incidence rates and the association between heatwaves and HRI, to compare the risks in 2021 with those of the summers of 2001–2020, and to assess how the impact differed by worker demographics and occupation. HRI cases were identified from workers' compensation claims that occurred between June and August in 2001–2021. Incidence rates used working population estimates from Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey, and a time-stratified case-crossover design with conditional Poisson regression estimated the impact of heatwaves. All analyses were stratified by year (2021 versus 2001–2020), age, sex and occupation.

## Key Points

- Of the 521 HRI claims identified, 107 (21%) occurred in 2021.
- The incidence rate was 3.97 claims per 100 000 workers (95% CI 3.26–4.80) in 2021, against 0.93 (95% CI 0.85–1.03) in the prior summers — a 327% increase.
- Rates in health occupations were higher in 2021 than in 2001–2020.
- Across 2001–2021, the risk of HRI on heatwave days was 4.33 times (95% CI 2.98–6.27) that on non-heatwave days, and the risk was higher among middle-aged workers and among workers in trades, transport and equipment operations.
- The 2021 heatwaves had a greater impact on younger and female workers than the heatwaves of prior summers.
- The authors conclude that heat is a crucial workplace hazard and that prevention strategies should prioritise at-risk workers and not be limited to heatwaves.
