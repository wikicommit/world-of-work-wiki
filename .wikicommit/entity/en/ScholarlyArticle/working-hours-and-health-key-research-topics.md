"Working hours and health – key research topics in the past and future" is a discussion paper published online on 18 March 2024 in the *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* (volume 50, issue 4, pages 233–243). It discusses past and present highlights of research on working hours and health and identifies key research needs for the future.

The authors analysed over 220 original articles and reviews on working hours and health published in the journal during the last 50 years, and also included key publications from other journals.

## Key Points

- Most of the articles identified focused on the effects of [[DefinedTerm/shift-work]] and night work; fewer studied long and reduced working hours and work time control.
- The field has moved from small-scale experimental and intensive field studies to large-scale epidemiological studies with precise exposure assessment, reflecting the recent emergence of register-based datasets and the development of analytic methods and alternatives to randomised controlled designs.
- The cumulative findings provide convincing evidence that shift work and [[DefinedTerm/long-working-hours]], which are often associated with night work and insufficient recovery, increase the risk of poor sleep and fatigue, sickness absence, occupational injuries, and several chronic health conditions such as cardiovascular diseases and cancer.
- The observed risks are strongly modified by individual and work-related factors.
- Although the observed health risks of shift work and long working hours are mostly low or moderate, the authors conclude that the widespread prevalence of exposure and the hazardousness of the many potential outcomes make these working time arrangements major occupational health risks.
- Further research is needed to identify exposure–response associations, especially for chronic health effects, and to elucidate underlying pathways and effective personalised intervention strategies.
