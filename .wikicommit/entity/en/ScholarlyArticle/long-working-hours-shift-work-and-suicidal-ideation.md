"The association between long working hours, shift work, and suicidal ideation: A systematic review and meta-analyses" is a review published online on 14 August 2024 in the *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* (volume 50, issue 7, pages 503–518). Noting that occupational psychosocial factors and mental health have been studied in several systematic reviews but that few address suicidal ideation specifically, it examines the associations of [[DefinedTerm/long-working-hours]] and [[DefinedTerm/shift-work]] with suicidal ideation.

The authors screened studies published between January 1970 and September 2022 in three electronic databases and Google Scholar, and pooled 28 articles in a random-effects meta-analysis. Risk of bias was assessed with a navigation guide, and the grading of recommendations assessment, development and evaluation (GRADE) approach was used to consider five items, including risk of bias and publication bias.

## Key Points

- After adjustment for covariates, the overall odds ratio for suicidal ideation was 1.44 (95% CI 1.26–1.63) for long working hours and 1.34 (95% CI 1.22–1.47) for shift work.
- Compared with people working fewer than 40 hours a week, those working more than 55 hours a week had a 1.65 (95% CI 1.37–1.98) higher risk of suicidal ideation.
- Fixed night shift workers had a 1.37 (95% CI 1.03–1.83) higher risk than non-shift workers.
- The authors conclude that evidence shows associations between long working hours, shift work and suicidal ideation, and that evidence-based interventions addressing these risk factors may help prevent the progression of suicidal ideation.

## Notes

The authors call for further studies, especially ones using standardised exposure definitions and longitudinal data.
