"Long working hours and all-cause mortality in China: A 26-year follow-up study" is an original article published online on 4 September 2023 in the *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* (volume 49, issue 8, pages 539–548). Noting that the relationship between long working hours and mortality has been debated in various countries, it investigates the association between [[DefinedTerm/long-working-hours]] and all-cause mortality in a large population-based cohort in China.

The retrospective cohort study (N=10 269) used the China Health and Nutrition Surveys (CHNS), a large, nationally representative data set, from 1989 to 2015. It compared long working hours (55 or more hours a week) with standard working hours (35–40 hours a week), estimating hazard ratios for all-cause mortality with Cox proportional hazards regression and stratified analyses by subgroup.

## Key Points

- 411 deaths (3.52 per 1000 person-years) occurred during a median follow-up of 11.0 years (range 4.0–18.0).
- After adjustment for covariates, long working hours were associated with a significantly increased risk of all-cause mortality (HR 1.49, 95% CI 1.02–2.18).
- In stratified analyses the association was present only among men (HR 1.78, 95% CI 1.15–2.75) and among participants who smoked (HR 1.57, 95% CI 1.05–2.57).
- The authors call for targeted interventions to reduce excessive working hours and to identify individuals at elevated risk, with support from labour organizations, policymakers and employers.
