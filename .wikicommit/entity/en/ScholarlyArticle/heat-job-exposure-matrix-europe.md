"Exposure to heat at work: development of a quantitative European job exposure matrix (heat JEM)" is an original article by de Crom, Pronk and fourteen co-authors, published online on 9 August 2025 in the *Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health* (volume 52, issue 1, pages 7–18; issue date 1 January 2026). Starting from the observation that climate change is exacerbating occupational heat stress, which makes objective and systematic exposure assessment essential for epidemiological studies, the authors developed a job exposure matrix (JEM) that assigns occupational heat stress exposure across Europe.

The heat JEM is aligned with the International Organization for Standardization standards ISO 7243, 8996 and 9920 and gives region- and year-specific estimates of annual heat stress hours by job title, using the International Standard Classification of Occupations 1988 for Europe, ISCO-88(COM). Heat stress is defined as the effective wet bulb globe temperature (WBGTeff) exceeding a reference WBGT (WBGTref). Outdoor and indoor WBGT were determined from historical, region-specific hourly meteorological data — temperature, radiation, humidity and wind speed — across Europe between 1970 and 2024, and adjusted for job-specific clothing to obtain WBGTeff. WBGTref was based on metabolic rate, calculated from body surface area and job-specific physical activity, and adjusted for acclimatization status. Further adjustments were made for the job-specific presence of local heat and cooling sources, the time spent indoors versus outdoors, and working schedules.

## Key Points

- The annual number of hours workers experience heat stress is highest in jobs that involve local heat sources and physically demanding tasks, especially where work clothing is mandatory.
- Southern Europe has a higher annual heat stress burden than other regions.
- Exposure varies across calendar years.
- Exposure is substantially higher for unacclimatized workers than for acclimatized workers.
- By incorporating job-, region- and year-specific factors, the heat JEM is presented as a harmonized tool for studying occupational heat stress, and its transparent framework allows updates with new data and extension to other years and regions.

## Notes

The matrix is intended as an exposure-assessment tool for epidemiological studies of occupational heat stress. For the wider topic, see [[DefinedTerm/climate-related-occupational-risks]].
