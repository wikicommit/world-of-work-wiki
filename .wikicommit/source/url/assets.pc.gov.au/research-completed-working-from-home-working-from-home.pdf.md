## Summary

This September 2021 research paper by Australia's Productivity Commission examines the large, pandemic-driven shift to working from home, finding that about 35% of Australian jobs could be done from home while only about 8% of employees had a formal arrangement before COVID-19. It analyses how worker and employer preferences will be reconciled through a "second wave" of experimentation (with hybrid models likely to dominate), whether work health and safety and workplace relations regulation are fit for purpose, and how more remote work could affect cities, wellbeing and equity. It concludes that the shift is broadly beneficial and that governments should monitor it and smooth, rather than impede, the transition.
