## Summary

An Insee Références study in *France, portrait social* (November 2025), written by researchers from Ined and Sciences Po, uses the Dares-Insee Working Conditions survey of 2019 to show that 48% of employees in metropolitan France regularly work at least one form of atypical hours — long, early-morning, evening, night or split usual hours, or regular weekend work. Women are slightly more exposed than men, especially to regular weekend work and split hours, and accumulate atypical hours with weekend work more often. Occupational category is the main determinant of exposure, with distinct effects by gender, while people born abroad and the less qualified are more exposed at comparable characteristics, and constrained part-time work sharply raises the risk of split hours.
