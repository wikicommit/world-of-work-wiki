## Summary

This CDC Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report article, published on 3 March 2017, analyses 2013–2014 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System data from 29 US states to compare the prevalence of short sleep duration (less than 7 hours a day) across 22 major and 93 detailed occupation groups. Overall 36.5% of employed adults reported short sleep; among major groups prevalence was highest in production (42.9%), healthcare support, healthcare practitioners and technical, food preparation and serving, and protective service, and among detailed groups it ranged from 21.4% among air transportation workers to 58.2% among communications equipment operators. The authors relate these differences to shift work and occupational regulation of work hours and call for work-related factors to be evaluated to guide prevention.
