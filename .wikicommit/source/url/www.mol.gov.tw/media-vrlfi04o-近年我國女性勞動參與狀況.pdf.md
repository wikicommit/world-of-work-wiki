## Summary

A Labour Statistics Bulletin from the Department of Statistics of Taiwan's Ministry of Labor (11 June 2019) reviews women's labour force participation over the two decades to 2018: the female participation rate first passed 50% in 2012 and reached 51.1% in 2018, peaking at 91.8% at ages 25-29 before falling from the early thirties, with prime-age rates above those of the United States, Japan and Korea up to 34 but lower after 50. It also covers women's concentration in services, the gender gap in average hourly earnings (14.6% in 2018) and Taiwan's Equal Pay Day, marriage- and childbirth-related exits from work, women's take-up of unpaid parental leave and return to work afterwards, and the reasons economically inactive women give for not seeking work.
