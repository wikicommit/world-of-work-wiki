## Summary

This theme page of the Danish National Research Centre for the Working Environment (NFA) describes its research on working hours and night shifts. It reports that night work in Denmark is most widespread in passenger transport, hospitals, and police, emergency services and prisons, and among younger people and men, and that night shifts are linked to disrupted circadian rhythms, higher risks of some forms of miscarriage and of accidents, more sickness absence and poorer health behaviour. It sets out NFA's research-based recommendations for organising night work: at most three consecutive night shifts, at least 11 hours between shifts, shifts of at most 9 hours, and normally no more than one night shift a week for pregnant women.
