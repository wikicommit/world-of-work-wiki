## Summary

WorkSafe New Zealand's guidance *Managing the risks of shift work* helps persons conducting a business or undertaking (PCBUs) meet their Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 duties for shift work, which it defines as any work requiring a worker to be awake when they would normally be asleep. It explains how shift work causes fatigue through sleep loss, extended wakefulness, circadian disruption and workload, and the resulting health, performance, injury and absence risks. It then sets out a continuous fatigue risk management cycle — worker engagement, management commitment, risk identification and assessment, smart rostering and other controls (shift timing, length, rotation, breaks, rest days, on-call work and workplace napping), policies, reporting, communication and training — and the monitoring of those controls.
