## Summary

A December 2019 research paper in Statistics Canada's Analytical Studies Branch Research Paper Series (11F0019M No. 437) that defines gig workers in terms of how work is reported in the Canadian tax system — unincorporated sole proprietors reporting business, professional or commission income on at least one T2125 form without a business number — and uses the Canadian Employer–Employee Dynamics Database, linked to 2016 Census microdata, to measure them. The share of gig workers among all workers rose from 5.5% in 2005 to 8.2% in 2016, with women consistently more likely than men to do gig work. Median net gig income was low ($4,303 in 2016), gig work was often temporary and more common among low-income workers, immigrants, graduate-degree holders and workers in arts, culture and recreation occupations.
