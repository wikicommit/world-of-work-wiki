## Summary

The findings section of a Statistics Canada Health Reports article on work injuries, which uses the 2003 Canadian Community Health Survey (cycle 2.1) to estimate how many employed Canadians aged 18 to 75 sustained a non-fatal, activity-limiting injury on the job and to relate those injuries to work-related and personal factors. An estimated 630,000 Canadians were injured at work in 2003, mostly men and blue-collar workers; strenuous jobs, shift work, multiple jobs and daily smoking raised the risk for both sexes, while long hours and lower income were linked to injury in men, and job stress and obesity in women.
