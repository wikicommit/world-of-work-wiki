## Summary

A 2002 article in Statistics Canada's Health Reports that profiles Canadian shift workers with the 2000/01 Canadian Community Health Survey and, using the National Population Health Survey, compares their work stress, psycho-social problems, health behaviours, chronic conditions and psychological distress with those of regular daytime workers. About 30% of men and 26% of women aged 18 to 54 who worked all year had non-standard schedules, mostly because the job required it; shift work showed no cross-sectional association with chronic conditions or distress, but men on evening, rotating or irregular shifts in 1994/95 had higher odds of a new chronic condition by 1998/99, and an evening shift was associated with a rise in distress over two years for both sexes. Fewer than one in five shift workers kept the same schedule over four years, which the article reads as consistent with a "healthy survivor effect".
