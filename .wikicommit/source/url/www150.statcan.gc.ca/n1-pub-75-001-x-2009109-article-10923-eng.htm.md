## Summary

A September 2009 Perspectives on Labour and Income article by Statistics Canada that uses the 2002 Canadian Community Health Survey (Mental Health and Well-being, Cycle 1.2) to examine how physical and mental health relate to employment and annual hours worked among Canadians aged 15 to 64. Using several measures of health status and estimation methods, including Heckman selection models and propensity score matching, it finds that relatively poor physical or mental health lowers the probability of being employed, with a stronger effect for men. Mental health problems were also associated with women working fewer hours, while results on hours were inconclusive for men.
