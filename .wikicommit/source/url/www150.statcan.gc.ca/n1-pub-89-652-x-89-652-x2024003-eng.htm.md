## Summary

A 2024 Statistics Canada study (catalogue no. 89-652-X) that uses time-diary data from the 2022 Time Use Survey to compare the time use and well-being of employees who teleworked from home on the diary day, teleworkers who worked on-site that day, and non-teleworkers. After adjusting for sociodemographic and job characteristics, work-from-home teleworkers saved over an hour of commuting and spent more time sleeping, eating, in leisure and in unpaid housework, and parents more time with their children, with no difference in paid work time. Work-from-home teleworkers were also more satisfied with their work-life balance, while on-site teleworkers reported the most time pressure.
