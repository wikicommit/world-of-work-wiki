## Summary

The first WHO/ILO Joint Estimates of the Work-related Burden of Disease and Injury, published by the World Health Organization and the International Labour Organization in 2021, estimate that 1.88 million deaths and 89.72 million disability-adjusted life years in 2016 were attributable to 41 pairs of occupational risk factor and health outcome across 183 countries. Exposure to long working hours (55 hours or more per week) was the risk factor with the largest number of attributable deaths, through stroke and ischaemic heart disease, followed by occupational particulate matter, gases and fumes and occupational injuries. The report describes the data and methods, trends from 2000 to 2016, inequalities by region, sex and age, and preventive actions for each risk factor.
