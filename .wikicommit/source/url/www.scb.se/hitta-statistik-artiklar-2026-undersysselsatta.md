## Summary

A Statistics Sweden (SCB) short analysis, last updated on 3 February 2026, reports that underemployment — wanting to work more hours per week than one usually does — has become more common in Sweden since 2018, rising during the pandemic and again with inflation and higher living costs, so that about six per cent of the employed are now underemployed. Based on the Labour Force Survey (AKU), it shows that underemployment is most common among young people, temporary employees and part-time workers, that the gap between women and men has narrowed, and that the extra hours the underemployed want have fallen to about nine per week on average.
