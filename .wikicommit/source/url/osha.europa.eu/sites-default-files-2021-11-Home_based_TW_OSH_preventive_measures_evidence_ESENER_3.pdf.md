## Summary

An EU-OSHA European Risk Observatory report (2021) that uses pre-pandemic data from the EU Labour Force Survey, the 2015 European Working Conditions Survey and the 2019 ESENER-3 enterprise survey to examine home-based teleworking and occupational safety and health in the EU. It finds that teleworkers reported more headaches and eye strain, anxiety, sleep problems, stress, working in free time and working while sick than non-teleworkers, and that establishments with teleworking in place reported prolonged sitting and psychosocial risks more often but also more often had preventive measures. Probit models show that establishment size, belonging to the services sector and being in a country group with higher teleworking prevalence were the strongest predictors of having teleworking in place.
