## Summary

A retrospective cohort study published in the Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health (2023) that used China Health and Nutrition Survey data from 1989 to 2015 (N=10 269) to compare long working hours (55 or more hours a week) with standard hours (35–40 hours). After adjustment, long working hours were associated with a higher risk of all-cause mortality (HR 1.49), an association found only among men and smokers in stratified analyses.
