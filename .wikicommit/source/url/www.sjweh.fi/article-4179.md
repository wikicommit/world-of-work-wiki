## Summary

A 2024 study in the Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health that used workers' compensation claims to characterise occupational heat-related illness among workers in British Columbia, Canada, comparing the record-breaking hot summer of 2021 with the summers of 2001–2020. Of 521 claims identified, 107 occurred in 2021, when the incidence rate was 327% higher than in prior summers, and heatwave days carried 4.33 times the risk of non-heatwave days. The authors conclude that heat is a crucial workplace hazard and that prevention should prioritise at-risk workers and not be limited to heatwaves.
