## Summary

An article in the Scandinavian Journal of Work, Environment & Health (2026) describing the development of a quantitative European job exposure matrix for occupational heat stress (heat JEM), which estimates region- and year-specific annual heat stress hours by job title using ISO-aligned wet bulb globe temperature methods and meteorological data for 1970–2024. Heat stress hours are highest in jobs with local heat sources and physically demanding tasks, especially where work clothing is mandatory, higher in Southern Europe, and substantially higher for unacclimatized workers.
